# Mystery Story Scoring Rubric

Use this rubric to score generated mystery stories consistently and strictly.

The goal is not to reward a story only because it has an interesting mystery idea. A strong score requires clear execution: stable characters, coherent timeline, planted clues, fair-play logic, readable prose, and a satisfying reveal.

Score each area out of 10, then total the score out of 100.

---

# Overall Scoring Philosophy

Start each category at **5/10**.

Add points only for clear strengths visible in the text.

Subtract points for specific problems.

Do not inflate scores because the premise is promising if the draft itself is confusing.

A good mystery idea can score highly in **Premise / Concept**, but the overall score should stay lower if the execution has continuity errors, unclear roles, pronoun mistakes, repeated generated phrasing, weak clue logic, or an incomplete reveal.

---

# Score Bands

Use these bands for calibration:

* **90–100**: Excellent, polished mystery. Strong prose, fair clues, vivid characters, satisfying reveal, minimal issues.
* **80–89**: Very strong. Clear plot and clue chain, good character work, only moderate polish problems.
* **70–79**: Solid draft. Coherent mystery structure, mostly stable roles, some prose or logic issues remain.
* **60–69**: Promising but rough. Good idea and some structure, but noticeable continuity, pacing, or prose problems.
* **50–59**: Usable concept, messy execution. Several role, clue, repetition, or timeline issues.
* **40–49**: Major continuity problems. Victim, culprit, investigator, or timeline may become confused.
* **Below 40**: Hard to follow as a story. Severe incoherence, broken roles, or no proper mystery logic.

# Category 1: Premise / Concept

Score how strong the central mystery idea is.

## 10/10

Original, compelling, distinctive mystery idea with strong dramatic potential.

## 8/10

Strong and memorable premise with a clever central mechanism or setting.

## 7/10

Good usable mystery idea, even if familiar.

## 5/10

Workable but ordinary mystery setup.

## 3/10

Weak, vague, or underdeveloped premise.

## 1/10

No clear mystery premise.

## Notes

Reward:

* A distinctive murder mechanism.
* A strong contradiction, such as time, location, alibi, or physical evidence.
* A setting that naturally supports the mystery.
* A premise that creates suspicion among multiple characters.

Penalise:

* Generic “body + clock + suspects” setup with no fresh angle.
* Premise that depends on impossible or unexplained logic.
* A mystery mechanism that is not connected to the murder.

---

# Category 2: Opening Hook

Score how effectively the story begins.

## 10/10

Immediate, vivid, clear, and gripping. The reader quickly understands the setting, victim, investigator, and first unsettling clue.

## 8/10

Strong opening with good atmosphere and a clear mystery.

## 7/10

Good hook, with minor confusion or over-explanation.

## 5/10

Decent but slow, generic, or unclear.

## 3/10

Confusing, flat, or too mechanical.

## 1/10

No effective hook.

## Notes

Reward:

* Named victim introduced early.
* Strong visual discovery.
* Immediate contradiction or mystery question.
* Atmosphere that supports the crime.
* Clear investigator viewpoint.

Penalise:

* Victim left unnamed.
* Body introduced too late after excessive clue explanation.
* Full solution explained in Chapter 1.
* Opening dominated by setting inventory.
* Opening dominated by technical mechanism before people are clear.
* Generated location text leaking into the prose.

---

# Category 3: Plot Structure

Score the shape and progression of the story.

## 10/10

Clean beginning, investigation, escalation, reversal, test, reveal, and consequence.

## 8/10

Strong structure with clear chapter functions and mostly smooth transitions.

## 7/10

Mostly coherent structure with some weak or repeated chapters.

## 5/10

Basic mystery shape exists, but it is repetitive, uneven, or underdeveloped.

## 3/10

Muddled progression; chapters do not build logically.

## 1/10

No coherent plot structure.

## Notes

Reward:

* Each chapter changes the investigation state.
* Suspects are pressured, cleared, or complicated in sequence.
* Clues are planted, reinterpreted, and used.
* The story narrows toward the culprit logically.
* The reveal follows from earlier chapters.

Penalise:

* Chapters repeat the same clue or interrogation without new movement.
* Culprit direction changes randomly.
* False suspects are cleared by assertion.
* The final reveal depends on information not planted earlier.
* The story has a setup but no real ending.
* The final chapter feels like another investigation chapter instead of a reveal.

---

# Category 4: Character Clarity

Score how clear and consistent the characters are.

## 10/10

All names, roles, relationships, motives, and pronouns are clear and stable.

## 8/10

Characters are mostly clear with minor slips.

## 7/10

Roles are understandable, but some relationships or details need sharpening.

## 5/10

Characters are mostly followable, but there are repeated confusions.

## 3/10

Frequent confusion over who is alive, dead, investigator, suspect, victim, or culprit.

## 1/10

Characters are incoherent.

## Hard Caps

Use these caps unless there is a very strong reason not to:

* If a dead victim appears alive later without flashback, recording, letter, diary, dream, or quoted memory, **Character Clarity cannot exceed 3/10**.
* If the victim is also the investigator by accident, **Character Clarity cannot exceed 3/10**.
* If the culprit is also the victim by accident, **Character Clarity cannot exceed 3/10**.
* If the victim’s identity is unclear, **Character Clarity cannot exceed 4/10**.
* If pronouns repeatedly switch for the same character, **Character Clarity usually cannot exceed 5/10**.
* If multiple characters change roles across chapters, **Character Clarity cannot exceed 4/10**.

## Notes

Reward:

* Clear cast roles.
* Strong first appearances.
* Stable relationships to the victim.
* Consistent pronouns.
* Suspects with distinct motives and pressures.

Penalise:

* Dead characters speaking later without framing.
* Investigator accidentally described as dead.
* Victim appearing as a suspect.
* Culprit cleared before the reveal.
* Characters referred to by inconsistent names.
* Pronouns switching between he/she/her/his for the same person.

---

# Category 5: Dialogue

Score how well the dialogue works.

## 10/10

Natural, distinct, characterful, and advances plot or pressure.

## 8/10

Mostly effective with some direct exposition.

## 7/10

Clear and functional, with occasional strong lines.

## 5/10

Readable but plain, repetitive, or overly direct.

## 3/10

Stiff, unnatural, unfinished, or mostly exposition.

## 1/10

Incoherent dialogue.

## Notes

Reward:

* Dialogue that reveals character under pressure.
* Suspects avoiding, deflecting, or contradicting themselves naturally.
* Emotional subtext.
* Distinct voices.

Penalise:

* Characters explaining the plot mechanically.
* Repeated lines like “we must uncover the truth.”
* Dialogue that sounds like a summary or validation checklist.
* Cut-off speech.
* Over-formal phrasing that no character would naturally say.
* Confession that is too easy or too early.

---

# Category 6: Atmosphere / Setting

Score how effectively the setting supports the mystery.

## 10/10

Vivid, specific, immersive, and directly connected to the mystery.

## 8/10

Strong mood and setting with useful sensory details.

## 7/10

Good atmosphere with some repetition.

## 5/10

Appropriate but generic.

## 3/10

Repetitive, pasted-in, inconsistent, or distracting.

## 1/10

No useful atmosphere.

## Notes

Reward:

* Setting details that create suspicion.
* Physical spaces that matter to alibis, clues, or movement.
* Sensory details that feel specific.
* Setting that reinforces genre and tone.

Penalise:

* Repeated weather/location openings.
* Generic phrases like “a tense weight to it.”
* Location-profile text leaking into prose.
* Multiple unrelated locations named before the real scene starts.
* Atmosphere that takes space away from character or investigation.
* Setting details that contradict the scene.

## Balance Guidance

For most chapters, setting should be about **10–15%**.

For Chapter 1, setting may be **15–20%**.

Setting should rarely exceed **25%** unless the setting itself is the clue.

---

# Category 7: Mystery Clues / Evidence Logic

Score how well the clues work.

## 10/10

Clues are planted fairly, interpreted gradually, support the reveal, and withstand scrutiny.

## 8/10

Strong clue chain with minor gaps.

## 7/10

Good clues, but some logic needs tightening.

## 5/10

Some useful clues, but the chain is weak or incomplete.

## 3/10

Clues contradict each other, appear suddenly, or fail to prove the reveal.

## 1/10

No coherent clue logic.

## Notes

Reward:

* Physical clues with clear relevance.
* Clues that first mislead, then later make sense.
* Witness statements that interact with physical evidence.
* A discriminating test that proves one theory and rules out another.
* Final accusation based on earlier evidence.

Penalise:

* Decisive proof introduced only in the final reveal.
* Clues explained fully when first introduced.
* Clue math that does not add up.
* Contradictory times or alibis.
* “The evidence proves…” without showing how.
* A test that merely repeats known facts instead of proving something.

## Important Distinction

The **mystery mechanism** is not automatically the **murder method**.

The story must distinguish:

* how the victim died
* how the culprit concealed or distorted the evidence
* how the detective proves the truth

If the story explains the clock/tide/mechanism but not the actual death, reduce this score.

---

# Category 8: Pacing

Score whether the story moves well.

## 10/10

Every scene advances plot, character, clue logic, or tension.

## 8/10

Mostly purposeful with only minor slow patches.

## 7/10

Good movement, but some repetition.

## 5/10

Readable but uneven or repetitive.

## 3/10

Many repeated scenes or stalled progress.

## 1/10

No momentum.

## Notes

Reward:

* Each chapter has a clear function.
* Investigation state changes by the end of each chapter.
* Suspect pressure escalates.
* Clues are introduced and reinterpreted at a good pace.
* Reveal does not come too early or too late.

Penalise:

* Same interrogation repeated with different names.
* Same clue explained repeatedly.
* Long atmosphere openings in every chapter.
* Clearing chapters that do not add drama.
* Discriminating test chapters that do not actually test anything.
* Final reveal that repeats previous chapters without adding consequence.

---

# Category 9: Ending / Reveal

Score how satisfying and logical the ending is.

## 10/10

Earned, logical, dramatic, emotionally satisfying, and clearly resolves the mystery.

## 8/10

Strong reveal with clear motive, method, opportunity, clue chain, and consequence.

## 7/10

Clear reveal with some missing detail.

## 5/10

Understandable but sudden, underexplained, or too report-like.

## 3/10

Confusing, unearned, contradictory, or incomplete.

## 1/10

No proper reveal or resolution.

## Required Reveal Elements

A strong reveal should explain:

* who did it
* why they did it
* how the victim died
* how the culprit tried to conceal it
* what opportunity the culprit had
* which clues exposed them
* why the false suspects are innocent
* what consequence follows

## Hard Caps

* If the culprit is also the victim by accident, **Ending cannot exceed 3/10**.
* If the culprit only confesses to tampering, lying, or hiding evidence but not to causing the death, **Ending usually cannot exceed 6/10**.
* If the reveal depends on evidence not planted earlier, **Ending usually cannot exceed 5/10**.
* If there is no confession, exposure, arrest, consequence, or clear resolution, **Ending usually cannot exceed 5/10**.
* If the ending contradicts earlier chapters, **Ending cannot exceed 4/10**.

## Notes

Reward:

* Culprit cornered by evidence, not convenience.
* Emotional motive.
* Clear murder method.
* Strong final deduction.
* False suspects cleared cleanly.
* Consequence that lands emotionally.

Penalise:

* “You did it” without proof.
* Instant confession after weak evidence.
* Culprit motive introduced only at the last moment.
* Report-like ending.
* Culprit says “I never meant for this to happen” without explaining what happened.
* Final line says the investigation is “just beginning” after the culprit has already been revealed.

---

# Category 10: Prose / Polish

Score the quality of the writing itself.

## 10/10

Clean, fluent, polished, natural prose.

## 8/10

Mostly polished with minor awkwardness.

## 7/10

Readable and controlled, but with some rough patches.

## 5/10

Readable but noticeably rough.

## 3/10

Repeated phrases, broken sentences, grammar issues, note-like lines, pronoun errors.

## 1/10

Very rough or hard to read.

## Notes

Reward:

* Clear sentences.
* Natural flow.
* Smooth transitions.
* Strong sensory detail without repetition.
* Prose that feels like fiction, not outline expansion.

Penalise:

* Broken or unfinished sentences.
* Encoding issues.
* Repeated generated phrases.
* Planning notes leaking into prose.
* Raw validation text appearing in chapters.
* Awkward phrases like “the doctor Finch” or “the captain Hale.”
* Lines like “The time was recorded as…”
* Lines like “The elapsed time was confirmed…”
* Lines like “The chapter moves forward through…”
* Doubled words.
* Wrong room names.
* Inconsistent capitalization.
* Pronoun mistakes.

---

# Major Penalty Rules

Apply these consistently.

## Dead Character Appears Alive

If a character is clearly dead in Chapter 1 but later appears alive without flashback, diary, letter, recording, dream, or quoted testimony:

* Character Clarity: max 3
* Plot Structure: usually max 5
* Mystery Logic: usually max 6
* Ending: usually max 4
* Overall score usually should not exceed 60

If that dead character is also revealed as culprit:

* Character Clarity: max 2
* Ending: max 3
* Overall score usually should not exceed 55

## Victim Unnamed

If the victim is unnamed or only called “the victim” for most of the story:

* Opening Hook loses points.
* Character Clarity loses points.
* Ending loses emotional impact.
* Overall score usually should not exceed 72 unless everything else is very strong.

## Pronoun Instability

If pronouns repeatedly switch:

* Character Clarity should usually be 5 or lower.
* Prose / Polish should usually be 5 or lower.
* If pronoun errors make scenes hard to follow, overall score should usually stay below 70.

## Prompt / Template Leakage

If the story includes validation text, planning notes, scene metadata, or instruction-like sentences:

* Prose / Polish should usually be 4 or lower.
* Pacing may lose points.
* Atmosphere may lose points.
* Overall score usually should not exceed 65 unless leakage is very minor.

Examples of leakage:

* “The chapter moves forward through vivid detail instead than recap.”
* “Required evidence was kept explicit in scene action.”
* “The elapsed time was confirmed as forty minutes.”
* “At Study, Early afternoon settled over the investigation…”

## Weak Murder Method

If the story explains the concealment mechanism but not how the victim died:

* Mystery Logic loses points.
* Ending usually cannot exceed 6.
* Overall score usually should not exceed mid-70s.

## Early Full Explanation

If Chapter 1 explains the full mechanism or says what the culprit did:

* Opening Hook loses points.
* Mystery Logic loses fair-play tension.
* Pacing loses points.
* Overall score may still be decent, but the opening should be marked down.

# Chapter Balance Standards

Use these when judging whether the story is balanced.

## Chapter 1: Discovery / Setup

Target:

* Setting: 15–20%
* Character introductions: 35–40%
* Discovery and reactions: 25–30%
* First clue: 10–15%

Chapter 1 should introduce the human mystery before the mechanism.

A strong Chapter 1:

1. Establishes setting and mood briefly.
2. Introduces the investigator.
3. Names and shows the victim.
4. Introduces major suspects with role, relationship to victim, surface behaviour, and hidden tension.
5. Plants the first clue.
6. Ends with a question or contradiction.

Penalise Chapter 1 if:

* The body appears too late.
* The victim is unnamed.
* Suspects are not meaningfully introduced.
* The full solution is explained.
* Technical clue mechanics dominate.
* Setting takes too much space.

## Early Investigation Chapters

Target:

* Setting: 10–15%
* Character pressure: 35–40%
* Clues / questioning / contradictions: 45–50%

These chapters should pressure suspects and develop clues.

Penalise if:

* They reintroduce biography.
* They repeat Chapter 1 facts.
* They explain clues without changing the investigation.
* They add atmosphere without movement.

## Suspect Pressure Chapters

Target:

* Setting: 5–10%
* Character pressure: 45–55%
* Evidence / alibi testing: 35–45%

These chapters should reveal character under pressure.

Good character pressure includes:

* fear
* motive
* shame
* loyalty
* hidden relationship
* contradiction
* partial lie
* non-murder secret
* emotional reaction to evidence

Penalise if:

* The suspect only repeats their alibi.
* The scene is biography instead of pressure.
* The suspect confesses too early.
* Nothing changes by the end.

## False Suspect Clearing Chapters

Target:

* Setting: 5–10%
* Character consequence: 30–40%
* Evidence proving innocence: 50–60%

A false suspect should be cleared by evidence, not convenience.

Penalise if:

* The chapter simply states “they were cleared.”
* No planted clue supports the clearance.
* The emotional reason they looked guilty is not revealed.
* The clearance does not shift suspicion.

## Clue Reinterpretation Chapters

Target:

* Setting: 5–10%
* Character reaction: 25–35%
* Clue reinterpretation: 55–65%

These chapters should change what an earlier clue means.

Penalise if:

* The clue is repeated but not reinterpreted.
* The chapter introduces decisive new evidence from nowhere.
* The interpretation does not affect suspects.

## Discriminating Test Chapters

Target:

* Setting: 5–10%
* Character reaction: 20–25%
* Test / proof / reversal: 65–70%

A discriminating test must separate one theory from another.

A strong test has this structure:

1. State the competing theories.
2. Perform or reveal the test.
3. Show an observable result.
4. Explain what the result proves.
5. Explain what the result rules out.
6. Show suspect reactions.
7. End with a changed investigation state.

Penalise if:

* No actual test happens.
* The chapter only recaps evidence.
* The result does not rule anything out.
* The test does not change the investigation.

## Final Reveal Chapters

Target:

* Setting: 5–10%
* Motive / emotional truth: 30–35%
* Evidence chain: 35–45%
* Confession / consequence: 20–25%

Penalise if:

* It reads like a report.
* Motive is vague.
* Murder method is unclear.
* Culprit confesses only to tampering.
* Innocent suspects are cleared by summary only.
* There is no consequence.

# How to Score Overall

After scoring the 10 categories, add them for a total out of 100.

Then check whether the total feels consistent with these calibration examples:

## 75–80

Strong concept, coherent roles, planted clues, clear culprit, some prose and polish issues.

## 68–74

Good mystery spine, but victim naming, clue math, final method, or pronoun errors still hold it back.

## 60–67

Promising but rough. Structure mostly works, but character pressure, prose, clue clarity, or continuity need work.

## 50–59

Good ingredients but serious execution problems, such as dead character appearing alive, unclear culprit/victim, weak reveal, or repeated prompt leakage.

## 40–49

Major contradictions. Story is hard to trust as a mystery.

## Below 40

Severe incoherence.

If the mathematical total seems too generous because of a major continuity problem, lower the final score to match the calibration band.

# Scoring Style

Be direct, specific, and consistent.

Do not say only “good” or “bad.”

Always explain what caused the score.

Prefer concrete comments like:

* “The victim is named, which improves clarity.”
* “The clock mechanism is explained too early.”
* “The false suspect is cleared by assertion rather than evidence.”
* “The culprit confesses to tampering but not to murder.”
* “The victim appears alive after death, which breaks continuity.”
* “The chapter repeats the same alibi without adding pressure.”
* “The final reveal needs motive, method, opportunity, and consequence.”

Avoid vague comments like:

* “Needs more depth.”
* “Could be better.”
* “Interesting but flawed.”

Always connect criticism to story function.

---

# Most Important Priorities

When in doubt, prioritise these:

1. Is the victim clearly named and consistently dead?
2. Is the investigator stable?
3. Is the culprit distinct from the victim?
4. Are pronouns stable?
5. Does each suspect have motive, opportunity, pressure, and alibi?
6. Are clues planted before they are used?
7. Does the timing or mechanism make sense?
8. Does each chapter change the investigation state?
9. Does the final reveal explain the murder, not just the cover-up?
10. Does the prose read like fiction rather than generated notes?

A story with a brilliant mechanism but broken character continuity should not score highly overall.

A story with a simple mechanism but clear roles, fair clues, strong pressure, and a satisfying reveal can score well.
