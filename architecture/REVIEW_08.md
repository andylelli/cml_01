# Architecture Review 08 — the target is 90, and the work that gets there is not the work we have been doing

**Written:** 2026-08-07 · **Type:** a gap analysis against a raised bar, plus the open-issue board.
Supersedes [REVIEW_07](REVIEW_07.md)'s ordering; [REVIEW_05](REVIEW_05.md) remains the deep record.

**The target moved from 80 to 90.** 80 has been hit — twice, externally, on the same premise (84 and
86). This document is about what 90 costs, and the honest answer is that it is a different kind of
work from the last two weeks'.

## 1. The finding that should reorder everything

Three external cold reads, same rubric, ten categories each. Laid side by side:

| category | 84 | 86 | 76 | **best ever** | gap to 9 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| premise | 9 | 9 | 8 | **9** | — |
| plot structure | 8 | 9 | 6 | **9** | — |
| mystery clues | 8 | 9 | 7 | **9** | — |
| ending / reveal | 8 | 9 | 5 | **9** | — |
| opening hook | 8 | 8 | 8 | 8 | **1** |
| character clarity | 8 | 8 | 7 | 8 | **1** |
| atmosphere | 8 | 8 | 8 | 8 | **1** |
| pacing | 8 | 8 | 7 | 8 | **1** |
| prose / polish | 7 | 8 | 6 | 8 | **1** |
| **dialogue** | **7** | **7** | **6** | **7** | **2** |

**Four categories already reach 9. Six never have. Dialogue has never exceeded 7 in any read.**

And the four that reach 9 — premise, plot, clues, ending — are precisely the four that geometry, the
regen ladder, the acceptance test and every item on the REVIEW_05 board target. **We have spent two
weeks improving the categories that were already topping out, and almost none on the six that cap the
score.**

That is not an argument that the structural work was wrong: the 76 run shows what happens when the
ending collapses (5/10), and the machinery that detects it is why we know. It is an argument about
*what comes next*. At 80, fixing the ending was the difference. At 90, it cannot be — the ending
already scores 9 when the story is sound.

**To reach 90 the six capped categories must move, and dialogue must move twice.**

## 2. What the readers actually say about the capped categories

Mined from the three reviews, in their own words.

**Dialogue (7, 7, 6) — the hard ceiling.** All three name the same thing:

> *"some lines are repetitive"* · *"Mostly good, though some catchphrases are still awkward… they sound
> like template residue"* · *"several lines are malformed/generated"*

with quoted examples: *"One mustn't leap to conclusions about polished facades often deceive the
untrained eye"*, *"Goodness these tangled puzzles we all must navigate rough seas"*, *"That's how it's
tradition governs nothing worth reimagining the wheel"*.

These are **grammatically broken sentences** — two idioms fused mid-clause. MEASURED across the three
manuscripts:

```
84  secret_chime      0 tic-shaped lines
86  weighted_pendulum 3                  (4.3 per 1000 sentences)
76  silent_deception 10                  (16.8 per 1000 sentences)
```

**Nothing in the pipeline detects them.** Not the linter, not the validators, not geometry. They are
generated by the character-voice system, they survive every gate, and all three readers flagged them.
Tracked as **X30**.

**Prose / polish (7, 8, 6).** Two distinct families, and only one is known:
1. *injector templates* — the *"was responsible; the evidence allowed no other reading"* sentence, which
   [§32.2](REVIEW_05.md) shows a reader striking out by name. M5 territory, first external evidence in
   hand.
2. *voice-tic glitches* — X30 above. Undetected.

**Character clarity (8, 8, 7), atmosphere (8, 8, 8), pacing (8, 8, 8), hook (8, 8, 8).** Strikingly
stable, never below 7, never above 8. Nobody complains about them in detail — which means there is no
defect to fix and the ceiling is *craft*, not correctness. **These will not move by adding detectors.**
That is the uncomfortable half of this document.

## 4. What 90 requires, stated plainly

**The instrument is ready for it.** The judge is calibrated (88.2% ranking, n=9) with a usable offset
of about +11, so an internal 79 predicts an external 90 — and [M1c](REVIEW_05.md) says any single
score carries ±3, so 90 must be demonstrated with **repeats**, not one read.

**Three things have to happen, in this order:**

1. **Stop the ending collapsing.** 5/10 on the 76 run cost roughly ten points on its own. The detector
   works; the repair is blocked. **N7.** Nothing else on the board is worth more.
2. **Kill the generated-prose families.** X30 (undetected, free to build) and M5 (evidenced, needs a
   decision). Together they are the whole of the prose and dialogue complaint, and dialogue is the only
   category two marks short.
3. **Then the craft ceiling** — hook, character, atmosphere, pacing, stuck at 8 with nobody complaining.
   No detector will move these. This is prompt and model work: M6 (give Agent 9 the rubric it is judged
   by) is the cheapest probe, and [THINK_01](THINK_01.md) Move 4 (a frontier model for generation) is
   the honest large one.

**What will not get to 90:** more detectors on plot, clues and endings. Those categories already score
9 when the story is sound.

## 6. The rule that survives from REVIEW_07

**No quality claim from a single scoring.** M1c measured a six-mark spread on identical input. At 80
that was a nuisance; at 90, where the margin between categories is one mark, it is the difference
between a result and a guess. Every claim in this document about a 9 or an 8 rests on **one** read per
story, and the three-read sample is small enough that the category table above should be treated as
the best available estimate, not a measurement.

The first thing R6 buys is the right to stop saying that.
