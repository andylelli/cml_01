# Architecture Review 08 — the target is 90, and the work that gets there is not the work we have been doing

**Written:** 2026-08-07 · **Type:** a gap analysis against a raised bar, plus the open-issue board.
Supersedes [REVIEW_07](REVIEW_07.md)'s ordering; [REVIEW_05](REVIEW_05.md) remains the deep record.

**The target moved from 80 to 90.** 80 has been hit — twice, externally, on the same premise (84 and
86). This document is about what 90 costs, and the honest answer is that it is a different kind of
work from the last two weeks'.

---

## 1. The finding that should reorder everything

Three external cold reads, same rubric, ten categories each. Laid side by side:

| category | 84 | 86 | 76 | **best ever** | gap to 9 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| premise | 9 | 9 | 8 | **9** | — |
| plot structure | 8 | 9 | 6 | **9** | — |
| mystery clues | 8 | 9 | 7 | **9** | — |
| ending / reveal | 8 | 9 | 5 | **9** | — |
| opening hook | 8 | 8 | 8 | 8 | **1** |
| character clarity | 8 | 8 | 7 | 8 | **1** |
| atmosphere | 8 | 8 | 8 | 8 | **1** |
| pacing | 8 | 8 | 7 | 8 | **1** |
| prose / polish | 7 | 8 | 6 | 8 | **1** |
| **dialogue** | **7** | **7** | **6** | **7** | **2** |

**Four categories already reach 9. Six never have. Dialogue has never exceeded 7 in any read.**

And the four that reach 9 — premise, plot, clues, ending — are precisely the four that geometry, the
regen ladder, the acceptance test and every item on the REVIEW_05 board target. **We have spent two
weeks improving the categories that were already topping out, and almost none on the six that cap the
score.**

That is not an argument that the structural work was wrong: the 76 run shows what happens when the
ending collapses (5/10), and the machinery that detects it is why we know. It is an argument about
*what comes next*. At 80, fixing the ending was the difference. At 90, it cannot be — the ending
already scores 9 when the story is sound.

**To reach 90 the six capped categories must move, and dialogue must move twice.**

## 2. What the readers actually say about the capped categories

Mined from the three reviews, in their own words.

**Dialogue (7, 7, 6) — the hard ceiling.** All three name the same thing:

> *"some lines are repetitive"* · *"Mostly good, though some catchphrases are still awkward… they sound
> like template residue"* · *"several lines are malformed/generated"*

with quoted examples: *"One mustn't leap to conclusions about polished facades often deceive the
untrained eye"*, *"Goodness these tangled puzzles we all must navigate rough seas"*, *"That's how it's
tradition governs nothing worth reimagining the wheel"*.

These are **grammatically broken sentences** — two idioms fused mid-clause. A first pass counted the
shapes the reviewers happened to quote (0, 3 and 10 across the three manuscripts), but that was
pattern-matching their examples rather than detecting the class. **§7 supersedes it** with a general
measurement and a corrected read of what the defect actually is: not nonsense, but *duplication* — a
character recycling a content-free line.

**Nothing in the pipeline detects them.** Not the linter, not the validators, not geometry. They are
generated by the character-voice system, they survive every gate, and all three readers flagged them.
Tracked as **X30**, now measured but deliberately not gated.

**Prose / polish (7, 8, 6).** Two distinct families, and only one is known:
1. *injector templates* — the *"was responsible; the evidence allowed no other reading"* sentence, which
   [§32.2](REVIEW_05.md) shows a reader striking out by name. M5 territory, first external evidence in
   hand.
2. *voice-tic glitches* — X30 above. Undetected.

**Character clarity (8, 8, 7), atmosphere (8, 8, 8), pacing (8, 8, 8), hook (8, 8, 8).** Strikingly
stable, never below 7, never above 8. Nobody complains about them in detail — which means there is no
defect to fix and the ceiling is *craft*, not correctness. **These will not move by adding detectors.**
That is the uncomfortable half of this document.

## 4. What 90 requires, stated plainly

**The instrument is ready for it.** The judge is calibrated (88.2% ranking, n=9) with a usable offset
of about +11, so an internal 79 predicts an external 90 — and [M1c](REVIEW_05.md) says any single
score carries ±3, so 90 must be demonstrated with **repeats**, not one read.

**Three things have to happen, in this order:**

1. **Stop the ending collapsing.** 5/10 on the 76 run cost roughly ten points on its own. The detector
   works; the repair is blocked. **N7.** Nothing else on the board is worth more.
2. **Kill the generated-prose families.** X30 (now measured — §7 — with a lead worth more than the
   detector: both pre-voice-contract stories score ZERO) and M5 (evidenced, needs a
   decision). Together they are the whole of the prose and dialogue complaint, and dialogue is the only
   category two marks short.
3. **Then the craft ceiling** — hook, character, atmosphere, pacing, stuck at 8 with nobody complaining.
   No detector will move these. This is prompt and model work: M6 (give Agent 9 the rubric it is judged
   by) is the cheapest probe, and [THINK_01](THINK_01.md) Move 4 (a frontier model for generation) is
   the honest large one.

**What will not get to 90:** more detectors on plot, clues and endings. Those categories already score
9 when the story is sound.

## 6. The rule that survives from REVIEW_07

**No quality claim from a single scoring.** M1c measured a six-mark spread on identical input. At 80
that was a nuisance; at 90, where the margin between categories is one mark, it is the difference
between a result and a guess. Every claim in this document about a 9 or an 8 rests on **one** read per
story, and the three-read sample is small enough that the category table above should be treated as
the best available estimate, not a measurement.

The first thing R6 buys is the right to stop saying that.

## 7. Steps 1–3, as built

**X29 — the contradiction is gone, on both paths.** `runSuspectEliminationRegenPass` targeted *"the last
chapter that names the suspect"*, falling back to the final chapter, which put clearances squarely in the
aftermath — whose contract forbids them. It now takes `lastClearanceChapter`, wired from geometry's reveal
chapter, so a clearance can never land after the reveal. Suspects are cleared during the investigation;
the aftermath owes consequence. Omitting the argument keeps the old unbounded behaviour, so nothing
outside the geometry path changes.

**And bounding the pass alone was half a fix** — found 2026-08-14 by asking what *else* writes a
clearance, not by a run. `enforceSuspectEliminationPresence`, the deterministic
floor beneath it, targets the same *"last chapter that names the suspect"* through the shared
`injectSentenceIfAbsent`, and the aftermath names everyone. It is the writer that runs when regen cannot
dramatize a clearance, and the *only* writer when `AGENT9_REGEN_SUSPECT_ELIM` is off, so the sentence the
aftermath contract forbids kept its own route into the aftermath — where `aftermath_repeat` then spends an
LLM call taking it back out again. Both call sites (the main floor and the deterministic-repair path) now
take the same ceiling from the same `lastClearanceChapter`, read once. Culprit-evidence and resolution are
deliberately left unbounded: those belong at the end.

Pinned by tests on both sides — the pass's ceiling and its fallback in `regen-integration.test.ts`, the
floor's in `eliminate-suspects-detective-a67.test.ts` — each with an omitted-argument case holding the old
behaviour, because the geometry-off path must not change.

**The residual, stated rather than fixed — X32.** A third family writes clearance prose —
`buildDeterministicClearanceParagraph`, three call sites, counted by `deterministic_clearance_paste_count`
— and it is not last-chapter targeting: it pastes where the CML's `suspect_clearance_scenes` and the
act-scoped fallback in `resolveBatchMatchingClearances` say, so a clearance scheduled into the final act
can still reach an aftermath chapter. That is a scheduling question for the ledger, not the same defect,
and `aftermath_repeat` is the detector that would report it.

**X30 — measured, and deliberately not shipped.** The first detector counted near-duplicate dialogue
and flagged the BEST-scoring story as heavily as the worst, because it could not tell two things
apart:

| | |
|---|---|
| legitimate | a suspect restating their alibi — *"I was in the dining hall from half past eight until just before nine"* — which a mystery requires |
| the defect | a character recycling a content-free catchphrase — *"We must consider the implications, don't you agree?"* |

The discriminator is **case content**: an alibi carries a time, a place or a name; a tic carries none.
With that filter:

```
story                external   dialogue   tics per 100 lines
clockwork_deceit          80        —            0.0
clocks_deception          68        —            0.0
secret_chime              84        7            0.8
weighted_pendulum         86        7            3.9
silent_deception          76        6            6.9
```

It fires hardest on the story readers marked lowest for dialogue, and the instances it flags on that
story are the exact lines the reviewer quoted. **But n = 5, and 84 versus 86 does not separate**, so it
is a MEASUREMENT and not a gate — [§6](REVIEW_05.md)'s rule, applied to a detector I wanted to be true.
`npm run probe:dialogue-tics`.

The two pre-voice-contract stories score 0.0, which is consistent with the defect having been
introduced by the character-voice system rather than being inherent to the model. That is a testable
lead and the cheapest next step on dialogue.

**X22 — decided: stop widening.** The aftermath detector misses restatements carried by a pronoun and
an evidence list. Catching those means resolving pronouns to a referent and reading an evidence list as
an argument, which is not a regex. Combined with X20, X21, X27 and X28 — four false-positive or
scope defects in one day, all in the same family — [§8](REVIEW_05.md)'s fourth bullet has arrived:
*the problem may be that these detectors are regex over prose at all.* Recorded as the honest limit
rather than chased.

**One flake, twice now.** `npm test` exited 1 with `Worker exited unexpectedly` in `@cml/api` and
`@cml/llm-client`, then passed on re-run at the full 2,714. The earlier occurrence named a different
workspace. A moving failure across workspaces is a runner/resource problem rather than a code defect,
but it is the guard everything rests on and it has now happened twice. Watch it. **2026-08-14: two
consecutive clean full runs** at 2,721 (the X29 floor tests are the +7), neither flaking. Two
occurrences, none since; the watch stays open — X31.
