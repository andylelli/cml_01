This is one of the stronger non-clock/non-hourglass drafts. The core idea is good: the lantern is a false time witness, and the tide/sightline test exposes the lie. It is more concrete than the weaker hourglass versions because the final proof is physical and visual: at a certain tide height, the lantern simply cannot be seen from the cliffs.

The roles are clear:

Investigator: Eleanor Voss
Victim: Dr. Mallory Finch
Culprit: Captain Ivor Hale
False suspects: Beatrice Quill, Sylvia Trent, Hugo Vane
Murder method: stabbing with a stiletto dagger
Cover-up mechanism: Hale stages the dock lantern signal early, then relies on witnesses misremembering it as later, near high tide
Key clues: tide tables, lantern visibility, dock footprints in silt, soot on Hale’s cuff, lantern-glass shard in Hale’s coat, smudged dock logbook, Hale’s notes about Finch and dock arrangements, torn fabric from Finch’s coat near dock
Motive: Finch threatened to expose Hale’s debts/dock irregularities to the board

I’d score it around 85/100. The opening is strong: Finch is found in the hotel foyer with a precise collarbone wound, the tide tables are immediately planted, and witnesses have already connected the lantern to a late-night time claim.

What works

The lantern/tide mechanism is the standout.

The story gives a clear contradiction:

Witnesses think the lantern was visible late, near 11:30 p.m.
But the tide and cliff angle show the lantern stopped being visible at 10:40 p.m.
Therefore the “late” lantern sighting was false or misremembered.
Hale used that false hour to shift the timeline.

That is a strong fair-play clue because it uses geography, tide, and sightline rather than just testimony. The final recreation, where the lantern disappears from view as the tide rises, is especially effective.

The evidence against Hale is also much harder than in several earlier drafts:

soot on his cuff
soot matching the lantern chimney
glass shard from the lantern caught in his coat
smudged dock logbook entries
Hale’s notes about Finch and the dock arrangements
torn fabric from Finch’s coat snagged near the dock
dock footprints made before the tide returned
Hale’s knowledge of tide tables and lantern maintenance

That gives the reveal more weight than a simple confession.

The motive is better integrated, too. Hale’s private notes directly connect Finch, the dock arrangements, and his fear of exposure. That is stronger than a motive appearing only in the confession.

Main remaining issues
1. The tide timing is good but still slightly muddy

The draft uses:

high tide: 11:30 p.m.
lantern last visible: 10:40 p.m.
window: 35 minutes

But 10:40 to 11:30 is 50 minutes, not 35. Earlier, Chapter 2 says low water ran out at 10:40 p.m., while Chapter 3 says the lantern was last visible at 10:40 p.m. and high tide was 11:30 p.m.

You need one exact tide rule:

The lantern was visible from the cliff only until 10:55 p.m.
High tide was at 11:30 p.m.
That left a thirty-five-minute false window.
Anyone claiming to see the lantern at 11:20 or later was relying on Hale’s staged earlier signal, not a real sighting.

Or:

The lantern was last visible at 10:40 p.m.
High tide was at 11:30 p.m.
That gives a fifty-minute gap, not thirty-five.

Right now the mechanism works, but the arithmetic needs correcting.

2. The murder location shifts slightly

Chapter 1 finds Finch dead in the foyer, but later the reveal says Hale waited for her by the dock and the torn fabric is snagged near the dock. That can work if Hale killed her at the dock and moved the body, or attacked her near the dock and she staggered back, but the draft does not state that clearly.

Add one sentence:

“Hale stabbed Finch at the dock path, then dragged her through the side entrance into the foyer before staging the lantern signal.”

Or:

“Finch reached the foyer after being stabbed at the dock, collapsing before she could name him.”

Pick one. The current draft leaves a gap between dock evidence and foyer body.

3. The final test repeats after the confession

Chapter 8 already gives the lantern recreation, evidence, Hale’s admission, and confession. Then it repeats the diagram/test sequence and says the test “came out the same way twice.” That should mostly come before the confession or be shortened after it.

Best order:

recreate lantern visibility
show soot/glass/logbook/fabric evidence
clear Beatrice/Sylvia/Hugo
accuse Hale
Hale confesses
stop proving

4. Chapter 9 and Chapter 10 overlap

Chapter 9 clears the innocent suspects. Chapter 10 again says every other suspect is cleared and then reveals Hale. Since Hale already confessed in Chapter 8, Chapter 10 should not be called “The Culprit Revealed” unless Chapter 8 ends before confession.

Cleaner structure:

Chapter 8: final test and accusation, but no confession yet.
Chapter 9: Hale confession and arrest.
Chapter 10: aftermath only.

Or:

Chapter 8: final test, confession, arrest.
Chapter 9: aftermath only.
Delete Chapter 10 or merge it.

5. Generated/prose leakage remains

These lines need removal or rewrite:

“It had taken thirty-five minutes in all.”
“Thirty-five minutes had passed before it was done.”
“I just hoped to avoid dismissal dr finch...”
“I only ever needed that paycheck dr finch...”
“Run again in front of them all...”
“You did it. The words settled...”

They are not natural story prose. The malformed Sylvia lines especially hurt polish.

6. The title says “False Hour,” which is good — make the mechanism match it exactly

The title works because the whole crime depends on a false witnessed hour. But the reveal should include one clean line:

“The lantern did not prove Finch was alive at 11:30; it proved only that someone had lit it earlier and let witnesses attach the wrong hour to its glow.”

That would make the title feel earned.

Fastest fixes
Fix the arithmetic: either make the gap 35 minutes or change the text to 50 minutes.
State exactly where Finch was stabbed and how the body reached the foyer.
Move the repeated test before Hale’s confession or cut it.
Merge Chapters 9–10 into one aftermath chapter.
Remove malformed Sylvia lines and all “run again / it had taken” leakage.
Keep the soot, glass shard, dock notes, torn fabric, and lantern visibility test — those are the strongest clues.
Overall

This is one of the best recent mechanism drafts. The tide/lantern test is visual, fair, and tied tightly to the seaside setting. It feels more distinctive than the basic clock drafts and more mechanically complete than the hourglass drafts.

As written: 85/100.

With the tide arithmetic fixed, the body-location path clarified, and the duplicated ending cleaned up, this could reach 90–92/100.
