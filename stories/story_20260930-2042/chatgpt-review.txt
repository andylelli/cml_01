What works
The opening now has a proper mystery hook. Reginald is found at the lighthouse base with ligature marks, a silk scarf fragment, a strange ledger entry, and a mannequin arm discovered almost immediately. That gives the reader a clear puzzle: why did witnesses think there were two people when the physical evidence suggests one?    Pasted text

Main issue: the timing still needs one clean version
The final reveal says Charles had a window from 7:45 to 9:00 to commit the murder and build the illusion. But the story also uses 9:00 shadows, 9:15 ledger entry, and a “quarter to nine” calculation earlier. That is close, but not fully locked.    Pasted text(20260930-194325)
Use one authoritative timeline:
7:45 — Charles enters the lighthouse.
8:00–8:45 — Reginald is killed and the mannequin/clock-light arrangement is prepared.
9:00 — witnesses see the false double shadow.
9:15 — Franklin’s altered ledger entry falsely places Charles back at the hotel.

Then Edith’s deduction becomes:
“The shadow at nine was not proof Charles was elsewhere. It was the last part of his arrangement.”

That would make the mechanism click.

Chapter 9 reveal works, but Charles needs one more line
Charles’s confession is clear:
“He was about to expose me... It would have cost me the Hemsworth estate.”

That works, but it is a little thin. Add one sentence that ties the forged codicil directly to the murder:
“He had the codicil in his hand,” Charles said. “By morning, everyone would have known it was mine.”

That would make the motive feel less abstract and more immediate.
