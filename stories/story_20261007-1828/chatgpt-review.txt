What works
The opening is stronger than the last version. The cold manor, converted circus stables, sealed bedroom, ordinary lock, fog, and circus objects in the room all establish the world before the clue-work begins.    Pasted text
The body setup is also better: Cecil is found with a clean puncture wound, the blood is already dried, the door is locked, the police/doctor are delayed, and the hinge/pivot is discovered beneath the carpet early. That gives the story a genuine sealed-room problem.    Pasted text
The clock clue is clear and useful. The clock is stopped at 9:15, but supper clearing, the stable bell, staff logs, and witness statements point toward a death closer to 9:45. That cleanly clears Harriet, Ottoline, and Marguerite while leaving Ivor exposed.    Pasted text
Biggest improvement: Chapter 8 now works
This is the major upgrade. In the previous version, Adela opened the panel too early, which weakened the demonstration. Here, Harriet tries first and fails; then Ivor kneels, presses, rotates, and opens the panel smoothly. That is much stronger because Ivor incriminates himself physically.    Pasted text
The proof chain is now satisfying:
Harriet cannot operate the panel.
Ivor can.
The passage fits the room geometry.
The letter opener bears Ivor's marks.
The clock creates the false time.
The alibis clear the women.
Therefore only Ivor had route, means, time, and motive.

That is the cleanest version of the solution so far.
Main issue: Ivor is still a bit too obvious
By Chapter 2–3, Ivor is already strongly framed: he is the legal man, knows the library, controls records, speaks defensively about the passages, and has visible anxiety around the panel/desk.    Pasted text
It is not fatal, because the mechanism is satisfying, but suspense would improve if the fingerprint/letter-opener connection were held back until Chapter 8. Chapter 7 currently says Harriet handled the letter opener, then later Chapter 8 says Ivor’s prints are decisive. That works, but it risks muddling the weapon evidence.    Pasted text
Best fix:

Chapter 8: reveal the strong print is Ivor's, and Harriet's earlier handling was innocent/older.

That preserves suspense and clarifies the evidence.
