## Chapter 10: The Evening After

"Isabel Morton's been gone since dawn," Eleanor Gresham said, her voice carrying through the hotel lounge as she folded a linen napkin and set it on the empty chair where Isabel would have sat. The dusk pressed against the windows, the sea below obscured by gathering shadows, and the lounge itself felt changed—quieter, as if the absence of one had shifted the balance of every conversation. Reginald Gresham Jr. paced beside the fireplace, his hand resting on the mantel, while Agatha Pemberton sorted her files at the writing desk, each sheet placed with deliberate care.

Reginald Gresham Jr. reached for the ledger, thumbing through its pages, his mind wandering to the inheritance he had once fought for, now less urgent.
