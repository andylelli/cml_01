This is better than the theatre cue drafts in concept and warmth. The village/festival mistaken-identity angle feels fresher, more human, and more classically “country-house mystery.” The central trick — someone impersonates Gerald by wearing Dr. Yardley’s altered jacket, but forgets the signet ring/watch details — is strong.

I’d score it around 85/100.

What works

The mistaken-identity mechanism is strong. Gerald is initially suspected because of the jacket and ring, but the clues gradually reverse meaning: the jacket was altered for a slimmer man, the signet ring was absent, and the watch was worn on the wrong wrist. That is proper detective fiction misdirection.

The sewing-shop scene is one of the strongest. The altered jacket is physically specific: sleeves shortened, shoulders narrowed, stray threads still in the lining, and a bloodied scrap matching the cloth found in the study. That gives the mystery concrete evidence rather than abstract suspicion.

Kenneth’s motive is also better than many previous culprits. The will dispute gives him a personal, understandable pressure: Dr. Yardley was going to destroy the older will and remove Kenneth’s claim.

Character Life / Relationship Richness: 8/10

This is a clear strength.

The village feels socially alive. People gossip near the bakery, villagers remember the ring, Harriet worries about dignity and old scandal, Gerald feels humiliated by suspicion, Halloway wants discretion, and Kenneth is driven by resentment over inheritance and social position.

Harriet’s confession in Chapter 7 is especially good. Her lie is not puzzle-only; it comes from pride, old intimacy with Dr. Yardley, fear of village gossip, and wanting to keep her dignity. That is exactly the kind of “human lie, not murder lie” that makes false suspects feel real.

Chapter 10 also has better emotional weight than many earlier endings. Kenneth’s “observer, never the heir” grievance gives him a life beyond the mechanism, and Gerald/Harriet’s responses show the village trying to rebuild trust after suspicion.

Main issue: the timing still wobbles

The strongest mystery chain is not actually the bell/sundial timing. It is:

jacket altered for a slimmer man
Gerald’s signet ring missing
watch worn on right wrist
Kenneth has tailoring notes
Kenneth has motive through the will

That works.

The weaker part is the exact timing:

man in jacket seen at 3:10
bell at 3:15
death at 3:20
Gerald supposedly in hall until 3:15
Kenneth says he was on the green until 3:00
some villagers say the man in jacket stayed until 4:20

There are too many time claims, and not all of them are necessary. The bell/sundial material makes the story feel more complicated without proving as much as the ring/watch/jacket clues.

Cleaner version:

3:00 — Kenneth leaves the festival committee briefly.
3:10 — A man in Dr. Yardley’s altered jacket appears on the green and is mistaken for Gerald.
3:15 — Gerald is still confirmed in the village hall when the church bell sounds.
3:20 — Dr. Yardley is killed.
Afterward — the signet ring is placed on Gerald’s desk to support the false identity.

That gives the reader a clean path.

Chapter 8 reveal is good

Chapter 8 is the strongest reveal chapter. Cecil turns the clues around one by one:

Gerald always wore the signet ring, but the man at the festival did not.
Gerald wore his watch on the left, but the festival man wore it on the right.
The jacket was altered for a slimmer frame.
Harriet, Gerald, and Halloway are accounted for.
Kenneth fits the physical disguise and has the will motive.

That is a satisfying detective explanation. It feels earned.

Chapter 9 is fine, but Chapter 10 has reveal residue

Chapter 9’s clearances are acceptable because they give Gerald, Harriet, and Halloway emotional closure, not just legal clearance. Gerald apologizing to Harriet is a good human beat.

Chapter 10 is mostly good aftermath, but the ending again falls into the old generator habit:

“You did it.”
Kenneth Gaunt said nothing, and the proof on the table said the rest.

Delete that. Kenneth has already confessed. The story should end on the village/harmony note, not on a repeated accusation.

Better ending:

“Harmony is not the absence of discord,” he murmured, “but the willingness to listen when the tune goes astray.”

He did not look back as he left the drawing room. Behind him, the village began its difficult work of mending.
Scaffold/prose issues to remove

These still stand out:

“Measured out, it ran to five feet ten inches.”
“Measured out, it ran to six feet.”
“The clocks put it at ten minutes past three.”
“It was half past six by the mantel clock.”
“sixty-five minutes had passed before it was done.”
final “You did it.”

Those are generated/scaffold remnants and should be deleted or rewritten into natural prose.

Overall

This is upper-tier. The village setting gives it warmth, the mistaken-identity trick is readable, and the characters have better human motives than many earlier drafts.

As written: 85/100.

With the timing simplified, scaffold lines removed, and Chapter 10 ending cleaned, this could reach 89–91/100.
