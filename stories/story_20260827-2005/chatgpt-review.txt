What works

The hourglass object is excellent for this style of mystery. It is visual, simple, and naturally tied to ritual. The brass plate saying “twenty minutes” gives the reader a clear rule, and the half-full sand gives Eleanor something physical to test.

The murder weapon is also clean. The decorative brass gull statue, blood, hair, and temple wound all point to a single blunt-force killing. That is much better than drafts where the weapon shifts between poker, candlestick, letter opener, and paperweight.

The false suspects are cleared in a straightforward way:

Beatrice is in the kitchen.
Sylvia is in the lounge.
Hugo is on the telephone in the dining room.
Hale alone controls the hourglass ritual.

That narrowing is readable and keeps the ending focused.

Biggest problem

The hourglass math does not support the accusation.

The story says:

hourglass duration: 20 minutes
Hale says he flipped it at 10:45
Finch is seen alive at 11:10
hourglass is half full at 11:10

But if a 20-minute hourglass was flipped at 10:45, then by 11:05 it should be empty. At 11:10, it should have been empty for five minutes. So yes, half-full proves Hale’s statement is false.

The problem is the draft sometimes phrases this as:

“it would have been nearly empty”

It would not be nearly empty. It would be completely empty.

More importantly, if Finch is seen alive at 11:10, and the hourglass is half full at 11:10, that implies the hourglass was flipped around 11:00. That does not by itself prove murder. It proves the ritual time was lied about.

The solution needs a clearer murder-time relationship:

The hourglass did not mark when Finch died. It marked when Hale wanted everyone to believe the final routine began.

If the glass was half full at 11:10, it had been turned at 11:00, not 10:45. Hale lied about the ritual by fifteen minutes. That lie gave him the missing window: he killed Finch before 11:00, then reset the hourglass so it would appear the ritual had begun earlier and his movements were accounted for.

Or cleaner:

Claimed flip: 10:45.
Actual flip: 11:00.
Hourglass seen half full: 11:10.
False implication: the ritual began before Finch was last seen.
Truth: Hale reset the hourglass after attacking Finch, using the half-fallen sand to fake the order of events.

Right now the story proves Hale lied about the hourglass, but not cleanly enough how that lie hides the killing.

Main issues
1. Hale’s confession contradicts the evidence

Hale says:

“I staged the hourglass to cover my tracks, flipping it at a quarter to eleven to create an alibi.”

But if he truly flipped it at 10:45, the sand would be empty by 11:05. The whole point is that he did not flip it at 10:45. He likely flipped or reset it around 11:00.

Change confession to:

“I said I had flipped it at a quarter to eleven. I had not. I turned it later, after the blow, hoping the half-fallen sand would make the routine seem older than it was.”

2. The test is good, but it proves only one fact

The ten-minute test proves:

after ten minutes, the hourglass is half full.

Good. But the ending must then translate that into the actual timeline:

half-full at 11:10 = flipped at 11:00
therefore Hale lied about 10:45
therefore Hale had unaccounted time between 10:45 and 11:00 / 11:10
therefore Hale could kill and reset the scene

Without those steps, the deduction feels incomplete.

3. The phrase “Dr. Finch stepped out of sight near ten minutes past eleven” is confusing

If Finch was seen alive at 11:10, she cannot also be dead before then. If the body is discovered at 11:10, don’t say she was seen alive then. The draft uses both ideas at different points.

Pick one:

Option A: Finch was last seen alive at 10:50; body discovered at 11:10; hourglass half full at discovery.
Option B: Finch was seen alive at 11:10; body discovered at 11:20; hourglass half full at 11:20.

Option A is much cleaner.

4. Chapter 9 repeats clearances after the confession

Chapter 8 already clears Beatrice, Sylvia, and Hugo before accusing Hale. Chapter 9 then repeats the clearances in aftermath form. It is not disastrous, but it slows the ending.

Better:

Chapter 8: full test, accusation, confession.
Chapter 9: aftermath only — grief, Beatrice learning Hale acted to protect her, broken hotel ritual.

5. Generated/prompt leakage remains

These lines should be removed:

“Body discovery scene victim severe head”
“It had taken twenty minutes in all.”
“Dr finch was glimpsed shivering outside ten minutes past eleven.”

They feel like internal notes, not story prose.

6. Motive is underdeveloped

The ending says Hale killed to protect someone, and Chapter 9 clarifies Beatrice says, “He did it to protect me.” That is emotionally useful, but it needs planting earlier.

Add earlier:

Beatrice is connected to the documents Finch threatened to expose.
Hale visibly reacts when Beatrice’s name is mentioned.
Finch had discovered that Hale covered up something for Beatrice.
Hale’s motive is loyalty, not just reputation.

Fastest fixes
Make the hourglass timeline exact: claimed flip 10:45, actual flip 11:00, half-full at 11:10.
Change “nearly empty” to empty.
State how the false hourglass reading protects Hale.
Decide whether Finch was last seen alive at 10:50 or 11:10.
Rewrite Hale’s confession so he admits he lied about the flip time.
Cut Chapter 9’s repeated alibi clearances.
Remove note-like/generated fragments.
Plant the Beatrice-protection motive earlier.
Overall

This has a good object mystery and a cleaner suspect field than many drafts, but the solution depends on timing, and the timing is not yet sharp enough.

As written: 77/100.

With the hourglass math clarified, this could jump to 84–87/100.
