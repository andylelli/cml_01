This is a decent clock-mystery draft with a strong theatrical setting, but it is not as strong as the revised Hemsworth lighthouse draft. The central idea — victim’s wristwatch says 6:00, theatre foyer clock says 5:45, culprit has wound the public clock forward by fifteen minutes — is clear and usable. The problem is that the story repeatedly explains the same clue instead of dramatizing it, and the culprit proof against Kenneth is not yet sharp enough.
I’d score it around 78/100.
The roles are:
Investigator: Ambrose Dunmore
Victim: Prudence Merrow
Culprit: Kenneth Ingram
False suspects: Clarissa Ellery, Julian Penhale
Murder method: stabbing with theatre prop dagger
Cover-up mechanism: Kenneth kills Prudence around 6:00, then manipulates the theatre foyer clock so witnesses and logbooks believe the key time was 5:45
Key clues: Prudence’s wristwatch stopped at 6:00, foyer clock showing 5:45, fresh wear on clock gears, misaligned clock hands, logbook entries tied to the wrong clock, Kenneth’s clock-tower access, Kenneth seen in Ambrose’s dressing room where the dagger is found, fresh handling marks on dagger
Motive: Prudence was about to expose Kenneth’s resistance to change / mismanagement / cover-ups, costing him his place as stage manager

What works
The central time clue is strong. Prudence’s wristwatch is stopped at 6:00, while the theatre foyer clock shows 5:45, creating the core contradiction immediately in Chapter 1.    Pasted text
The physical clock evidence is also useful: the mechanism shows fresh wear, the minute hand is misaligned, and the clock has been shifted by fifteen minutes. That gives the reader a tangible mechanism rather than just witness confusion.    Pasted text
The theatre setting is good. Keys, prop racks, stage schedules, dressing rooms, logbooks, foyer clocks, ticketed access, and backstage rules all belong together. The setting feels coherent, and the crime grows naturally out of the institution’s routines.    Pasted text
Main issue: the trick is repeated too many times
The draft keeps returning to:
foyer clock = 5:45
wristwatch / pocket watch = 6:00
difference = fifteen minutes
clock wound forward

That point is made in Chapter 1, Chapter 2, Chapter 3, Chapter 6, Chapter 8, and Chapter 9. By the reveal, the reader is far ahead of the story.    Pasted text    Pasted text    Pasted text
Keep the clue progression like this:
Chapter 1: introduce the contradiction.
Chapter 2: show witnesses trusted the foyer clock.
Chapter 4: show logbooks copied the false clock time.
Chapter 8: prove Kenneth changed the mechanism.
Chapter 9: reveal the real sequence.

Cut most other repetitions.
The time logic needs one correction
The story repeatedly says the clock was “wound forward,” but the displayed times create a possible wording problem.
If the true time is 6:00 and the foyer clock shows 5:45, then the foyer clock has been set back fifteen minutes, not forward.
Unless the intended meaning is that Kenneth wound it forward after the murder from 5:45 to 6:00, but the text says witnesses saw 5:45 while true time was 6:00. The clean version should be:
Prudence died at 6:00.
Kenneth set the public foyer clock back to 5:45.
Witnesses and logbooks then placed events fifteen minutes too early.

Or, if you want “wound forward”:
Prudence died at 5:45.
Kenneth wound the clock forward to 6:00.
Her stopped wristwatch was then staged or altered to match the false later time.

The current evidence supports the first version better: clock set back fifteen minutes.
Kenneth’s proof needs to be more decisive
Right now Kenneth is guilty because:
he had clock-tower access,
he handled the mechanism,
he entered the dressing room where the dagger was found,
Prudence was going to expose him.

That works, but it is a little soft. Kenneth is the stage manager, so some access and handling are normal. The reveal needs one clue that turns ordinary access into guilty access.
Add one decisive physical link:
A trace of clock oil on Kenneth’s cuff matches the fresh oil inside the clock mechanism.

Or:
The dagger’s hilt bears the same black clock grease found on the winding key Kenneth used after six.

Or:
The logbook page for Kenneth’s clock-tower visit is written in the same ink as the altered entry, but the blotting paper shows he wrote it after the murder.

Then the accusation becomes more than “he had access.”
Character Life / Relationship Richness: 6/10
There are useful fragments: Ambrose’s wartime trauma, Clarissa’s fear of losing authority, Julian’s rejected set design and affair with Prudence, Kenneth’s practical stage-manager identity, Prudence’s reforming pressure.    Pasted text
But the relationships are not as alive as the Hemsworth draft. Prudence especially needs more presence. We are told she threatened Kenneth and Julian, but we do not feel her as a person.
Add one early memory that makes Prudence vivid:
Prudence had a habit of correcting a crooked prop even while speaking, as if disorder offended her physically. More than once Kenneth had found her after rehearsal, silently resetting the stage he had already locked.

That would make her reforming pressure personal and theatrical.

Chapter 9 reveal: clear but too mechanical
The reveal identifies Kenneth and lays out the method:
Kenneth reached Prudence through the prop room,
stabbed her with the theatre prop dagger,
slipped away to the clock tower,
and changed the foyer clock by fifteen minutes.

That is clear.    Pasted text
But Kenneth’s confession is thin:
“She was about to expose me.”
“She would have cost me my place.”

Give him one more line that ties his motive to the theatre’s routines:
“She wanted the board to replace me with systems and reports,” Kenneth said. “After thirty years, I was to be treated like a loose hinge.”

That would make his motive more human and more specific.
Chapter 10 is decent but too tidy
The aftermath has the right shape: Kenneth is led away, the clock is restored, Clarissa resumes control, Julian begins a new design, Ambrose puts the dagger away, and the theatre returns to routine.    Pasted text
But it is too neat. The theatre should not instantly feel healed. Add a little residue:
For the first time, the stagehands checked the foyer clock without trusting it.

That line would show that the case changed the institution.

“He surveyed the scene” repeated in Chapter 1.
“This timestamp is confirmed here but further context…” — direct scaffold note.    Pasted text

“The rule of this place…” repeated too often.

The biggest immediate deletion is this:
(This timestamp is confirmed here but further context for its investigative value will be reserved for chapter 6.)

That should never appear in the story.
Fastest fixes
1. Decide whether the foyer clock was set back or forward by fifteen minutes; the current evidence says back.
2. Reduce clock-gap repetition by at least 50%.
3. Add one decisive physical clue tying Kenneth to both dagger and clock mechanism.
4. Give Prudence one vivid pre-death memory so the victim feels alive.
5. Cut Julian’s monologues down into sharp theatrical wit.
6. Remove scaffold/meta phrases.
7. Make Chapter 10 a little less tidy by showing the theatre’s trust in routine has been damaged.
Overall
This is a solid theatre-clock mystery, with a good setting and a clear time-deception premise. It is stronger than the Ashford Manor/Pike drafts, but weaker than the revised Hemsworth lighthouse story because the emotional web is thinner and the mechanism currently has a forward/backward timing ambiguity.
As written: 78/100.
With the clock direction fixed, Kenneth tied physically to the mechanism, and the repetitive clue exposition trimmed, this could reach 84–86/100.
