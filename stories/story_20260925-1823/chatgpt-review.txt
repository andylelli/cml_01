What works

The atmosphere is much better. The country-house setting now has rain, wartime blackout curtains, rationing slips, cold rooms, old paper, damp velvet, and a dig site attached to the manor. Those details make Ashford feel more specific than the earlier version.

The forged letter clue remains the best fair-play element. The ink was not available before half past eleven, so the letter found among Langley’s papers must have been introduced after her death. That is a strong, readable deduction.

The duplicate key scene is also stronger than before. The original key locks properly; the duplicate catches but does not fully engage. That gives the “locked study” puzzle a physical explanation.

The Chapter 8 dust comparison is the best proof scene: Charles’s shoe does not match; Pike’s shoe does. The ladder dust shows a single climber, which undercuts the false story and points directly to Pike.

Main issue: the method is still too busy

The solution currently asks the reader to track:

Pike possessed the duplicate key.
The duplicate key did not fully lock the study.
Pike forged a letter after Langley’s death.
Pike used ink unavailable before 11:30.
Pike impersonated Charles Wentworth.
Pike used a forged excavation pass.
Pike left ladder dust matching his shoe.
Pike handled the silk scarf after Margot cleaned it.
Pike strangled Langley with the scarf.

That is a lot. It is followable, but not elegant.

The clean version should be:

Main deception:
Pike tried to make it look as if Charles Wentworth moved through the dig site/study route.

Three proof locks:
1. The forged letter was written after Langley died.
2. The duplicate key came from Pike and explains the locked study.
3. The ladder dust matches Pike, not Charles.

Everything else should support those three points.

The locked-room geography is still unclear

The story says Pike reached Langley in the study, escaped by the ladder, and used the duplicate key. But the physical relationship between study, dig site ladder, stables, and locked door is not as clear as the tidal-cave route.

In the reveal, Harcourt says Pike used the forged pass and duplicate key, escaping by the ladder during 10:10 to 10:45.

But I still need one clean spatial explanation:

The study door was not the true exit. Pike used the duplicate key to make the study appear locked, then left by the service passage to the dig ladder, where he wore or carried Wentworth’s pass to draw suspicion toward Charles.

Or:

The ladder was not how Pike entered the study; it was how he created Wentworth’s false trail.

Right now the draft risks making the reader ask: Why does the ladder matter to the locked study?

Timeline needs one authoritative version

The timeline is clearer than before but still not crisp enough.

Use:

9:00–10:00 — Margot and Evelyn are accounted for by kitchen/dig-site logs.
10:10–10:45 — Pike’s true murder window; Charles is supposedly at the stables.
10:45–11:30 — Pike adjusts the scene and plants evidence.
After 11:30 — Pike introduces the forged letter using the newly delivered ink.
11:30 — apparent time of death / false paper trail.

Then the deduction becomes:

The letter does not prove Langley lived until 11:30. It proves someone was manipulating her papers after death.

That is the same kind of simple “aha” your best drafts use.

Character Life / Relationship Richness: 7/10

This is improved.

Margot now has a real personal drive: a previous public dismissal, a need to prove herself through evidence, and a complicated relationship with Langley’s authority.

Evelyn’s character is also clearer. Her need for order, household loyalty, and fear of another dismissal give her scenes more emotional texture. Her Chapter 7 confession — that she lied about timing to cover a staff member whose child was ill — is a good human motive that clears her without making her bland.

Sir Edmund has wartime scarcity, rationing, estate stewardship, and fear of scandal. Charles has order, gambling debts, and pride in logs/protocol. Pike has audit/reputation anxiety. These are solid ingredients, but they need more dramatized conflict and less explanatory narration.

Humour / Wit: 6/10

There are better lines here than in the previous Pike draft.

Good:

“Wind doesn’t wear shoes.”

Also good:

“We all love thorough customs.”

And Pike’s line works once:

“Numbers don’t lie, people do.”

The problem is repetition. These still feel like assigned tags:

“Let’s cut to the chase.”
“That’s the way of things.”
“Order, Mr. Wentworth insists.”
“Numbers don’t lie, people do.”

Use each once. Let the characters be witty through situation rather than slogans.

Chapter 9 reveal is better, but still under-explained

Chapter 9 names Pike clearly and gives his motive. That is good.

But the accusation jumps too quickly from “scarf, pass, dust, key” to “Pike killed Langley.” The reveal would be stronger if Harcourt separated the proof:

“The letter lied about time.
The key lied about the locked room.
The pass lied about Wentworth.
But the dust did not lie about you.”

Then:

“Your shoe matched the single climber. Charles’s did not. There was no Wentworth at the ladder, Mr. Pike. Only you, carrying his name as cover.”

That would make the final logic much sharper.

Chapter 10 is improved but still has scaffold language

The aftermath has the right idea: Pike is led away, Margot is vindicated, Evelyn resumes duties, Charles updates household logs, Sir Edmund protects the estate, Harcourt’s reputation is preserved, and Langley is remembered through her field notebook and reading lamp.

But some phrases are still too mechanical:

“This was something he could not have done before the arrest”
“This was a task he could not have performed before the case was closed”
“The consequence for him was immediate”
“Her action showed a truth about her life”
“the last paragraph belonged to Evelyn Marsh”

Those are outline/scaffold phrases. Replace them with direct action.

For example:

Harcourt sealed the duplicate key in its envelope and wrote Pike’s name across the label.

instead of explaining that he could not have done it before.

Prose issues to fix immediately

These stand out:

“his shoes catching on the thick Persian carpet…” repeats in the first sentence.
“Harcourt closed his notebook…” repeats at the end of Chapter 1.
“Leonard Pike’s voice was even…” repeats in Chapter 4.
“Why would anyone need a second key?” repeats in Chapter 4.
“Are you suggesting Sir Edmund…” repeats in Chapter 6.
“She returned to the ladder base…” repeats in Chapter 8.
Chapter 10 has multiple scaffold lines, especially “could not have done before the arrest” and “last paragraph belonged to Evelyn Marsh.”

Fastest fixes
Clarify the geography: how study, dig ladder, service route, and key fit together.
Lock the timeline: 10:10–10:45 murder, after 11:30 forged letter.
Simplify the proof to three decisive locks: letter, key, dust.
Cut the scarf/stables subplot by half; keep it as weapon evidence.
Rewrite the reveal around: “letter lied, key lied, pass lied, dust did not.”
Remove duplicated sentences.
Remove Chapter 10 scaffold phrases.
Use each catchphrase only once.
Overall

This is a noticeable improvement over the previous Ashford draft. The atmosphere is stronger, Evelyn and Margot are more human, the forged-letter clue is good, and the dust comparison gives you a workable final proof. But it still needs structural simplification and heavy line editing.

As written: 74/100.

With the geography clarified, timeline locked, duplicated lines removed, and reveal sharpened, this could reach 82–84/100.
