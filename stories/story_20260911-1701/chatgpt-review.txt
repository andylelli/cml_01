I’d score it around 84/100.

What works

The opening has a good mystery image: Edith dead on the village hall floor, a letter opener nearby, and a painted shadow on the wall that should move with the flickering lamp but does not. That is a strong visual hook.

The clue logic is also easy to grasp. A real shadow would tremble when the lamp flickers; this one remains static. Add the untouched dust beneath it, and the reader can understand that nobody stood there.

The false-suspect material is better than average. Leonard lies to protect Hector; Hector has his own forgery shame; Ivor’s sarcasm hides grief and unease. Those are good “human lies,” not random red herrings.

Character Life / Relationship Richness: 8/10

This is one of the draft’s strengths.

The poets’ circle gives the characters a shared world beyond the murder. They have reputations, rivalries, old loyalties, artistic insecurity, embarrassment, and public shame. Edith feels like someone who mattered: strict, honest, formidable, and missed after death.

Chapter 10 is unusually good for this generator. It is mostly true aftermath: Hector proposes a memorial, Leonard expects Edith to walk in and set them right, Ivor offers tea, and Montague finds comfort in ordinary routine rather than triumph.

That helps the story feel less like a puzzle machine.

Main issue: Chapter 8 repeats itself after the confession

Chapter 8 gets to the reveal effectively: Montague shows that the shadow is painted, clears the others, accuses Quentin, and Quentin confesses.

But after Quentin says “It was me,” the chapter starts explaining the same proof again:

Montague sets out competing theories
repeats the lamp test
repeats that the shadow is still
repeats that Quentin is the only remaining suspect

That should be cut. Once Quentin confesses, stop proving.

Chapter 9 also partially repeats Chapter 8

Chapter 9 is titled “Clearing the Innocent,” which is fine, but it again explains the wound, the painted shadow, the lamp distance, the flicker rate, the alibis, and Quentin’s role.

Better structure:

Chapter 8: final test, accusation, Quentin confession.
Chapter 9: brief clearances and emotional consequences only.
Chapter 10: aftermath at the village hall.

You already have a good Chapter 10. Protect it by trimming Chapters 8–9.

Timing/location needs a clean sentence

The intended solution seems to be:

Witnesses thought Edith was in the foyer at 9:10 because they saw the shadow.
Edith was actually killed in the adjoining poets’ circle room at 9:10.
The painted shadow made her seem alive/present elsewhere.
Quentin used that illusion to protect himself.

But “same time, different room” needs clearer explanation. Why does making Edith appear in the foyer help Quentin if she was killed at 9:10 in the adjoining room?

Use something like:

“The shadow did not change the hour,” Montague said. “It changed the place. At ten minutes past nine, you needed everyone to believe Edith was standing alive in the foyer. In truth, she was already dying in the poets’ circle room, where you alone had reached her unseen.”

That makes the trick sharper: not false time, false location.

Scaffold/prose leakage to remove

These lines hurt polish:

“Hector cleared his throat, the noise sharp in the silence if the shadow is false…”
“ten twenty past nine”
“Montague Penhale set out one competing theory against the other…”
“Either witnesses believe…”
“Nobody in the room could unsee what had just happened.”
the repeated generated setting paragraph in Chapter 9

The repeated Mistlecombe description in Chapter 9 is especially obvious and should be deleted.

Small continuity fixes

Quentin’s alibi/time statement has a broken phrase:

“I was near the coat rack as the clock struck nine twenty nine forty-five.”

Make it:

“I was near the coat rack from nine twenty until nine forty-five.”

Also, Chapter 8 says Quentin murdered Edith “in the adjoining room at ten minutes past nine,” but if witnesses were looking at the foyer shadow at that exact time, the story should explain how Quentin got from the poets’ circle room to his claimed position.

Overall

This is upper-tier but not top-tier yet. The painted-shadow clue is elegant, the village poets’ circle has personality, and the final aftermath is genuinely better than usual.

As written: 84/100.

With Chapter 8 trimmed after confession, Chapter 9 shortened, the “false place not false time” explanation added, and scaffold lines removed, this could reach 89–91/100.
