## Chapter 7: The Missing Link

Eleanor Voss pressed her palm to the cold terrazzo of the stairwell landing. Past midnight, the Seabreeze Hotel had gone quiet around her, the radio in the lobby reduced to a murmur that barely climbed the stairs. Oil clung to the filigree, the same faint sheen she had seen on the latch. She had watched this cufflink catch the light at Hale's wrist not three days before. Her breath caught, then steadied. No one misplaced a cufflink here by accident, not torn like this, not hidden so badly. She closed her fingers around the fragment and slid it into her pocket, and the cold of the metal seemed to settle somewhere under her ribs. She did not move for a moment. When she did, it was to look again at the latch, as if to be certain it would still be there. The clocks put it at twenty minutes past ten at night.

She rose, knees stiff, and crossed to the window. The street lamp outside sat fixed at its old angle, and its beam came slanting through the bevelled glass. Eleanor tilted the interior lamp a few degrees and watched the shadows on the terrazzo warp and re-form, a lattice of darkness that swallowed, then gave up, a scatter of faint marks that might once have been footprints. She adjusted the angle again, and again, until the pattern from her memory of the night of the murder swam up out of the floor. At the correct angle the terrazzo looked untouched. Light and glass alone had done that. The night air found its way through the window's poor seal, and her breath began to fog faintly in the lamplight. She held still, watching how little it took-a degree, perhaps two-to shift the whole pattern of shadow and reflection. This was not an accident of architecture.

Someone had understood exactly how the windows and the fixed lamp worked together, understood it well enough to use it. She thought of Captain Hale's voice, unhurried and precise, walking her through the quirks of the hotel's windows and the lamp's mechanism as though reciting something he'd known for years. He had spoken of it too easily for an idle guest, and yet his composure had not held; she remembered the tremor that had come into his hand, the way his eyes had gone to the latch as though it might speak first. What he was protecting, she still could not say. But he was protecting something. The cufflink sat in her pocket like a stone. She pressed her palm flat against it through the fabric and felt the cold of the metal come through. This was no longer a matter of suspicion passed back and forth over tea. It could be held, turned over, weighed.

And yet the certainty brought no comfort with it-only the knowledge that speaking it aloud would tear something larger than one man's alibi. She looked once more at the shadows shifting on the floor, at the sheen on the latch, and did not move to leave. A laugh cracked the quiet of the corridor-Sylvia Trent, unmistakable even half-heard. Sometimes they show us what we've missed. " Sylvia's eyes narrowed with interest, but she asked nothing further, only lingered at the threshold, watching Eleanor return to the lamp and the window with the wary attention of someone deciding whether to be amused or afraid. Sylvia's footsteps faded, and the landing went cold and still again.

Eleanor stood with the evidence laid out in her mind if not before her eyes: the torn cufflink in her pocket, the oil on the latch, the shadows that could be made to hide almost anything. She stayed a moment longer, feeling the weight of what she now knew settle into place. There was one step left before she could not turn back from it.

## Chapter 9: Clearing the Innocent
