This is one of the stronger versions overall. It finally has a more interesting clock mechanism than “the clock was simply set wrong”: the minute hand moves normally, but the hour hand is held back by a wire/catch, creating a false visual reading. That is a good puzzle idea.

I’d score it around 84/100.

What works best

The clock-room inspection is the strongest technical scene. The draft clearly shows the wire looped around the hour wheel, the hour wheel being caught while the minute hand runs free, and Eleanor physically feeling the drag and release. That gives the solution a tactile, fair-play basis.

The core mechanism is also better than the earlier “clock fast/slow” versions because it is more specific:

the whole clock is not wrong
the minute hand is honest
the hour hand is delayed
the face gives a false reading
the pocket watch exposes the true time

That is much more satisfying.

The final test mostly works. Eleanor demonstrates the drag, identifies the wire, compares the tool marks, links the gloves and maintenance log to Hugo, and then gets a confession.

Character Life / Relationship Richness: 8/10

This is a real improvement.

The characters now feel less like alibi machines and more like people under pressure. Beatrice is not just “the manager”; she is someone terrified of disorder and reputation collapse. Sylvia has a private romantic scandal, which explains her evasiveness without making her the killer. Hugo is not just mechanically capable; he is tied to Beatrice and kills partly to protect her. Hale is not just a false suspect; he becomes someone diminished by having trusted the wrong structure of order.

Chapter 10 is especially good for this metric. It is mostly aftermath rather than another proof chapter: Hale reflects on corrupted certainty, Beatrice admits truth leaves them stranded between relief and regret, Sylvia turns the whole thing into brittle performance, and Eleanor leaves with the weight of truth rather than triumph.

That is exactly the direction you wanted.

Main issue: the time logic still needs tightening

The intended clean logic seems to be:

lobby clock appears to show 9:10
pocket watch says 9:25
difference is 15 minutes
the hour hand lag creates the false reading
Hugo uses that false time to distort alibis

That can work.

But the draft sometimes says the pocket watch is at 9:25, sometimes “quarter past nine,” and in Chapter 8 says the watch is “stalled at the edge of half-past nine.” That inconsistency hurts the mystery logic.

Lock it everywhere as:

Lobby clock visible reading: 9:10
Minute hand: 9:15 position
Hour hand: held back, making the face read falsely
Pocket watch stopped at: 9:25
True time of death: 9:25
Discrepancy: 15 minutes

Or simpler:

False lobby-clock time: 9:10
True pocket-watch time: 9:25
Difference: 15 minutes

Then remove every stray “quarter past nine” or “edge of half-past nine” unless it refers to a different clock clearly.

Motive needs one earlier plant

The ending says Hugo killed to protect a friend, then Beatrice asks:

“You did this for me, didn’t you?”

That is a strong human idea, but it arrives too late.

Plant it earlier with one small moment:

Hugo watches Beatrice being questioned and almost interrupts.
Beatrice says Finch had found something in the ledgers that would ruin her.
Hugo quietly removes a letter from Beatrice’s tray or tries to shield her from Eleanor’s questions.

That would make the reveal emotionally earned.

Chapter 9 is almost too much

Chapter 9 starts as “clearing the innocent,” which is fine, but then it effectively reopens the Hugo confession again. Since Chapter 8 already has Hugo confess, Chapter 9 should not restage his guilt. It should only clear Hale, Beatrice, and Sylvia, then let the emotional consequences surface.

Best fix:

Chapter 8: Hugo confession.
Chapter 9: clear Hale, Beatrice, Sylvia. No second confession.
Chapter 10: aftermath only.

Remaining polish issues

These lines still feel generated/scaffolded:

“It had taken fifteen minutes in all.”
“Fifteen minutes had passed before it was done.”
“Not faith-proof.”
“the blood spread wide beneath him” — Finch is female, so this should be “her.”
“stalled at the edge of half-past nine” — conflicts with 9:25.

The “beneath him” pronoun slip is small but worth fixing because it breaks trust in the prose.

Overall

This is very close to being one of the best versions. The hour-hand sabotage is a strong mechanism, the clock-room scene is tactile and fair, and the character richness is finally beginning to feel like part of the story rather than an add-on.

As written: 84/100.

With the time references locked, Hugo/Beatrice planted earlier, Chapter 9 trimmed, and the scaffold/pronoun issues removed, this could reach 89–91/100.
