This is a strong country-house mechanism draft with one serious continuity problem. The mirror-passage idea is excellent, the atmosphere is good, and Harriet/Marguerite/Adela have more life than many recent casts. But the ending currently breaks the story: Ottoline is exposed as the killer and apparently taken away, then Chapter 10 has her calmly tending the fire and doing “one last sweep” as if she is still free in the house. That has to be fixed.
I’d score it around 80/100 as written.

Investigator: Adela Halloway
Victim: Cecil Thorne
Culprit: Ottoline Fairweather
False suspects: Harriet Bellamy, Ivor Yardley, Marguerite Selwyn

Cover-up mechanism: Ottoline uses the concealed passage behind the angled corridor mirror to reach Cecil’s room unseen, then relies on dust, clock confusion, and false suspicion around the greenhouse/kitchen knife to misdirect suspicion

What works
The opening image is strong. The angled mirror, the precise dust outline, the knife, Cecil’s body, the locked-after-dinner corridor, and the tension between Harriet and Marguerite all create a proper country-house puzzle immediately.    Pasted text
The mirror mechanism is the best part. The mirror is set at forty-five degrees, shows only the corridor wall, and hides a two-foot space behind it. That is a concrete, visual trick the reader can understand.    Pasted text
The final demonstration in Chapter 8 is also good. Harriet touches the frame and leaves no disturbance; Ottoline instinctively presses the notch beneath the molding, the mirror gives way, and the dust shifts around her fingers. That is exactly the kind of live proof these mysteries need.    Pasted text
Biggest problem: Chapter 10 contradicts the reveal
Chapter 8 names Ottoline as the killer, gives her confession, and explains that she used the mirror passage to reach Cecil unseen.    Pasted text
Chapter 9 then shows Ottoline being escorted down the staircase by constables.    Pasted text(20261002-173737)
But Chapter 10 opens with Ottoline calmly asking whether she should relight the fire, tending the coals, making a joke, writing in the logbook, and then going for “one last sweep before bed.”    Pasted text(20261002-173737)
That cannot stand unless Chapter 10 is meant to happen before her arrest, but it clearly reads as aftermath. Fix it one of two ways:
Option A — remove Ottoline from Chapter 10.
Replace her fire-tending role with a new maid, Harriet, or Marguerite.

Option B — make Chapter 10 occur before the constables take her away.
Then reorder Chapter 9/10 so the arrest happens at the end.

The cleaner fix is Option A. Ottoline should be absent in the aftermath. Her absence should be felt through the house’s disorder.
