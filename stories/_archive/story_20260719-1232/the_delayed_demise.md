## Chapter 10: The Revelation

The hush in the coastal hotel dining room was broken only by the faint creak of chair legs shifting on polished floorboards and the distant, muffled sound of wind battering the windowpanes. Morning light, pale and uncertain, filtered through the heavy curtains, casting a wan glow over the white linen and silver remnants of last night's disaster. The air was thick with the scent of cold tea and the sharper tang of something chemical-an echo of the violence that had torn through the winter calm. Hugo Vane stood at the head of the table, his hand resting on the sealed glass vial, the residue of deadly poison still visible within. The others sat in a strained semicircle: Captain Ivor Hale, shoulders hunched; Beatrice Quill, her gloved hands knotted in her lap; Sylvia Trent, posture rigid, eyes rimmed with sleeplessness. Dr.
