Main issues by chapter
Chapter 1: Discovery
Victim is not named.
The chapter says “him” and “the victim,” but the reader needs to know who died.
Clock logic is shaky.
It says if the victim died at 11:10, this contradicts witnesses who saw him alive at 11:20. That works, but it should be presented as a contradiction, not a conclusion.
Eleanor says she saw him alive “just before” 11:10, but later witnesses saw him at 11:20.
This is useful if intentional, but it needs clearer framing.
Beatrice says they must wait for the investigator.
But Beatrice is acting like the investigator throughout the story. Decide whether she is detective or just a witness.
Chapter 2: Conflicting Accounts
Generated location lines still appear.
The Drawing Room/Gardens setup feels pasted in before the real scene starts.
Victim pronouns switch.
Eleanor says “him,” then “She seemed troubled,” then “her mind.” Choose one.
Awkward phrase:
“the the answers danced in the shadows.”
Detective gender changes.
Beatrice says the investigator will arrive and “She will know,” but Chapter 1 says “He will know.”
Good point: Eleanor’s inconsistent account creates suspicion effectively.
Chapter 3: The Note
Opening sentence repeats twice.
Eleanor’s timeline changes.
In Chapter 1 she last checked the clock around 11:10; here she says around 11:20.
The note is a good clue.
A meeting at 10:30 complicates the timeline and gives the investigation a concrete object.
But the chapter title promises the note, and the note only appears at the very end.
Bring it in earlier.
Awkward phrase:
“We are obliged to hold for the investigator to arrive.”
Chapter 4: Interrogating Dr. Finch
Good change: Dr. Finch is alive and has a clear hospital alibi, so she is no longer confusingly both victim and suspect.
But location changes to Caldwell Manor instead of Little Middleton Manor.
Use one estate name.
Dr. Finch’s alibi is plausible but too easy.
Medical records should be verified by a named nurse, surgeon, or operating log.
The victim’s gender switches again:
Beatrice asks about “she was seen alive at twenty minutes past eleven.”
The note time, clock time, and witness time need to be lined up more clearly.
Chapter 5: Captain Hale’s Alibi
Pronoun errors are heavy here.
Beatrice becomes “he,” Hale is “she/her,” then “his.”
Hale’s alibi is clear: he was at the club.
That helps the plot.
But Hale’s alibi is said to be solid before Beatrice actually verifies it.
Show the witness confirmation later.
“Performing my duties at the club” sounds odd if Hale was playing cards.
Still using Caldwell Manor instead of Little Middleton Manor.
Chapter 6: Eleanor’s Financial Troubles
This is one of the strongest chapters.
It finally gives Eleanor a motive: debts, foreclosure, and pressure over the estate.
The fingerprints on the clock are a strong clue.
But Beatrice states the clue too bluntly:
“This evidence points to Eleanor Voss’s involvement.”
Note-like lines remain:
“Direct evidence ties Eleanor Voss to the mechanism access point…”
Pronoun slip:
“The realization struck his like a lightning bolt.”
Good structural move: Hale is cleared, shifting pressure back to Eleanor.
Chapter 7: The Discriminating Test
The title is misleading.
There is no real test here; it is another confrontation about fingerprints and motive.
The “investigator will arrive” line repeats again.
If Beatrice is solving the mystery, remove this.
The victim gender switches again:
Beatrice says “her death,” while earlier the victim is “him.”
The chapter mostly repeats Chapter 6.
Financial trouble + fingerprints + Eleanor denial appear again.
It needs a real test.
For example: Beatrice could ask Eleanor to demonstrate how she touched the clock, then catch her knowing a hidden mechanism.
Chapter 8: Clearing Hale and Finch
Good purpose: it clears the false suspects.
But it opens with repeated location lines again.
Hale and Finch are called “both men,” but Hale and Finch are described with female pronouns elsewhere.
Finch says, “I was here,” though her alibi is at the hospital.
Beatrice repeats the idea of waiting for the investigator again.
Eleanor arrives dramatically at the end, which is a good setup for the confrontation.
Chapter 9: Confrontation
This is the clearest ending so far.
Eleanor’s financial motive, fingerprints, and cleared suspects lead to her confession.
But Beatrice arrests Eleanor.
Unless Beatrice has legal authority, she should say something like, “The police will have to hear this.”
The victim is still unnamed.
The ending says “the victim,” “him,” and “his death,” which weakens the emotional impact.
Eleanor confesses to tampering with the clock, but not clearly to murder.
She says she “never meant for it to lead to this.” That sounds like accidental involvement, not necessarily murder.
The final sentence is report-like:
“Eleanor Voss was responsible…” It should end in scene, not summary.
Best fix now

This version’s best cast setup is:

Detective figure: Beatrice Quill
Victim: Lord Ivor Voss
Culprit: Eleanor Voss
False suspects: Dr. Finch and Captain Hale
Key clues: stopped clock at 11:10, witness sighting at 11:20, note for 10:30, Eleanor’s financial trouble, fingerprints on clock mechanism.

To improve it most quickly, do these four things:

Name the victim in Chapter 1: Lord Ivor Voss.
Make the victim male throughout: he/him.
Remove all “waiting for the investigator” lines and let Beatrice be the detective.
Make Eleanor confess clearly: did she kill Ivor, or only tamper with the clock after finding him dead?
