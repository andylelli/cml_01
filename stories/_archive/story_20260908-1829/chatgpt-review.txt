I’d score it around 78/100.

What works

The opening has a clear crime scene: Adela Ashgrove is dead in the manager’s office, struck with a brass paperweight, with a suspicious ledger beside her. The body, weapon, altered record, and suspects are introduced efficiently.

The best evidence is the altered ledger page. The blotched ink, heavier handwriting pressure, chemical odor, solvent traces, and roughened paper give the investigation a concrete physical basis.

The torn glove is also useful. It gives Lavinia a physical link to the solvent and the doctored ledger pages, which is stronger than relying only on motive or authority.

Main problem: two timing tricks compete

The draft has two separate timing discrepancies:

ledger says chemical delivery at 6:30
chemist’s diary says delivery arrived 7:15
difference: 45 minutes

And:

hotel tide clock says high tide at 4:15
pier tide table says high tide at 4:05
difference: 10 minutes

Both could be useful, but the story never cleanly explains how they fit together. The chemical delivery timing points to one kind of cover-up; the tide-clock timing points to another.

By Chapter 8, the reveal says the ten-minute tide gap is the window in which the record was falsified and the crime concealed, but that does not explain the 6:30 vs 7:15 delivery contradiction.

Pick one main mechanism.

Best version:

True delivery time: 7:15.
False ledger time: 6:30.
Purpose: Lavinia makes it look as if Adela had already reviewed the chemical order earlier, before the argument.
True murder time: shortly after 7:15.
Cover-up: Lavinia uses solvent to alter the ledger after killing Adela.

Or, if you want the tide clock:

True high tide: 4:05.
Hotel tide clock falsely shows: 4:15.
Purpose: the hotel’s internal records are ten minutes slow/late, giving Lavinia a false office-window alibi.

But don’t use both unless they connect.

The tide-clock idea is underused

A coastal hotel should make the tide clue important. Right now, it feels decorative. The story mentions high tide at 4:15 versus 4:05, but the murder happens around breakfast / chemical deliveries / office logs, so the tide does not naturally prove the crime.

To make it matter, tie the body or ledger to the tide:

the chemical delivery could only reach the hotel service entrance after the causeway reopened at low tide
the harbourmaster’s tide table proves the delivery could not have arrived at 6:30
saltwater on the ledger/glove proves the forged entry was made after someone came from the pier
the tide clock was altered to support the false delivery time

Then the coastal setting becomes part of the puzzle, not just atmosphere.

Character Life / Relationship Richness: 7/10

This is decent but not exceptional.

Lavinia has a coherent identity: authority, hotel reputation, family legacy, order, control. Dorothy represents laboratory precision and resentment toward the hotel’s record-keeping. Katherine values order but softens later. Ferdinand has loyalty to the kitchen and the hotel’s daily survival.

Chapter 10 has some good aftermath: Dorothy remembers Adela’s precision, Ferdinand admits everyone resented her, and the remaining staff begin to imagine rebuilding routine after Lavinia’s removal.

But the relationships are still generic. Add one specific history:

Adela once saved Dorothy’s career by correcting a lab error.
Lavinia inherited the hotel after a family bankruptcy and has been hiding losses.
Ferdinand knows Lavinia covered for him years ago, which is why he hesitates.
Katherine resented Adela because Adela saw through the hotel’s polished lies.

That would push character richness toward 8.

Chapter 8 is overstuffed

Chapter 8 tries to do everything:

solvent traces
handwriting pressure
Lavinia’s access
torn glove
Dorothy/Katherine/Ferdinand alibis
paperweight murder
tide-clock discrepancy
motive
Lavinia’s confession/explanation

The result is comprehensible but not elegant.

A cleaner Chapter 8 should have one central demonstration:

Dorothy tests the ledger ink.
Solvent reacts.
Torn glove reacts with same chemical.
Access log shows only Lavinia entered.
Diary proves the delivery time was changed.
Lavinia’s alibi collapses.

That is enough. The tide clock should only stay if it directly proves the delivery time.

Chapter 9/10 are better separated, but still repeat

Chapter 9 mostly clears Dorothy, Katherine, and Ferdinand. That is acceptable, though it repeats evidence already used in Chapter 8.

Chapter 10 is mostly aftermath, but it still re-explains the whole case:

Lavinia killed Adela because of hotel control
blotched ledger
altered handwriting
tide clock 4:15 vs 4:05
ten-minute fatal gap

That proof belongs in Chapter 8. Chapter 10 should focus only on grief, changed relationships, and the hotel’s future.

These are generator artifacts and broken template phrases. They lower the prose score significantly.

Overall

This is a solid but not top-tier draft. It has a good setting, clear culprit, decent physical evidence, and a believable authority/reputation motive. But the mystery mechanism is trying to be three things at once: ledger forgery, chemical-tampering proof, and tide-clock timing trick.

As written: 78/100.

With one clean mechanism — preferably ledger solvent + false delivery time, with the tide clue directly tied to delivery access — this could reach 84–87/100.
