Reader's answer to "can you detect any humour in it?" — seed 1358, --humour sharp, 2026-09-15.

Yes — there is humour in it, but it is mostly dry, restrained, character-based humour, not comic set pieces. That suits the tone. It gives the story a little social sparkle without damaging the seriousness.

The humour mainly comes from Edith's dry understatement, Violet's brittle wit, and the way formal people say sharp things politely.

Examples that work:

"If only Leonard had been as punctual in life as he was in death, perhaps we should all be elsewhere this morning."

That is a good Violet line. It is morbid, socially inappropriate, and revealing: she uses wit to avoid discomfort. It also fits the story's clock motif.

"Order is a lovely thing, Lady Innes. It rarely survives inspection."

This is not laugh-out-loud funny, but it has a nice drawing-room sting. It also neatly attacks Agatha's whole worldview.

"If the clocks are to be believed, perhaps I should have committed the deed in time for supper."

This is probably the funniest line in the draft. Millicent's civility makes it sharper. It also works because it comes during the false-solution pressure, when she is being unfairly suspected.

"Only if it's likely to confess."

Edith's reply to "Do you intend to interrogate the furniture next?" is a good investigator quip. It gives her personality without making her flippant.

"You'll find the right polish, Miss Ashcombe. You always do."

This is gentler humour, almost affectionate. It works well in the aftermath because it turns Millicent's practical competence into warmth.

How much humour is there?

I'd say the humour level is about 6.5/10.

Enough to give the story life, not enough to make it feel comic. That is probably the right balance for a manor/choir-school murder.

Who carries the humour best?

Violet is the strongest comic voice. Her humour is defensive, stylish, and faintly cruel. She should keep getting the sharpest lines.

Edith should get the dry replies. Her humour should be quiet, precise, and almost reluctant.

Millicent works best with controlled, unexpectedly cutting lines.

Agatha should not be funny often. She is funniest only indirectly, because her seriousness is so rigid.

What could improve

The humour is good, but it could be more differentiated. Right now several characters speak in similar polished aphorisms. Give each a different comic flavour:

Violet: theatrical, brittle, socially sharp.
Edith: dry, investigative, understated.
Millicent: practical, disciplined, unexpectedly barbed.
Agatha: severe, unintentionally absurd in her obsession with order.

Overall: yes, definitely detectable humour, and it helps. I would add maybe 2–3 more small dry exchanges, but no big jokes. Keep it as drawing-room acid, not comedy.
