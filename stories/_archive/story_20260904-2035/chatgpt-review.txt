This is stronger than the previous Starlight Theatre ledger draft in character and atmosphere, but weaker in ending discipline because Chapter 9 and Chapter 10 repeat the confession/reveal again.

I’d score it around 82/100.

The roles are:

Investigator: Dr. Edmund Rutherford
Victim: Clarissa Gaunt
Culprit: Desmond Jardine
False suspects: Iris Norbury, Harriet Penhale
Murder method: blow to the head with ornate candlestick
Cover-up mechanism: Desmond manipulates the backstage bell / rehearsal log so the official cue appears to be 7:20, concealing the true five-minute window after the bell at 7:15
Key clues: mechanical bell at 7:15, official director’s cue at 7:20, erased pencil marks, amended rehearsal log, independent stopwatch, Desmond’s timing-device access, candlestick with blood and hair

What works

The core contradiction is clean: the mechanical bell sounds at quarter past seven, but the official director’s cue is recorded at twenty past seven. That creates a simple five-minute gap the reader can understand.

The opening plants the murder weapon clearly too: Clarissa Gaunt is found with a head wound, and the ornate candlestick beside her is bloodied with a strand of hair caught in it. That gives the murder method a solid physical anchor.

The best part of the mystery design is that the mechanism matches the theme. This is not only “a clock was wrong”; it is about authority deciding what counts as official. Desmond insists that the director’s cue matters more than the backstage bell, which makes his deception feel tied to his character.

The final trap mostly works. Rutherford places the stopwatch beside the log, demonstrates the five-minute interval between bell and public cue, and shows that the official record has been amended after the fact.

Character Life / Relationship Richness: 8/10

This is one of the better drafts for the new metric.

Desmond is more than “the culprit with access.” He represents old theatrical order, routine, control, and fear of obsolescence. His motive is personal and thematic: Clarissa challenged his authority and threatened to have him replaced.

Iris has a real voice: brittle, sardonic, defensive, and observant. Harriet is clipped and formal, with ambition and secrecy pressing under the surface. Their Chapter 10 exchange has actual human texture: rivalry softening into mutual recognition after the ordeal.

Rutherford also has a better emotional stake than some prior investigators. He is not only solving a puzzle; he remembers Clarissa’s candor, doubts, and hope for better routines, which gives the investigation a personal thread.

This is exactly the kind of texture you were aiming for: authority, trust, resentment, fear of being replaced, and the emotional cost of truth.

Main issue: Chapter 9 repeats the reveal

Chapter 8 already exposes Desmond. Rutherford runs the bell/stopwatch demonstration, explains the five-minute gap, accuses Desmond, and Desmond confesses to striking Clarissa with the candlestick.

Then Chapter 9 does it again. It starts by clearing Iris and Harriet, which is fine, but then it re-explains the candlestick, bell, erased pencil marks, motive, forged timeline, and Desmond confesses again.

That weakens the ending. Once Desmond confesses in Chapter 8, Chapter 9 should not make him confess again.

Better structure:

Chapter 8: final demonstration and confession.
Chapter 9: brief clearances only, no second accusation.
Chapter 10: aftermath only.

Chapter 10 is almost good — until the last line

Most of Chapter 10 works well. It shows Iris and Harriet processing what happened, the company losing its old order, and the possibility of a more honest future.

But the final sentences ruin the aftermath:

“You did it.”
Desmond Jardine said nothing, and the proof on the table said the rest.

That is reveal-mode again. Desmond has already confessed and been removed. This line should be deleted.

End at:

He closed his eyes briefly, then followed, the chill of the autumn air trailing behind him as he stepped out into the dimming light.

That is much cleaner.

Logic issue: why does the five-minute gap matter?

The draft explains the gap, but it still needs one very plain sentence telling the reader exactly how Desmond used it.

Add this to Chapter 8:

“The bell at quarter past seven brought the actors into motion and gave you your private window,” Rutherford said. “The public cue at twenty past became the official beginning. By changing the log to match the later cue, you made those five minutes disappear — the very five minutes in which Clarissa was killed.”

That would lock the mechanism.

Scaffold/prose leakage to cut

These lines still hurt polish:

“Five minutes had passed before it was done.”
“Dr. Edmund Rutherford set out one competing theory against the other…”
“Either The director’s shouted rehearsal start…”
“Nobody in the room could unsee what had just happened.”
final “You did it.”

Those are generator fingerprints, not fiction.

Overall

This is a good upper-tier draft. The setting, theme, and clue mechanism fit together well. The character richness is stronger than usual, and the authority/trust theme gives the mystery a real spine.

As written: 82/100.

With Chapter 9 trimmed, Chapter 10’s final reveal residue removed, and the five-minute gap explained in one clean sentence, this could reach 87–89/100.
