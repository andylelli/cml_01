What works
The opening is much stronger than the previous compass version. It begins after the murder, with the paperweight, wound, office disorder, frozen clock, and suspects all clearly in place. The earlier problem where Montague seemed alive and then suddenly dead is gone.   Pasted markdown
Nora is also more vivid here. She has a mechanical way of seeing the world, a traumatic speed-trial memory, practical confidence, and a defensive wit. The line about her teenage race failure gives her more life than a simple “mechanic suspect” label.   Pasted markdown
The compass demonstration is clear: tilted compass gives a consistent 15-degree error; level compass points true. That gives the reader a physical trick they can understand.   Pasted markdown
The ledger evidence is also cleaner than before. Ink freshness, different pens, different pressure, altered times, and the mismatch between entries at 8:45, 9:15, 9:30, and 9:45 all support the idea that the record was rewritten after the fact.   Pasted markdown
Main issue: the compass still needs a decisive sightline
The draft says the judge’s reading “placed the suspect on the beach,” and the compass error would shift the position by 15 degrees. That is promising, but it still needs one exact visible map-rule.   Pasted markdown
The story should say something like:
The judge’s tilted compass reading placed Nora on the beach path at 9:15.
But when Bertram held the compass level from the same window, the bearing pointed not to the beach path but to the hotel office side door.
That meant the judge had not seen Nora safely away from the office. He had seen her leaving it.

That would make the compass not just “interesting” but decisive.
Right now the proof is still mostly:
Nora had access.
Nora knew the compass.
Nora altered the ledger.
Gaunt threatened Nora.
Therefore Nora killed him.

That is plausible, but it needs one physical lock tying Nora to the office at the death moment.
The murder weapon link is still weak
The paperweight is introduced well and labelled as belonging to the office. But the reveal does not really use it.   Pasted markdown
Add one small decisive clue:
A crescent of machine oil on the paperweight’s underside matched the oil on Nora’s spanner.

Or:
The adhesive label on the paperweight was torn where Nora’s grease-stained sleeve had caught it; the missing fibre matched her mechanic’s jacket.

Or:
The paperweight had been taken from the locked office drawer; the brass key on Nora’s chain opened it.

That would lift the murder proof from good suspicion to solid accusation.
