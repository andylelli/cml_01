This is probably the strongest mechanism draft so far, but it still has execution damage.

Investigator: Evelyn Harcourt
Victim: Lord Charles Harcourt
Culprit: James Harcourt
False suspects: Beatrice Langley, Agnes Wilkes, Edward Mallory
Murder method: aconite poison hidden in hollowed ice cubes
Cover-up mechanism: sundial shadow delayed by heat/gnomon expansion
Supporting misdirection: stable-yard echoes, forged ledger, false timeline
Motive: James’s debts and fear of ruin

What works

This has a much better murder method than the earlier sundial draft. The death is not just “staged by a sundial”; the actual killing is aconite concealed in a hollow ice cube, with delayed poisoning explaining why Lord Charles seemed normal when he left for the garden. That gives the mystery a real method, not just a time trick.

The clue chain is strong:

thermometer reads around 80°F despite autumn chill
sundial shadow is delayed by heat
servants misread footsteps because of stable-yard acoustics
hollow ice cubes suggest delayed poison delivery
ledger entries expose James’s debts and forged timeline
final sundial test proves the time shift
James had motive, knowledge, access, and opportunity

The false accusation chapter is also good. Beatrice looks guilty because the ledger and sundial evidence seem to place her in the window, but the case collapses when the evidence is reinterpreted. That is the kind of reversal these stories need.

Biggest problem

The sundial mechanism is clever, but physically shaky.

The story says heat expands the brass gnomon enough to delay the sundial shadow by ten minutes. That is a big claim. In a realistic fair-play mystery, readers may not believe that a brass gnomon expanding in heat would shift the shadow that much.

A cleaner version would be:

The gnomon had been loosened at its base.
Heat softened/expanded the fixing, allowing it to tilt slightly.
That tilt delayed the shadow by ten minutes.
James knew this because he maintained the sundial.

That is more believable than simple metal expansion doing all the work.

Main issues
1. Prompt leakage is still present

This line is very bad:

“Evelyn Harcourt pressed on to the next concrete detail: Poison concealed hollow cubes releasing toxin.”

And then:

“That detail shifted the reasoning.”
“the trail bent toward Mechanism delayed poisoning hollow cubes.”

This is internal reasoning text, not fiction. It should be deleted completely.

2. Pronouns still need a hard pass

Examples:

Lord Charles’s hand is described as “her right hand.”
James is sometimes “she/her.”
Edward is sometimes “she/her.”
Evelyn occasionally refers to James with the wrong pronoun.

This is the biggest reason the score is not closer to 80.

3. The victim’s cause of death should be planted earlier

Chapter 1 does not strongly signal poisoning. It gives the odd sweat bead and the temperature clue, which helps, but the body examination should include one or two more fair-play indicators:

faint numbness/tingling complaint from earlier
bitter taste in glass
unusual constriction or stiffness
no wound despite sudden collapse
water glass near the sundial

Right now the ice/aconite method becomes strong later, but it would feel even fairer with a small early plant.

4. The final test partly works, then leaks again

The final test is conceptually strong: Evelyn recreates the sundial delay at 80°F and shows the shadow lags by ten minutes. Then she rechecks the ice tray and ties the poison to James. That is a real discriminating test.

But after the good test, the prose slips back into explicit “two competing readings” validation language. Trim that part and keep the scene dramatic.

5. The clearances are still too report-like

The final chapter clears Beatrice, Agnes, Edward, and even Evelyn in a fairly mechanical style:

“Therefore, you are innocent.”
“Your alibi is confirmed.”
“As for myself…”

That is useful for validation, but it reads like a checklist. The same information should be dramatized.

Better:

Beatrice had never left the kitchen. Agnes’s footsteps were echoes, not movements. Edward was seen in town. Only James had touched the ice, knew the sundial, and needed the ledger to lie.

Why this is stronger than the previous sundial draft

The earlier sundial version had a fresher setting but a confused time contradiction: 3:30 vs 4:20 vs staged watch vs actual murder time.

This version has a cleaner central idea:

The sundial appears to mark noon, but heat makes the shadow lag by ten minutes.
The actual crime window is before 11:30.
James uses hollow ice poison and the delayed sundial reading to distort the timeline.

It still needs physics cleanup, but the story logic is easier to follow.

Fastest fixes
Replace “brass expansion alone” with “loosened/tilted gnomon affected by heat.”
Delete all prompt-leakage reasoning lines.
Fix all pronouns.
Plant one early poison clue in Chapter 1.
Make the final clearances less report-like.
Explain aconite’s delayed effect in one clean sentence.
Trim repeated “emotional cost” and “truth remained elusive” phrasing.
Overall

This is a good draft and one of the best recent ones.

As written: 74/100.

With pronouns fixed, leakage removed, and the sundial physics made more believable, this could reach 80–83/100.
