This is better than the unfinished Hugo draft and roughly on par with the stronger clock-chime versions, but it has one big issue: the final chapter over-explains the solution after Chapter 9 already solved it. The mystery itself is mostly complete and coherent.

The roles are clear:

Investigator: Eleanor Voss
Victim: Dr. Mallory Finch
Culprit: Captain Ivor Hale
False suspects: Beatrice Quill, Sylvia Trent, Hugo Vane
Murder method: strangulation
Cover-up mechanism: Library clock tampered with to show 11:10, hiding true death time of 9:15
Key clues: snapped watch chain, note marked 9:15, Library clock at 11:10, scratched rim, ajar glass, oil residue, disturbed gears, muffled chime, Hale’s access as steward
Motive: Dr. Finch threatened to expose charity-account mismanagement

I’d score it around 80/100.

What works

The core mystery engine is much cleaner than the previous incomplete version:

the Library clock reads 11:10
a note says 9:15
the watch chain is snapped and shows another hour
the clock glass is ajar
the rim is scratched
gears are disturbed
fresh oil suggests recent handling
the chime is muffled/odd
Hale has steward access and clock knowledge
the clock was set later to create a false time of death

That is a real clue chain. The story gives physical evidence, testimonial evidence, and motive evidence.

The strongest part is the note marked “a quarter past nine”. It gives the reader an honest counterpoint to the false clock, which makes the final solution easier to understand.

Biggest improvement

This draft actually completes the reveal.

Unlike the previous version, this one names the culprit, explains the clock deception, gives Hale a motive, and includes a confession. That alone raises it significantly.

The core solution is readable:

Dr. Finch died at 9:15.
Hale tampered with the Library clock to show 11:10.
The false clock time made the murder seem later.
Hale used his access as steward to manipulate the clock and move through the hotel.
He killed Finch after an argument over the charity accounts.

That is coherent.

Main remaining issue

Chapter 10 repeats Chapter 9.

Chapter 9 already gives:

suspect clearances
note vs clock contradiction
Hale’s access
Hale’s motive
strangulation
clock tampering
confession
emotional aftermath

Then Chapter 10 restates nearly all of that in a more procedural style.

Chapter 10 should be shortened into pure aftermath:

Hale is taken away.
Beatrice, Sylvia, and Hugo react.
Eleanor reflects on the hotel, the charity, and the cost of truth.
The clock remains as a symbol.

No more formal alibi-clearing.

Main problems
1. The false solution is odd

Chapter 6 appears to accuse Captain Hale, but Captain Hale is ultimately guilty. That means it is not really a false solution.

To fix this, frame it as:

Eleanor suspects Hale early, but for the wrong reason.
She lacks proof.
The missing piece is the note / clock mechanism / access window.
Chapter 9 proves the right culprit through the correct chain.

Or make Chapter 6 point more strongly at Beatrice or Sylvia instead.

2. The murder method needs earlier support

The final reveal says Hale strangled Dr. Finch. But Chapter 1 emphasizes:

broken pearls
snapped watch chain
body angle
wrist bruise
clock tampering

Those can support strangulation, but the draft should make it clearer:

bruising around Finch’s throat
broken pearls caught under her collar
pressure marks beneath the jaw
Hale’s sleeve snagged on the necklace during the struggle

That would make strangulation feel planted.

3. Hale’s physical link could be stronger

Hale has access and motive, but one hard physical clue would make the reveal stronger:

oil from the clock mechanism on Hale’s cuff
a pearl from Finch’s necklace in Hale’s pocket
clock-case dust on his gloves
his watch chain matches a scratch on the clock rim
a staff member saw him leave The Library near 9:20

This would push the mystery closer to the high 80s.

4. The chime mechanics are still a little vague

The story says the chime sounded oddly/muffled and was manipulated, but the exact clock trick should be stated more cleanly:

Hale killed Dr. Finch at 9:15.
He then forced the Library clock forward to 11:10 and disturbed the chime mechanism so it would sound later.
The clock’s display created a false death window, while the note and watch chain preserved the true time.

Or:

The clock was not measuring time after the murder. It was staged as a false witness.
5. The “Eleanor alibi” section in Chapter 10 is unnecessary

Chapter 10 suddenly has Beatrice, Sylvia, and Hugo confirming Eleanor’s whereabouts. That feels like validation logic rather than story. Eleanor has not been a serious suspect, so clearing her at the end wastes space.

Cut it entirely unless the plot explicitly made Eleanor suspicious earlier.

6. Generated phrasing remains

These lines need cleanup:

“a faint smudge trailing the tarnished edge the glass case not quite seated”
“the chip etched into the gold edge the tumbler ajar”
“One must learn the rhythm mustn t you think?”
“a quarter past nine by the mantel clock” when the Library clock says 11:10

Not fatal, but they lower polish.

Fastest fixes
Make Chapter 10 aftermath-only.
Plant strangulation evidence in Chapter 1.
Add one physical clue tying Hale to the clock or necklace.
Clarify exactly how the chime was manipulated.
Reframe Chapter 6 as “right suspect, wrong proof,” not a false solution.
Remove the late Eleanor-alibi section.
Clean malformed generated phrases.
Keep the 9:15 note vs 11:10 clock as the central contradiction.
Overall

This is a good, complete clock-deception mystery. It has a clear culprit, clear victim, workable motive, and a much better clock contradiction than the incomplete Hugo version.

As written: 80/100.

With Chapter 10 tightened, Hale given one stronger physical link, and strangulation planted earlier, this could reach 85–88/100.
