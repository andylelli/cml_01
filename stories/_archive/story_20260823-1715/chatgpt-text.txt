This is a strong tide-mechanism draft, and it is better than the earlier tide versions because the tide clue is now tied to a physical test: the weighted pipe cannot float free until the tide reaches a certain point. That gives the mystery a concrete proof scene instead of just “the tide table says so.”

The roles are clear:

Investigator: Eleanor Voss
Victim: Dr. Mallory Finch
Culprit: Captain Ivor Hale
False suspects: Beatrice Quill, Sylvia Trent, Hugo Vane
Murder method: struck with a weighted metal pipe
Cover-up mechanism: pipe wedged beneath a rock ledge so the tide would release it later, creating a false time marker
Key clues: tide table, bloodied pipe, scrape marks on rock ledge, pipe wetness gradient, hair on pipe, tide test, Hale’s fingerprints, Hale’s alibi gap
Motive: Finch threatened to expose Hale’s past record/name

I’d score it around 81/100. The opening gives a clear corpse, tide table, violent wound, bloodied pipe, and rock-ledge scrape marks, so the physical mystery is planted early.

What works

The pipe-and-tide mechanism is the best part. The draft now gives the tide an actual job:

the pipe was used as the weapon
the pipe was wedged beneath the rock ledge
the rising tide released it later
that release created a false timing assumption
Eleanor recreates the event with a controlled test
the test shows the murder happened before the pipe floated free

That is a proper fair-play mechanism.

The controlled test is also strong. Eleanor wedges the pipe under the ledge, waits for the tide to rise, and watches the evidence refuse to match the neat alibi story. That is much better than a reveal based only on witness statements.

The final accusation has a good evidence chain:

Hale’s alibi only covers him until eleven.
The murder occurred before the pipe floated free at quarter past eleven.
The pipe has Hale’s fingerprints.
Hale had motive because Finch threatened to expose him.
Hale admits he struck Finch and tried to let the sea take the blame.

That is clear and satisfying.

Main remaining issues
1. The time logic needs one crisp version

The draft uses:

tide table: 10 minutes past eleven
pipe floats free: quarter past eleven
Hale’s alibi: until eleven
murder: before pipe floated free

This mostly works, but the final logic should be stated more cleanly:

Hale killed Finch before 11:00.
He wedged the pipe beneath the ledge.
At 11:15, the tide released the pipe, making it appear the pipe had only just arrived with the water.
The test proves the pipe’s release was delayed, not natural.

Right now, the reader can follow it, but has to work a little too hard.

2. Chapter 1 has generated time glitches

This line hurts polish:

“By then it was a ten minutes past eleven. It had taken fifteen minutes in all. By then it was a ten minutes past eleven.”

That should be removed or rewritten. It sounds like raw timing-note leakage.

3. The phrase “certified wave crest hour” is awkward

This line feels generated:

“The certified wave crest hour atop the murder day…”

Use natural prose:

“The harbormaster’s certified high-tide time was clear.”

4. The murder weapon is strong, but Hale’s link should be planted earlier

The reveal says Hale’s fingerprints are on the weighted pipe. That is useful, but it would feel even fairer if earlier chapters planted:

Hale handled boating equipment
Hale knew the rock ledge
Hale had access to the pipe
Hale had reason to know the tide chart
Hale wore gloves but one finger smear remained

5. Chapter 10 is mostly aftermath, but the first line is a bit report-like

The aftermath begins with “the subdued atmosphere… was palpable,” which feels slightly generic. But the evidence bag with the pipe is a good image, and the chapter is doing the right job: emotional fallout after the reveal.

Fastest fixes
State the true timeline in one clean paragraph.
Remove repeated/generated time lines in Chapter 1.
Replace “certified wave crest hour” with natural wording.
Plant Hale’s access to the pipe/ledge/tide table earlier.
Keep Chapter 10 as aftermath-only.
Add one small physical drowning/beach detail if Finch was found at the tide line, but keep the pipe blow as the true cause.
Overall

This is one of the better non-clock mechanisms. The tide is not just scenery; it becomes the false witness. That is exactly what you want.

As written: 81/100.

With the timing phrased cleanly and the generated lines removed, this could reach 86–89/100.
