This is one of the cleaner mystery-shape drafts, and it is stronger than the previous gate-latch story because the central deception is easier to understand:

Investigator: Eleanor Voss
Victim: Dr. Mallory Finch
Culprit: Captain Ivor Hale
False suspects: Hugo Vane, Beatrice Quill, Sylvia Trent
Murder method: stabbing with hidden dagger
Cover-up mechanism: Hale impersonates Finch using one of the three identical masquerade costumes
Core clue: torn costume fabric found away from the body
Fixed time: pocket watch stopped at 10:10 / ten minutes past eleven depending on wording, but the text mostly intends ten minutes past eleven

I’d score it around 76/100.

What works

This draft has a strong mystery engine. The masquerade setup naturally explains why witnesses could misidentify the killer. The three identical costumes are not just decoration — they become the central mechanism of deception.

The final solution is also much cleaner than many earlier drafts:

Hale kills Finch with a hidden dagger.
He puts on a matching costume.
He impersonates Finch after she is already dead.
Witnesses think Finch is still alive.
The torn fabric proves the impersonation.
The stopped watch fixes the real time of death.

That is a proper fair-play structure.

The false solution against Hugo is useful too. It gives the story a middle turn rather than moving directly from Hale suspicion to Hale confession.

Biggest problems
1. The time wording is inconsistent

The watch is repeatedly described as showing:

ten minutes past eleven

But your summary language or some implied setup can make it feel like 10:10 rather than 11:10. The story should lock this as:

11:10 p.m.

Use one exact format throughout.

2. Chapter 3 opens awkwardly

Chapter 3 begins by immediately announcing the dagger and blood before the scene has properly unfolded:

“Here is what we have…”

Then it seems to restart the chapter and rediscover the dagger. That feels like a duplicated generation pass.

Cut the first few paragraphs and begin with Eleanor moving through the ballroom and finding the fabric/dagger naturally.

3. Chapter 9 and Chapter 10 repeat the reveal

Chapter 9 already does the full reveal and confession. Chapter 10 repeats:

Hale killed Finch.
Hale wore the second costume.
Hale created the false timeline.
Beatrice, Hugo, Sylvia, and Eleanor are cleared.

That makes Chapter 10 feel unnecessary.

Best structure:

Chapter 9: final trap, accusation, confession.
Chapter 10: aftermath only — arrest, emotional fallout, dawn after the storm.

4. The clearances are still too report-like

Lines like:

“Therefore, Beatrice Quill is cleared.”
“Hugo Vane is cleared.”
“Sylvia Trent is cleared.”
“And myself — Eleanor Voss…”

are clear, but they read like validation output.

Better:

Beatrice had been under too many eyes near the orchestra. Hugo’s telegram receipt fixed him in the lobby. Sylvia’s set-change alibi held. Eleanor herself had never been out of sight long enough to play Finch’s ghost. Only Hale had both the height and the soldier’s control to carry the masquerade through.

5. Pronouns still slip

Examples include:

Hale described with “she/her.”
Hugo described with “she/her.”
Eleanor sometimes referred to as “he/his.”
Captain Hale’s name sometimes followed by feminine pronouns.

This is the main polish issue now. Structurally, the story works; the pronouns are what make it feel machine-generated.

6. The title is good, but the theme could be sharper

Masquerade of Authority is a good title because Hale’s false identity and military authority both matter. But the story could do more with that idea:

Hale hides behind uniform, duty, and reputation.
The masquerade lets him hide physically.
His “authority” becomes another costume.

That would make the title feel earned.

Fastest fixes
Standardize the death time as 11:10 p.m.
Remove the duplicated opening of Chapter 3.
Merge Chapter 10 into aftermath only.
Rewrite clearances as dramatic deduction rather than formal report.
Fix pronouns throughout.
Plant the tailor/fabric distinction earlier, before the final reveal.
Make Hale’s “authority as costume” theme more explicit.
Overall

This is a solid 76/100 draft.

It has one of the best core mystery devices so far: impersonation after death using identical masquerade costumes.
