What improved

The character pressure is stronger. Beatrice now has an actual emotional/moral conflict: Ashcroft promised her a future, threatened her, and planned hotel expansion that would damage the coastal ecosystem. That gives her more than a generic “secret” motive.

The clue chain also makes more sense:

tidal clock shows the main clock is false → schedule is altered → service elevator footprints contradict Beatrice’s shift claim → Beatrice had clock access → Beatrice had motive → Beatrice confesses.

That is a working mystery progression.

Biggest remaining issues
1. Chapter 1 still explains too much too early

This line gives away the mechanism too directly:

“The culprit wound back the main mechanical clock by forty minutes…”

That should not appear in Chapter 1. Chapter 1 should only show the contradiction:

The main clock showed 9:15.
The tidal clock indicated 9:30.
Eleanor realised one of them was lying.

Save “wound back forty minutes” for the later test or reveal.

2. The victim is named, but awkwardly

The story says:

“The body was that of a gentleman Ashcroft…”

That should be cleaner:

“The body was Ashcroft, the hotel’s owner…”

Even better:

“Julian Ashcroft, the hotel’s owner, lay at the foot of the grand staircase…”

A full name would make him feel more like a person and less like a function.

3. The body appears too late

Chapter 1 spends several paragraphs on clocks before showing Ashcroft’s body. For mystery impact, the order should be:

hotel atmosphere → body → investigator reaction → suspects → first clock contradiction

Right now it is:

hotel atmosphere → clock → tidal clock → explanation of tampering → suspects → body

That makes the opening feel more like clue analysis than discovery.

4. The murder method is still not clear enough

Beatrice says:

“I confronted him in Guest Room 207… When he refused, I acted in desperation.”

That explains motive and concealment, but not the death itself. Did she strike him, poison him, push him, suffocate him?

Add one clear line:

“I struck him with the brass doorstop when he turned away. I never meant the blow to kill him.”

or:

“I pushed him during the struggle, and he hit his head against the bedframe.”

Without that, she confesses to the situation and cover-up more than the actual murder.

5. The setting/character percentages are closer, but still not perfect

This draft is roughly:

Setting: 20%
Character pressure: 30–35%
Mystery/clue movement: 45–50%

Target for this kind of story should be closer to:

Setting: 10–15%
Character pressure: 35–40%
Mystery/clue movement: 45–50%

So the clue movement is about right. The main adjustment is still:

reduce repeated atmosphere by 5–10%, and give that space to character pressure or sharper clue action.

6. The final clearance section is too report-like

The ending has lines like:

“the doctor Finch was cleared because…”
“the captain Hale was cleared because…”
“As for myself, Eleanor Voss was present…”

That reads like validation output rather than fiction. It should be dramatized:

“Dr. Finch never left the guest corridor,” Eleanor said. “Three guests saw her there, and the corridor log agrees.”
Finch closed her eyes in relief.

Same information, more story-like.
