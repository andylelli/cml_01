Eleanor's voice, when it came, was soft but carried the authority of someone who had walked the labyrinth of doubt and emerged with certainty. 'We have come to the end,' she said, her words measured, 'and the truth will not be denied any longer. ' She began with the evidence, methodical as a surgeon. 'The drink served at eight o'clock in the evening was the fulcrum upon which this crime turned. The bitterness in Sylvia Trent's glass was no accident. It was the first sign that her death was not the work of fate, but of design. ' Eleanor lifted the glass, the lamplight catching the faint, oily slick that still clung to its rim. 'The poison was slow-acting, its effects delayed. The exact moment the toxin was administered coincides with the victim's symptoms: seven hours after consumption.

'Dr. Mallory Finch, your alibi holds. You were summoned to tend to a patient on the second floor, your absence corroborated by both the hospital records and the testimony of the patient and her companion. Because you were not present during the critical window, you could not have tampered with the drinks, which proves your innocence. 'Captain Ivor Hale, your whereabouts during the toast are confirmed by multiple witnesses. You remained in the lounge, never entering the kitchen or approaching the drinks before the toast.

Because your absence is corroborated by others, you could not have poisoned the glass, which proves your innocence. ' Beatrice's hands fell still, and she let out a shaky breath, her composure faltering as the truth settled in. Eleanor paused, her gaze steady and unflinching as she faced Hugo Vane. 'That leaves you, Mr. Vane. You were seen near the drinks tray before the toast, your movements oddly deliberate. Witnesses recall you adjusting the glasses, your hands lingering just a moment too long.

Only someone with access to the drinks before the toast could have introduced the poison. ' Hugo Vane's face was ashen, his fingers drumming a frantic rhythm on the table's edge. 'You tampered with the glass meant for Sylvia Trent, trusting that the delayed action of the poison would shield you from suspicion. The bitterness in the glass, the timing of the symptoms, the hidden bottle-all point to you. ' A hush fell, the only sound the wind howling outside and the distant creak of the hotel's bones.

Hugo Vane did not resist as he was led away, his steps heavy, the fight gone from him. The others watched in silence, the reality of consequence settling in with the finality of a closing door. In the aftermath, the dining room was a tableau of grief and relief, the survivors left to reckon with what remained. Captain Ivor Hale slumped in his chair, his composure shattered, while Dr. Mallory Finch pressed a trembling hand to her brow, her eyes shining with unshed tears. Beatrice Quill stared at the tablecloth, her ambition rendered hollow by the night's events. The cost of truth was written on every face. 'Two witnesses placed me elsewhere until well after the toast,' she said quietly, her eyes meeting those of Dr. Mallory Finch and Captain Ivor Hale, who both gave small, solemn nods. 'You saw me in the library with you, Dr. Finch, and you, Captain Hale, confirmed I did not leave your company until the drinks had already been served. I could not have been responsible.
