## Chapter 7: Captain Hale's Defensive Moves

Eleanor Voss pressed her palm to the cold brass of the clock casing, the late night air thick with the scent of metal and the faint, lingering polish. The truth, she realized, would not restore anything-only strip the last illusions from the night. He crouched, the lamplight glancing off the silver at his temples, and began to loosen the screws at the base of the clock. The air was thick with expectation, the only sound the scrape of metal on metal and the soft, erratic breathing of a man who knew he was being watched. " She kept her tone measured, letting the question hang between them. Captain Hale did not look up. "The staff are unreliable," he replied, his words clipped. "Best to see to these things oneself. The lamplight caught on the faint scuff near the clock's suspension spring-a mark Eleanor had noted before, but now, under Captain Hale's scrutiny, it seemed to pulse with new significance. She stepped closer, her shoes making no sound on the rug, and saw that his hands trembled ever so slightly as he attempted to remove a length of copper wire, its end looped delicately around the pendulum. The wire came away with a faint metallic snap, and Captain Hale tucked it swiftly into his pocket, as if to erase its existence. The gesture was practiced, but the desperation in it was unmistakable. If you're implying something, Miss Voss, I suggest you say it plainly. Eleanor let the silence stretch, her gaze fixed on the spot where the copper wire had been. The contradiction was now undeniable: the assumption that the pendulum had simply stopped was overturned by the evidence of deliberate, manual interference.

She straightened, her posture composed but her mind racing, and watched as Captain Hale replaced the clock's casing, his movements growing more hurried, less precise. Captain Hale's shoulders stiffened, and for a moment he looked not like a man of command, but like someone desperately trying to hold together a story that had begun to unravel. The clock's face, still fixed at a quarter to three, seemed to mock the very notion of certainty. For a brief, almost absurd moment, Eleanor considered the irony: here, in a hotel obsessed with order and routine, it was the very act of tidying away evidence that had exposed the truth. She allowed herself a wry, inward laugh before the tension returned, sharper than before. In the hush that followed, Eleanor lingered by the clock, her fingers tracing the outline of the scuff mark, the echo of Captain Hale's actions still vibrating in the air.

The contradiction between the physical evidence and the assumed mechanism was now impossible to ignore. The previous theory, so carefully constructed, had been overturned-not by confession, but by the desperate, silent choreography of a man trying to erase his own trace. The investigation had reached its pivot: suspicion, once diffuse, now narrowed and sharpened, waiting for the final reckoning that the night would not delay much longer.

## Chapter 8: The Discriminating Test

"We begin now," Eleanor Voss declared, her voice carrying in the cold hush of early morning.
