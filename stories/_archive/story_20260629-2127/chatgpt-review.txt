What works

The setting-mechanism match is the best thing here. A sundial clue belongs naturally in a manor garden, and the stopped watch gives the clue a physical object to anchor around. That is more distinctive than another “clock was tampered with” version.

The plot shape is also strong. The story moves through:

body and sundial clue
conflicting household timeline
ledger inconsistencies
Edward’s financial motive
access logs
sundial experiment
clearances
Edward’s confession

That is a proper mystery progression.

The ending is more complete than many earlier ones. Edward confesses to stabbing Edith, staging the watch beneath the sundial, instructing the staff to repeat a false timeline, and acting because Edith discovered his financial fraud. That gives method, motive, opportunity, cover-up, and consequence.

Biggest problem

The sundial time logic is confused.

The story gives multiple incompatible times:

watch shows 4:20
sundial shadow suggests 3:30
actual murder time was 4:20
servants claim Edith was alive near sunset
Edward staged the watch to show 3:30
Chapter 1 also says the watch shows 4:20, then later says the shadow/watch indicate 3:30

That makes the central clue hard to trust.

For this story to work, the time system must be brutally simple.

Use one clean version:

True murder time: 4:20 p.m.
Staged time: 3:30 p.m.
How staged: Edward set Edith’s stopped watch to 3:30 and placed it under the sundial so the shadow would appear to confirm that false time.
Why it matters: 3:30 gives Edward an alibi or shifts suspicion to the household staff.
What exposes it: the shadow length and angle at discovery could not have occurred at 3:30 under that day’s light/weather records; the real shadow geometry points to 4:20.

Or reverse it, but do not use both.

Main issues
1. The opening contradicts itself

Chapter 1 first says:

“The hour stood at twenty minutes past four in the afternoon.”

Then later:

“The time indicated by the sundial’s shadow on the watch face… was unmistakable: half past three in the afternoon.”

Both cannot be the first clear clue unless the distinction is explicitly explained.

Better:

“The stopped watch read half past three, but the shadow crossing it could only have fallen that way at twenty minutes past four.”

That gives a clean contradiction.

2. The weather/log phrases are broken

Several lines look like generated evidence fragments that were pasted into prose:

“The estate’s barometer needle settling on drizzle'the groundskeeper's entry noting silhouettes…”
“the rainfall tally noted by the manor groundskeeper'Eliza's entry…”
“drizzle noted in the manor's official diary'security's entry…”

These are severe polish problems. They should become normal prose:

“The groundskeeper’s weather log recorded clear light at 4:20, with shadows sharply visible across the west lawn.”

3. The discriminating test is still semi-abstract

The sundial experiment is a good idea, but it needs to be physically clearer.

Make Arthur do this:

place identical watch on the plinth
mark the shadow at 3:30
wait or calculate the 4:20 angle
compare it with a photograph/sketch/groundkeeper note from the discovery
show the household that only the 4:20 angle matches the mark on Edith’s watch

Right now the test says the evidence proves itself, but the geometry remains vague.

4. The clearances are too report-like

Chapter 9 and 10 still use lines like:

“Charles Whitmore was cleared because…”
“Beatrice Hargrave is cleared because…”
“As for myself—Arthur…”

This sounds like validation output. Arthur does not need to clear himself unless he was genuinely suspected.

Rewrite as deduction:

“Beatrice had been in the kitchen between half past three and the supper bell; three servants had seen her there. Charles was away at the council meeting. Daphne never left the east wing. The logs did not scatter suspicion — they concentrated it.”

5. Pronouns still slip

Daphne is described with masculine pronouns in places, and Arthur/Edward pronouns also drift. This is less destructive than role collapse, but still lowers polish.

Fastest fixes
Fix the time logic into one clean contradiction.
Rewrite Chapter 1’s clue sentence so the watch and shadow do not contradict accidentally.
Convert broken weather/log fragments into clean evidence.
Make the sundial experiment more physical and visual.
Remove report-style clearances.
Fix pronouns throughout.
Explain exactly how the false 3:30 time protected Edward.
Overall

This has one of the best premises so far because the sundial mechanism feels fresh and tied to the setting.

But the central clue currently contradicts itself, which is a major issue for a fair-play mystery.

As written: 70/100.

With the time logic fixed and the experiment clarified, this could reach 78–82/100.
