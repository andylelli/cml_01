"I'd like to think I'd have found another way," he answered, not quite believing it himself. "But the truth is, we're all shaped by the rooms we inhabit-and by the shadows we cast." He let the silence stretch, the only sound the soft patter of rain and the distant echo of a horse-drawn cart in the lane. Dorothy Underhill gathered her things, her movements brisk but not unkind. "We'll manage, Clarissa. We always do. The brewery will go on, and so will we. But nothing will be quite the same." She paused at the door, offering Gerald a nod that carried both gratitude and resignation. "Thank you, Doctor. For seeing us through-whatever that means now."

Left alone in the dim office, Gerald stared at the silhouette cutout, its shape both absurd and tragic. The demonstration had exposed more than a mechanism; it had laid bare the fragile bonds of loyalty, ambition, and desperation that held Ingram Brewery Village together. As the fire sank low and the summery night pressed close, Gerald allowed himself a single, rueful smile. Justice, he thought, was never a simple matter. It always left a mark.
