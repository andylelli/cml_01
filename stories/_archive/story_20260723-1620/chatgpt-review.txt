The roles are stable:

Investigator: Eleanor Voss
Victim: Sylvia Trent
Culprit: Captain Ivor Hale
False suspect: Beatrice Quill
Other suspects: Dr. Mallory Finch, Hugo Vane
Murder method: strangulation
Cover-up mechanism: identical masquerade costume/mask used to impersonate Sylvia
False alive sighting: witnesses think they see Sylvia at 11:10
Key clues: two identical costumes, two masks in Hale’s room, measurements, Hale’s access, loud music masking the crime
Motive: Sylvia threatened to expose Hale’s debts / war secrets / public disgrace

What works

The central mechanism is strong because it fits the setting naturally:

masquerade party
masks and costumes everywhere
witnesses relying on costume instead of face
two identical Sylvia costumes
music loud enough to cover movement or confusion
Hale using the second costume to make Sylvia appear alive later

That is much more organic than some previous mechanisms. The disguise does not feel bolted on; it belongs to the masquerade premise.

The best clue chain is:

Sylvia is dead.
Witnesses claim they saw Sylvia alive at 11:10.
The “Sylvia” they saw was really someone in an identical costume.
Two masks/costumes are found connected to Captain Hale.
The measurements prove the duplication was planned.
Hale had access, motive, and the solitary timeline gap.
Therefore Hale used the masquerade to create a false alive sighting.

That is a real mystery structure.

Biggest improvement

The false solution works better here than in several earlier drafts.

Eleanor first considers Beatrice because:

Beatrice has financial trouble.
Beatrice needs Sylvia’s help.
Beatrice could plausibly use the masquerade chaos.
Beatrice’s dramatic personality makes her easy to suspect.

Then the solution collapses because Beatrice’s lounge alibi with Hugo and staff support is stronger than expected.

That gives the story a proper detective reversal.

Main remaining issues
1. The measurements clue is still a little artificial

The “precise measurements” are useful, but they feel slightly too convenient unless you explain why Hale had them.

Better versions:

Hale commissioned the duplicate costume.
Hale kept tailor’s notes in his room.
The measurements match Sylvia’s costume and Hale’s body adjustments.
A hotel seamstress confirms Hale requested emergency alterations.

Right now, the measurements prove planning, but the story should explain why they exist.

2. The murder method needs more physical detail

The reveal says Sylvia was strangled, but the opening focuses more on costume, mask, and tableau. Add one early clue:

bruising beneath the powder along Sylvia’s jaw
crushed sequins at the neckline
torn ribbon from the mask used as ligature
fabric fibers caught under Sylvia’s fingernails
a dark mark hidden beneath the collar

This would make the strangulation feel planted rather than announced later.

3. Hale’s possession of both masks is almost too obvious

Finding both masks in Hale’s room in Chapter 2 points very strongly at him. That is fine if the story later casts doubt on whether they were planted, but otherwise it makes the solution easier.

To improve the mystery, make it ambiguous:

Hale claims the masks were stored there for the event committee.
Other guests had access to his room.
The staff delivered costume props there by mistake.
Only later does the measurement slip prove Hale personally arranged them.

That would preserve suspicion without making Hale too obvious too soon.

4. Chapter 10 still has validation-style prose

The visible start of Chapter 10 includes phrasing like:

“The evidence was undeniable: Beatrice Quill’s alibi was confirmed…”

That is much better than the old “could not have been Captain Hale” leakage, but it still sounds like a report rather than fiction.

Rewrite as aftermath:

Beatrice sat apart, clutching her empty glass. No one looked at her now with suspicion, but Eleanor saw how the accusation had marked her all the same.

That says the same thing dramatically.

5. Pronoun drift is reduced but still present

There are still moments like:

Captain Hale described with “her eyes” or “watched her”
Eleanor’s focus slipping into “he” patterns
mixed referents when multiple suspects are in the same paragraph

It is less severe than the previous clock draft, but it still needs a pronoun pass.

6. The clock time is underused

The clock fixed at 11:10 appears early and links to the false sighting, but the final proof mostly rests on costumes and masks. Use the time more sharply:

Sylvia was already dead before 11:10.
The person seen at 11:10 was the impersonator.
The loud music at 11:10 gave Hale the exact moment to perform the deception.

That would make the clock/music/costume elements work together.

Fastest fixes
Add one physical strangulation clue in Chapter 1.
Explain why Hale had the measurements.
Make Hale’s possession of the masks plausibly deniable early, then decisive later.
Tie the 11:10 clock/music moment more directly to the false alive sighting.
Rewrite Chapter 10 as emotional aftermath, not alibi-summary prose.
Run a final pronoun consistency pass.
Cut repeated catchphrases by about half.
Overall

This is a strong draft. The masquerade device finally feels central, fair, and thematically right.

As written: 80/100.

With the strangulation evidence planted earlier, the measurement clue made more natural, and Chapter 10 cleaned up, this could reach 86–89/100.
