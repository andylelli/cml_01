A brief lull settled over the room, the tension momentarily broken by the distant sound of a radio in the Grand Lobby. The melody was faint, a bittersweet counterpoint to the charged silence. Beatrice allowed herself a small, ironic smile, the moment of levity a welcome reprieve from the mounting suspicion. Yet the relief was fleeting; the evidence demanded attention. The investigation now teetered on the edge of a new understanding. The clock's unmoving hands, the faint scratch upon its face, and the irreconcilable accounts of the night's events pointed to a deliberate effort to obscure the truth. Beatrice's thoughts churned. If witnesses placed Eleanor present and breathing at precisely twenty minutes past ten, and the clock insisted on ten minutes past eleven, then someone had engineered this confusion. A sudden movement drew Beatrice's eye-Eleanor, pressing a hand to her chest, her breath quickening. The socialite's composure was crumbling, the duality of her desires laid bare. Her longing for acceptance warred with the fear of scandal, and in that moment, Beatrice glimpsed the cost of the crime: not just a life lost, but a web of loyalties stretched to breaking. Captain Hale's bravado faltered. He leaned against the doorframe, fingers drumming a restless rhythm. "Look, if you're going to accuse someone, do it outright.
