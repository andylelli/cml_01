This is a good visual mystery idea, but it is weaker than the brewery shadow/cutout draft because the physical mechanism is less believable and the ending slips back into explanation. The alley mirror/recess concept is promising, but it needs clearer geometry and a stronger reason why the illusion helps the murder.

I’d score it around 80/100.

What works

The opening has a strong detective image: Edmund Jessop dead in the alley, a clean puncture wound, a bloodied letter opener, and a strange seam in the theatre facade. The story also quickly establishes that witnesses believe the alley is a straight forty-foot passage, while Rosalind notices misaligned brickwork and an altered surface near the entrance.

The best clue is the recently installed angled mirror/panel. It is visual, theatrical, and thematically fitting: a theatre literally uses reflection and facade to deceive witnesses. The mirror being six feet from the alley entry, with a two-foot recess behind it, gives the puzzle physical specificity.

Adela is also a logical culprit. She controls backstage access, maps, accounts, maintenance, and facade modifications, and her signature appears on the relevant installation documents. That gives her means and institutional authority.

Main problem: the illusion does not yet prove the murder

The story keeps saying the mirror makes the alley look like a continuous forty feet, but it does not fully explain how that allowed Adela to kill Edmund.

Did the mirror:

hide Adela inside the two-foot recess?
make witnesses think Edmund was farther down the alley?
conceal the stage exit?
create a false empty-alley view?
shift the apparent location of the body?
distort time-of-death assumptions?

The draft gestures at all of these, but never locks one clean explanation. The reveal says Adela controlled the panels, mirror, recess, doors, and access, but the actual “aha” mechanism remains vague.

Use one clean version:

The mirror did not lengthen the alley for decoration. It hid the recess. From the stage exit, the reflection made the wall appear continuous and the alley seem empty. Adela waited in the two-foot recess, stabbed Edmund as he passed, then stepped back behind the angled panel. Witnesses looking from the theatre saw only the reflected side street and believed the whole forty-foot alley was clear.

That makes the mirror useful, not just suspicious.

The geometry needs to be easier to picture

Right now the numbers are there — forty feet, six feet, two feet — but the reader may still struggle to visualize the trick. A mirror illusion needs very plain spatial writing.

Add a simple explanation like:

Real alley: thirty-four feet.
Mirror begins: six feet from the entrance.
Hidden recess: two feet deep behind the panel.
Apparent alley: forty feet, because the mirror reflects the side street and disguises the bend.

Then in the reveal:

“The witnesses did not see the alley,” Rosalind said. “They saw a reflected extension of it.”

That would sharpen the whole mystery.

Character Life / Relationship Richness: 7/10

This is decent, but not as strong as the brewery story.

The cast has some life: Prudence has public humiliation in her past, Barnaby has a failed audition, Marguerite is blunt because of survival and wages, Henrietta speaks in brittle theatrical aphorisms, and Adela is shaped by near-bankruptcy and fear for the theatre.

The best human moment is Marguerite in Chapter 10 saying she wants her wages paid before the next scandal. That is exactly the sort of non-puzzle character detail that makes a world feel real.

But Adela’s motive is still too broad: “protect the theatre” is understandable, but generic. Make it specific:

Edmund discovered Adela had mortgaged the theatre twice.
Edmund planned to expose forged patronage accounts.
Edmund was going to withdraw Barnaby from the production, ruining the theatre’s last chance at solvency.
Adela had spent years saving the theatre after bankruptcy and saw Edmund as the man who would destroy it overnight.

That would make the confession hit harder.

Chapter 9 reveal is too blunt

Chapter 9 does the final demonstration, but it becomes a list of evidence:

wound, blood, seam, facade, panel, mirror, recess, authority, access, opportunity.

Then it simply declares Adela revealed as culprit.

Better would be an actual discriminating test:

Rosalind places Barnaby at the stage exit and asks him whether the alley is empty.
He says yes.
Then Marguerite steps out from the two-foot recess behind the mirror.
The witnesses realise the mirror has hidden a living person in plain sight.
Only Adela knew the panel had been installed and could have used it at the right moment.

That gives the reader an “oh!” instead of a lecture.

Chapter 10 starts well, then reverts to proof mode

The first half of Chapter 10 is good aftermath: Barnaby subdued, Prudence unsettled, Marguerite practical about wages, Henrietta adjusting her mirror, Rosalind watching routines resume. That is the right direction.

But then it slides into alibi/timeline recap again:

Prudence accounted for
Barnaby confirmed by mill shift records
forensic report
manipulated time of death
scene staged to mislead investigators

That belongs in Chapter 8 or 9, not Chapter 10.

End on aftermath instead. The strongest possible ending is around:

The theatre would survive, but the scars would endure.

Then stop.

Scaffold/prose issues to remove

These phrases still feel generated:

“The usher’s testimony shifted the reasoning.”
“The chain of evidence drew taut.”
“The investigation had begun, and the questions multiplied.”
“Relief flickered…” repeated many times.
“The chain of elimination began to take shape.”
“The forensic report indicated…” in Chapter 10.

The most obvious one is “The usher’s testimony shifted the reasoning,” which reads like internal prompt/validation language, not prose.

Overall

This has real potential because the setting and clue type match: theatre, facade, mirror, stage illusion, public appearance. But the current draft needs the mirror mechanism to be physically clearer and more decisive.

As written: 80/100.

With clearer geometry, a real live demonstration of the hidden recess, a sharper Adela motive, and Chapter 10 trimmed back to pure aftermath, this could reach 86–88/100.
