This is a good concept but a weaker execution than the previous “Masquerade of Authority” draft. The central mask trick is interesting, but this draft gets less clean near the end because it has two final chapters doing almost the same job, and Chapter 9 retreats into report-like alibi clearing instead of delivering the final emotional confrontation.

The roles are mostly stable:

Investigator: Hugo Vane
Victim: Eleanor Voss
Culprit: Captain Ivor Hale
False suspects: Dr. Mallory Finch, Beatrice Quill, Sylvia Trent
Murder method: strangulation
Cover-up mechanism: duplicate green-embroidered mask used to impersonate Eleanor after death
Key clues: torn mask at the left eye, green thread, second matching mask, mismatched clocks, bellhop’s ledger, footprints, Hale’s corridor opportunity
Motive: Hale’s debts, control of the hotel/family affairs, fear Eleanor would expose or displace him

I’d score it around 76/100.

What works

The torn mask clue is good. It is visual, specific, and tied to the masquerade premise.

The strongest version of the logic is:

Eleanor wears a green-embroidered mask torn at the left eye.
Witnesses remember the torn mask, so they think they saw Eleanor alive later.
There are two identical masks.
Hale had access to both.
One mask is found near Eleanor’s body.
The other mask in Hale’s room is torn in the same way.
Hale used the second mask to impersonate Eleanor after killing her.
The green thread links the struggle and the disguise.

That is a fair-play mystery mechanism.

The role swap also mostly works: Hugo as investigator, Eleanor as victim. This is handled better than some earlier role-swapped drafts because Hugo remains the viewpoint character more consistently.

Biggest issue

The ending structure is inefficient.

Chapter 8 already solves the case. It gives:

the mask logic
the alibi clearances
Hale’s opportunity
the impersonation method
the strangulation
the confession
the constable taking Hale away

Then Chapter 9 starts another “culprit exposed” sequence, but instead of adding new emotional aftermath, it re-clears Beatrice, Sylvia, and Finch in formal language.

That makes Chapter 9 feel like a duplicate validation chapter rather than a proper final chapter.

The fix is simple:

Keep Chapter 8 as the reveal.
Delete or rewrite Chapter 9 as aftermath only.

Chapter 9 should show Hugo processing Eleanor’s death, Beatrice/Sylvia/Finch reacting, Hale being removed, and the hotel returning to uneasy silence.

Main issues
1. Chapter 9 has validation/report phrasing

Lines like this are too mechanical:

“Beatrice Quill’s alibi was confirmed because multiple witnesses saw her in the lounge at the time.”

And:

“Sylvia Trent’s alibi was confirmed because multiple witnesses saw her in the lounge at the time.”

And:

“Dr. Mallory Finch is ruled out by independent confirmation and the physical impossibility of her presence at the scene.”

These are logically useful, but they do not sound like fiction. They sound like a checklist.

Better:

Beatrice sat with her notebook clutched to her chest, no longer defending herself. The lounge register and half a dozen witnesses had done that for her.

2. The mask trick is good, but the “same tear” needs one clearer explanation

The story says both masks are torn at the left eye. That is compelling, but the deduction should be sharper:

Eleanor’s mask was torn during the struggle.
Hale later tore his own mask in the same place to make witnesses confuse the two.
The tears are similar but not identical under close inspection.
The green thread on Eleanor’s gown came from the real struggle, not the staged mask.

That would make the clue feel less magical and more forensic.

3. The clocks are introduced but not paid off strongly

Chapter 2 introduces two mismatched clocks:

one at 11:10
one at 10:50

But the final solution is mostly about masks. The clocks need to matter, or they should be cut.

Possible fix:

The false Eleanor sighting happened between the two clock times.
Hale used the clock confusion to widen his window.
The bellhop’s ledger fixes the true sequence and defeats the clock confusion.

Otherwise, the clocks are atmospheric clutter.

4. Footprints appear late and then fade

Chapter 7 introduces fresh footprints leading from the terrace/garden. That could be a strong physical clue, but it does not become central in the reveal.

Use them:

The larger prints match Hale’s boots.
They show someone left by the garden route after impersonating Eleanor.
They connect Hale’s room, the terrace, and the corridor.

Right now they are a promising clue that the ending does not fully cash in.

5. The false solution is odd

Chapter 6 frames suspicion around Hale, then says the solution is flawed because the masks make things ambiguous. But Hale is ultimately guilty anyway.

That can work as “right culprit, wrong explanation,” but the draft should state the distinction:

Chapter 6: Hugo suspects Hale because Hale possessed the masks and acted nervous.
Problem: two identical masks mean possession alone is not enough.
Chapter 8: Hugo proves Hale through the full chain: ledger + bellhop + matching tear + green thread + corridor timing + impersonation.

That would make the detective’s reasoning feel more sophisticated.

6. Strangulation needs an earlier physical clue

The reveal says Hale strangled Eleanor, but Chapter 1 focuses mostly on the mask tableau. Add a concrete clue early:

bruising under the jaw
torn ribbon around the throat
pressure marks hidden by the mask ribbon
green thread caught beneath Eleanor’s fingernails
broken clasp at the neckline

This would make the method feel planted, not just revealed.

7. Pronoun drift is much improved but not gone

This draft is less chaotic than the worst pronoun-drift versions. Hugo usually stays he/him, and Eleanor is consistently the victim. But there are still places where sentence subjects blur or generated phrases create awkward references.

The main issue here is less gendered pronoun drift and more referent drift: a sentence starts with one character and the next pronoun or phrase points vaguely to another.

Fastest fixes
Make Chapter 9 aftermath-only, or delete it.
Use the clocks in the final deduction, or remove them.
Pay off the footprints in the reveal.
Add one physical strangulation clue in Chapter 1.
Clarify why both masks have the same tear.
Reframe Chapter 6 as “right suspect, insufficient proof.”
Replace alibi-summary lines with dramatic aftermath prose.
Do one final referent/pronoun pass.
Overall

This has a strong mask-based mystery engine, but the ending needs consolidation.

As written: 76/100.

With Chapter 9 rewritten as aftermath and the clock/footprint clues properly paid off, this could reach 82–85/100.
