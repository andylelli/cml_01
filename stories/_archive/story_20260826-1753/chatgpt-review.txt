What works best

The right-foot / left-limp contradiction is the standout clue.

The story plants Hale’s left-leg injury clearly in Chapter 2, then contrasts it with the right-deep prints from the veranda. That is an elegant mystery clue because it looks incriminating at first — Hale’s slippers match — but becomes exculpatory once Eleanor thinks about gait mechanics.

The Chapter 8 test is also strong. Hale walks in the slippers and leaves a deeper left print; Beatrice walks in them and leaves a deeper right print matching the original trail. That is visual, fair, and easy for the reader to understand.

Main issue

The draft still has the same weakness: Beatrice’s role is muddy.

She says:

“I did as I was told.”
“I wore the coat, the slippers.”
“But I could not have struck the blow.”

That means she participated in the staged false trail. But the story treats her as simply cleared, almost like an innocent suspect.

You need one clean explanation:

Best version: Beatrice knowingly impersonated Hale to help Hugo create a harmless public deception, but did not know Hugo would use that staged appearance to cover a murder.

That lets her be guilty of helping the cover-up/masquerade, but innocent of the killing.

Remaining problems
1. Hugo needs one harder physical link

The evidence against Hugo is mostly:

master keys
staff access
unobserved office window
motive to protect the hotel
seaweed in service corridor
confession

That works, but the proof would be stronger with a direct link to the candlestick or murder room:

brass polish/blood trace on Hugo’s cuff
candlestick groove wiped but retaining residue from Hugo’s glove
master key with blood in a groove
Finch’s torn note naming Hugo
seaweed in Hugo’s office carpet

Right now the strongest physical proof belongs to the impersonation, not the murder.

2. The five-minute murder window is too tight

The reveal says Hugo used the service corridor at 11:05, while Beatrice created the false veranda sighting at 11:10, and five minutes was enough to slip away, kill Finch, and return.

That can work only if the layout is very compact. Add one clear geography line:

“The manager’s office opened directly onto the service stair beside Room 212; from there, Hugo could reach the Canary’s Suite in less than a minute.”

3. The reveal repeats after the confession

Chapter 9 already proves the footprint trick, clears Hale, clears Beatrice of the killing, points to Hugo, and gets Hugo’s confession. Then it keeps going with more test language and repeated demonstration.

Stop after Hugo confesses.

4. Chapter 10 still contains validation leakage

These lines need to go:

“Eleanor Voss pressed on to the following solid point the file now held…”
“That observation tilted the logic stacked against the rest…”
“Hugo Vane was responsible; the evidence allowed no other reading.”

They are not story prose; they are generator residue.

There is also leakage earlier around the footprint clues and candlestick clues, which keeps the polish score low.

Fastest fixes
Define Beatrice as duped helper, cover-up accomplice, or knowingly false witness.
Add one physical clue tying Hugo to the candlestick or Room 212.
Add one sentence explaining the short route from Hugo’s office to the murder room.
Cut the repeated test/proof after Hugo’s confession.
Delete all “record now held / evidence allowed no other reading” leakage.
Keep the footprint-gait test exactly as the centerpiece.
Overall

This is still a strong identity-misdirection draft. The footprint clue is better than many of the clock variants because it is visual, fair, and character-specific.

As written: 82/100.

With Beatrice’s role clarified, Hugo tied physically to the murder, and the validation leakage removed, this could reach 88–90/100.
