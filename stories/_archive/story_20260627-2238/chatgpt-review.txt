What improved

The victim problem is fixed. Hugo Vane is named in Chapter 1 and stays dead. Eleanor is now clearly the investigator, not the corpse. That alone makes the draft much more workable.

The central clue is also stronger than the previous version because it uses two time sources:

Hugo’s pocket watch: ten minutes past eleven
grand clock: quarter to eleven

That creates a clean contradiction.

The reveal also has the right major ingredients:

Finch tampered with the clock.
Finch had access to the winding key.
Finch feared exposure because of Hugo’s painting.
Hugo was strangled.
Finch confesses.

That is a complete mystery solution.

Biggest problems
1. Pronouns are still badly unstable

This is the largest remaining problem. Dr. Mallory Finch is introduced with he/his, then later becomes she/her in places. Captain Hale is also sometimes described with feminine pronouns.

Examples:

“Dr. Mallory Finch stood nearest the body, his posture…”
later, “Dr. Mallory Finch's composure faltered. She adjusted her cufflinks…”

This hurts character clarity. It does not destroy the story like the earlier dead-victim issue, but it stops the score going higher.

2. Chapter 3 introduces an 8:45 commotion that confuses the timeline

The story already has a strong time contradiction:

pocket watch 11:10
grand clock 10:45

Then Chapter 3 adds:

witnesses heard a commotion around 8:45

That muddies the case. If 8:45 matters, it needs a clear function. Otherwise, remove it.

Cleaner version:

Witnesses heard a commotion shortly after 11:00, but the grand clock said 10:45.

Keep the time puzzle simple.

3. The discriminating test is not strong enough

Chapter 7 is called “The Final Test,” but the test mostly consists of Eleanor confronting Finch with the key, clock, and proximity. It does not physically demonstrate enough.

A stronger test would be:

Eleanor inserts the winding key.
She shows the grand clock can be set backward without stopping the chime.
She compares the scratch pattern made by the key to the existing scratch.
She proves the clock was changed from 11:10 to 10:45 after Hugo died.

Right now it is more of an accusation than a true test.

4. Prompt leakage remains

This line is still bad:

“Eleanor Voss pressed on to the next concrete detail: Clock winding fireplace. That detail shifted the reasoning.”

That must be removed. It is internal reasoning/proof text, not story prose.

There are also repeated generated-style lines like:

“the emotional cost of the crime…”
“the uneasy truce between…”
“the pursuit of answers continued…”

These are not as bad as raw leakage, but they feel formulaic.

5. The final clearance section is too report-like

Chapter 9 clears everyone again, including Eleanor herself:

“I was cleared because my movements…”

This feels like validation output, not fiction. The investigator does not usually need to formally clear herself unless the story made her a real suspect.

Better:

Eleanor let the others’ relief settle. Hale had been seen too often to be guilty; Beatrice had never left the kitchen; Sylvia’s library witnesses held. The case no longer needed their fear.

Same information, but prose-like.

Fastest fixes
Lock pronouns for every character.
Remove or clarify the 8:45 commotion.
Make the final test physically demonstrate the clock manipulation.
Delete all prompt leakage.
Rewrite Chapter 9 as emotional aftermath, not clearance report.
Explain exactly why changing the grand clock to 10:45 protected Finch or implicated others.
Overall

This is a solid improvement over the previous draft. It fixes the victim-name issue and has a complete culprit/method/mechanism chain.

As written: 71/100.

With pronouns fixed, the 8:45 confusion removed, and a stronger physical clock test, this could reach 77–80/100.
