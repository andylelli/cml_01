This is a decent clock-friction draft, but it is weaker than both the weighted-pendulum Hugo draft and the Beatrice wire-hinge draft. The atmosphere is good and the clock clue has potential, but the solution has a serious structural problem: Chapter 8 clears everyone except Hale, then Chapter 9 says Hale already confessed, but the story never properly shows the final accusation/confession scene.

The roles seem to be:

Investigator: Eleanor Voss
Victim: Dr. Mallory Finch
Culprit: Captain Ivor Hale
False suspects: Beatrice Quill, Sylvia Trent, Hugo Vane
Murder method: blow to the head with a heavy bronze/brass candlestick
Cover-up mechanism: crank/anchor-lever friction slows the grandfather clock, making the chimes lag and creating a false time of death
Key clues: clock hands at 11:10, delayed chimes, crank on anchor assembly, worn anchor lever, scratch near pendulum window, unnatural drag on pendulum rod, guest-room clocks and elevator bell showing 8:15, staff belief in 8:45, true murder time later stated as 9:45
Motive: Hale’s rivalry with Finch and fear/exposure around hotel order/maintenance

I’d score it around 76/100. The opening has strong physical detail — Mallory dead beneath the grandfather clock, the bloodied brass object, the scratched lever, odd crank, and delayed chimes — but the story does not turn those clues into a fully dramatized final reveal.

What works

The mechanical clue is interesting:

crank on anchor assembly
worn anchor lever
abnormal friction
pendulum drag
delayed chimes
guest-room clocks disagreeing with the main hall clock
comparison test where disengaging the crank makes the pendulum swing freely

That is a workable fair-play mechanism. The best scene is Chapter 7, where Eleanor disengages the crank and proves the clock’s rhythm changes. That gives the story a concrete detective test instead of just a verbal explanation.

The murder weapon is also clearer than in some earlier drafts. The story identifies the candlestick, the head wound, and the matching blow fairly directly, which helps.

Biggest problem

The story skips the actual culprit reveal.

Chapter 8 clears Beatrice, Sylvia, and Hugo, then says:

only one name remained

That points to Hale. But instead of showing Eleanor accusing Hale, proving his opportunity, linking him to the crank, and getting the confession, Chapter 9 jumps to aftermath:

Hale’s confession has already been signed and delivered.

That is a major issue. The reader needs the confrontation.

You need a missing Chapter 9 scene like:

“Hale,” Eleanor said, “the crank was not part of the original mechanism. Your maintenance records prove you installed it. The friction slowed the pendulum, delaying the chimes and making the hotel believe Dr. Finch died at quarter to nine. But the guest-room clocks, elevator bell, and the freed pendulum prove the true hour was quarter to ten.”

Hale’s hand went to his watch chain.

“You struck her with the candlestick,” Eleanor continued. “The blood on its base matches the wound, and the metal smear on your toolkit matches the crank. You altered the clock because every alibi in this hotel obeyed it.”

For the first time, Hale looked away from the clock.

Without that confrontation, the ending feels unfinished.

Main issues
1. Time logic is too muddy

The draft uses several times:

grandfather clock: 11:10
mantel clock: 9:45
commotion: 8:45
guest-room clocks/elevator bell: 8:15
true murder time: 9:45

That is too many time anchors.

The clean version should be:

False accepted time: 8:45
True death time: 9:45
How: Hale slowed/delayed the main clock’s chime using crank friction.
Proof: guest-room clocks and elevator bell stayed correct; main hall clock lagged.

Or reverse it if you prefer, but choose one true/false relationship and repeat it consistently.

2. The crank mechanism needs a clearer rule

The story says the crank creates drag and slows the pendulum. Good. But the effect should be stated simply:

The crank pressed against the anchor lever.
The added friction slowed the escapement.
The hands and chimes lagged behind the true time.
Anyone relying on the main hall clock believed the wrong hour.

That would make the mechanism easier than the current phrasing.

3. Hale’s guilt is under-proved

Hale has motive, access, tools, and maintenance records, but the story should add one hard physical link:

oil from the crank on Hale’s glove
matching tool marks from Hale’s toolkit
his fingerprint on the crank
a missing screw from his maintenance kit
candlestick polish/blood trace on his cuff

Right now the evidence points toward him, but the final proof happens off-page.

4. The false suspects are over-cleared

Chapter 8 spends a lot of time clearing Beatrice, Sylvia, and Hugo through records. That is fine, but it becomes report-like:

“The evidence ruled her out completely.”
“Hugo was ruled out by the very machinery he had once doubted.”
“The evidence is unambiguous.”

Those lines should be dramatized rather than stated.

5. Chapter 9 is aftermath without reveal

The aftermath itself is decent: the hotel feels changed, the routines feel broken, and the surviving suspects react to Hale’s downfall. But it belongs after a proper reveal scene, not instead of one.

6. Generated/glitched prose is still significant

These lines hurt polish:

“That's how it's tradition governs nothing worth reimagining the wheel…”
“eighty minutes had passed before it was done.”
“Goodness these tangled puzzles we all must navigate rough seas…”
“Good grief this tangled web we each must navigate rough seas…”

These are clear generation artifacts. They cap the score below the 80s.

Fastest fixes
Add a full Hale accusation/confession scene before the aftermath.
Lock the timeline to one true death time and one false accepted time.
State the crank/friction mechanism in one clean paragraph.
Add one physical clue tying Hale to the crank or candlestick.
Cut malformed catchphrase lines.
Make Chapter 8 shorter and less report-like.
Keep Chapter 9 as aftermath, but only after the missing reveal.
Overall

This has a good mechanical idea and strong atmosphere, but it is structurally incomplete because the final reveal happens off-page.

As written: 76/100.

With a proper Hale confrontation, cleaner time logic, and the generated glitches removed, this could reach 84–87/100.
