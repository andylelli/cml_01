I would place this around the same level as the previous better drafts, but not above them. The biggest improvement is the clearer detective figure: Inspector Harold Finch. The biggest weakness is that the time evidence does not currently make sense.

Main issues:

The clock time contradicts itself.
In Chapter 1, the clock shows “quarter to nine,” but the time is also “recorded as ten minutes past eleven,” then later “twenty minutes past ten.” That gives three different key times in the same opening.
The witness-noise timeline is confusing.
The story says witness accounts heard a noise at “quarter past eight,” but later Chapter 2 discusses “quarter past ten” and “ten minutes past eleven.” These need one consistent timeline.
The clock-tampering is revealed too early.
Finch realises almost immediately that the clock was altered to obscure the chronology. That removes some mystery before the investigation has built tension.
Pronouns still slip.
Finch asks Evelyn a question, but the narration says “she asked.” Agnes is also described with “his voice,” even though Agnes is usually a female name.
The prose is stronger than before, but still overwritten.
Phrases like “the clock’s betrayal,” “truth slipped away like the morning mist,” and “the weight of the investigation pressing down” are atmospheric, but they repeat the same emotional effect too often.

Best fix: create a single timeline before rewriting. For example:

8:15 — witnesses hear the clock strike.
8:45 — victim is actually killed.
9:25 — clock is wound forward/back.
11:10 — body is discovered.
