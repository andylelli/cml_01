This is a promising sundial draft, but weaker than the manor-clock and bell-tower drafts because the central physical mechanism is confused. The setup has good mystery ingredients — sundial, frost, stopped watch, thermometer, warped copper, false time — but the final explanation does not fully make sense.

The roles are clear:

Investigator: Inspector Harold Wren
Victim: Lady Beatrice Ellsworth
Culprit: Charles Pembroke
False suspects: Margaret Huxley, Simon Clarkson, Eleanor Fairchild, Thomas Granger
Murder method: blow to the head with the sundial’s copper gnomon
Cover-up mechanism: cold/frost/warped copper sundial creates a false time reading
Key clues: stopped watch, sundial shadow, 31°F thermometer, frost crystals, warped copper plate, footprints, vehicle log, Charles near garden gate, Charles’s black glove
Motive: Charles’s ambition, estate plans, and fear Lady Beatrice would expose or block him

I’d score it around 72/100.

What works

The image of the crime scene is strong:

Lady Beatrice dead beside the sundial
bloodied gnomon as murder weapon
frost in June
thermometer at 31°F
stopped watch
sundial shadow
warped copper plate

That is a good classic clue table. The draft gives the reader several physical facts immediately, which is better than drafts where the solution appears from nowhere.

The murder weapon also fits the setting. A sundial’s gnomon being used as a blunt weapon is specific and memorable.

Biggest problem

The sundial mechanism does not quite work.

The story says the copper plate contracted in the cold, warped, and delayed the sundial’s shadow. But a sundial shadow is not like a clock hand or a bell chime. Cold metal might warp the plate slightly, but it would not normally “delay” sunlight by several minutes in a clean, readable way.

The current explanation is:

thermometer reads 31°F
copper contracts
sundial plate warps
shadow appears to show 10:20
true death time is different
Charles relies on the false sundial reading

That is imaginative, but physically vague. The reader needs to understand exactly how the sundial lied.

A cleaner version would be:

Charles physically loosened and rotated the sundial plate by five minutes.
The frost concealed the fresh scrape marks around the base.
The 31°F thermometer explains why the frost remained long enough to hide the tampering.
The stopped watch gives the true time.
The sundial gives the staged time.

That would be much stronger than relying on cold contraction alone.

Main issues
1. The watch time changes

Chapter 1 says Lady Beatrice’s watch stopped at 10:15. Later chapters repeatedly say the watch stopped at 10:20. The final reveal also uses 10:15 again.

This needs to be locked.

Best version:

True time of death: 10:15
False sundial reading: 10:20
Purpose: create a five-minute false window for Charles’s alibi

Or, if you want a bigger alibi shift, make it:

True time of death: 10:10
False sundial reading: 10:20

Right now the contradiction is partly accidental rather than deductive.

2. The final reveal gives a broken time statement

The reveal says:

“Lady Beatrice died before twenty minutes past ten. In fact, the true time of death was ten minutes to eleven.”

That is impossible, because 10:50 is after 10:20, not before it.

This is the biggest continuity error in the draft. It should probably say:

“The true time of death was ten minutes past ten.”

or:

“The true time of death was a quarter past ten.”

Pick one and use it consistently.

3. The thermometer is vivid but overburdened

The 31°F thermometer is a great clue. But it should explain frost, not the entire false-time mechanism.

Better role for the thermometer:

The frost proves the sundial had been iced or chilled deliberately.
The frost preserved old footprints and blurred fresh ones.
The frost concealed scrape marks where Charles rotated the sundial plate.
The frost made witnesses trust a dramatic natural explanation.

That makes it useful without asking it to perform impossible physics.

4. The false solution is not very strong

The story points suspicion at Eleanor Fairchild because:

her footprints are near the sundial
she was in the garden
she has ambition/social motive

But the footprint clue is quickly weakened because the prints lack dew and were made earlier. This is fine, but it could be sharper.

Make Eleanor’s false suspicion stronger:

her shoe print is found beside the gnomon
she had argued with Lady Beatrice over inheritance/social position
she had access to the garden before discovery
only later does Wren prove the print was older because frost formed over it

That would make the false solution more satisfying.

5. Charles needs a stronger physical link

The black glove near the garden gate is useful, but it appears late and feels a little convenient.

Plant it earlier:

Charles is described wearing black leather gloves in Chapter 1.
One glove is missing in Chapter 4.
A matching glove is found near the garden gate in Chapter 9.
Soil/frost/blood on the glove links him to the sundial and gnomon.

That would make the final clue feel earned.

6. Chapter 10 is likely unnecessary

Chapter 9 already gives the final test, clears suspects, accuses Charles, produces the glove, and gets a confession. Chapter 10 appears to be another clearance/reveal chapter.

This has been a repeated pattern in several drafts: the story solves the mystery, then keeps explaining the solution.

Best fix:

Chapter 9 = final test and confession
Chapter 10 = aftermath only

No more alibi clearing in Chapter 10.

7. Generated phrasing remains

These lines hurt polish:

“By then it was a twenty minutes past ten.”
“Inspector harold wren watched…”
“The result proved one theory and ruled out the other…”
“more an issue of ambiguity than of deliberate misdirection”

These should be removed or rewritten naturally.

Fastest fixes
Lock the watch time: probably 10:15.
Fix the impossible line “true death was ten minutes to eleven.”
Replace “cold delayed the sundial shadow” with a clearer tampering mechanism.
Use frost to hide scrape marks or preserve/alter footprint evidence.
Plant Charles’s black glove earlier.
Strengthen Eleanor Fairchild’s false-suspect path.
Make Chapter 10 aftermath-only.
Remove generated-note phrases and repeated “twenty minutes past ten” glitches.
Overall

This has a strong visual premise but a weaker physical explanation than the clock and bell drafts.

As written: 72/100.

With the sundial mechanism clarified and the time contradiction fixed, it could reach 80–84/100.
