What works

The character introductions are much better. Chapter 1 names Sir Lionel, introduces Lady Constance, Charles, Eleanor Marsh, and Dr. Fenwick with clear household roles and visible tensions. This follows the “suspect-shaped introduction” advice much better than earlier drafts.

The mystery structure is also stronger. The story now has a proper false solution around Charles, then redirects suspicion toward Lady Constance through correspondence, access, motive, and clock evidence. The final physical test with the winding key is a real improvement.

The discriminating test is the best part: Bramley states two theories, demonstrates the backward winding, matches resistance/dust/paint damage, and uses that to collapse the false timeline. That is much closer to the structure you want.

Main problems
1. The murder method is still unclear

The reveal says Lady Constance is responsible for Sir Lionel’s murder, and she admits she acted to restore order, but the actual killing is not clearly described. Did she poison him, strike him, suffocate him, stage a fall?

This keeps the ending from scoring higher. Add one clear sentence:

“I gave Lionel the draught myself,” Lady Constance said. “When he collapsed beneath the clock, I wound the hands back to make the hour protect me.”

Or:

“I struck him with the decanter after dinner, then altered the clock before anyone entered the hall.”

2. Chapter 9 and Chapter 10 repeat each other

Chapter 9 already performs much of the final trap and points to Lady Constance. Chapter 10 then repeats the alibi clearances and performs the test again.

Best fix:

Make Chapter 9 the preparation/test setup.
Make Chapter 10 the full reveal and confession.

Or merge them into one stronger final chapter.

3. Some report-like clearance lines remain

Chapter 10 says:

“Charles Wentworth is cleared because…”
“Eleanor Marsh is cleared because…”
“Inspector Harold Bramley was cleared because…”

That last one especially feels like validation text leaking into the story. Bramley does not need to be cleared unless he was a suspect.

Rewrite clearances as dramatic deduction, not scoring output.

4. Pronouns still need a hard pass

Examples:

Sir Lionel is described as “her evening suit.”
Eleanor Marsh is described with “his gaze.”
Dr. Fenwick is introduced with “She carried herself…”
Lady Constance’s “his proximity” appears in relation to her account.

These errors lower character clarity and prose polish.

5. The time logic is not fully grounded

The clock shows 10 minutes past 11, and the clock was wound back 40 minutes, but the actual death window is not cleanly stated. The story should lock this:

False displayed time: 11:10
True time: 11:50
Why this matters: Lady Constance had an alibi for 11:10 but not 11:50.

Or choose different times, but state the false time and true time clearly.
