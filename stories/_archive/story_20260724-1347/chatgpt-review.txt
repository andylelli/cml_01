This is one of the better clock-mechanism drafts. The manor setting suits the device well, the clock clue is more technical than usual, and the final test gives the mystery a real deductive center. The main weakness is that the timing math is still slightly muddy, and Chapter 9 contains some report-like repetition.

The roles are clear:

Investigator: Inspector Evelyn Harcourt
Victim: Lady Beatrice Marwood
Culprit: Charles Fenwick
False suspects: Annabelle Marwood, Harold Grimshaw, Margaret Cox, Simon Hastings
Murder method: stabbing with a ceremonial dagger
Cover-up mechanism: Fenwick alters the grandfather clock’s pendulum so the chimes create a false timeline
Key clues: misaligned minute hand, scratch on pendulum, shortened/altered pendulum period, servant’s note about odd chimes, watchmaker’s screwdriver, wound-drying inconsistency, Fenwick cufflink beneath clock cabinet
Motive: Fenwick’s desperation over the failing estate and Lady Beatrice refusing financial help

What works

The clock mechanism is the best part. This draft understands that the clock is not just decoration; it is the murder machine’s alibi tool.

The strongest chain is:

the household trusts the grandfather clock
everyone anchors their alibi to the chimes
the clock had been “running oddly” earlier
the pendulum has a scratch and altered swing
the minute hand is misaligned
the clock is running fast
the wound’s blood/drying state contradicts the supposed chime-time death
Fenwick alone has the skill, access, tools, and motive to alter the mechanism
his cufflink under the clock cabinet places him at the scene

That is a proper detective clue chain.

The final test is also much stronger than many earlier reveals because Harcourt actually demonstrates the deception. She measures the pendulum, compares it with her pocket watch, and uses the experiment to break the household’s trust in the chimes.

Biggest improvement

The culprit proof is much more physical than usual.

Fenwick is not exposed only because he looks nervous. He is exposed by:

unique mechanical expertise
possession of a watchmaker’s screwdriver
brass filings / pendulum scratch
the servant’s note about odd chimes
the clock’s measured fast-running period
blood-drying evidence contradicting the clock time
the cufflink beneath the clock cabinet

That is a much stronger evidentiary bundle than the drafts where the culprit mainly confesses after being emotionally pressured.

Main remaining issue

The timing math needs to be simplified.

The draft says the clock gains thirty seconds per hour, and then says that if the clock chimed at 10 minutes past 11, the real time was 9 minutes and 30 seconds past 11. That difference is only thirty seconds, which is too small to meaningfully alter most murder alibis.

If the murder plot depends on shifting the time enough to create a false alibi, the clock needs to be wrong by more than thirty seconds total.

A cleaner version:

Fenwick adjusted the pendulum in the afternoon.
By the time of the murder, the clock had gained twenty minutes.
The household heard the clock chime 11:10, but the real time was only 10:50.
Fenwick used that twenty-minute discrepancy to place himself in the library when, in truth, he still had time to kill Lady Beatrice and return.

Or:

The clock was not merely running fast; Fenwick manually advanced the chime mechanism.
The pendulum clue proves tampering, but the hand-setting explains the larger false timeline.

Right now the clock clue is smart, but the numerical consequence is too small.

Main issues
1. The pendulum mechanism is strong but under-scaled

“Thirty seconds per hour” is realistic-sounding, but dramatically weak. A murder alibi usually needs several minutes, not half a minute.

Fix by making the clock gain:

five minutes per hour
fifteen minutes over the afternoon
twenty minutes between tea and the murder
or by combining pendulum adjustment with manual hand-setting

2. The line “It had taken thirty seconds per hour in all” is awkward

It appears early and feels like generated mechanism-note leakage. It should be replaced with natural observation:

“The pendulum was fractionally too brisk. Over several hours, that small haste would become a dangerous lie.”

3. The final test slightly over-explains the alibi clearances

Chapter 9 clears Annabelle, Harold, Margaret, and Simon one by one. That is useful, but the prose becomes procedural.

Instead of repeating formal clearances, dramatize them:

Annabelle’s defiance collapsed into exhausted relief.
Harold removed his spectacles and wiped them with shaking hands.
Margaret looked not triumphant but old.
Simon stopped writing for once.

Keep the logic, but make it feel like fiction.

4. The cufflink is good, but should be planted earlier

The cufflink works as a final physical tie to Fenwick, but it appears late. Plant one earlier detail:

Fenwick wears ornate family-crest cufflinks in Chapter 1 or 2
one sleeve later looks bare
Harcourt notices the missing cufflink before revealing it

That makes the reveal feel earned rather than convenient.

5. The murder weapon needs a cleaner ownership trail

The ceremonial dagger is a good manor-mystery weapon. But the story should connect it more specifically to Fenwick:

Fenwick had access to the display cabinet
Fenwick knew the dagger’s position
Fenwick’s fingerprints are absent because he polished the hilt
a matching crest/tool mark links the dagger to the clock cabinet area

6. The false Annabelle direction is decent but could be sharper

Annabelle has motive through inheritance/debts, and the household suspects her. That works. But the false solution would be stronger if one clue genuinely points to her before being overturned.

Add:

Annabelle had argued with Lady Beatrice shortly before the chimes
a torn letter to Annabelle is found near the clock
Lady Beatrice had just refused to help Annabelle financially
Annabelle’s guest-wing alibi depends on the clock, so it initially looks weak

Then Harcourt’s clock test can rescue her alibi.

7. Minor generated phrasing remains

Lines like these should be cleaned:

“The Grandfather clock room and study”
“a creased note handed over by her pocket”
“pressed into her palm pocket”
“in a kitchen where clocks dictated every move and records”
“This was evidence, plain and undeniable…”

These are not plot-breaking, but they lower polish.

Fastest fixes
Increase the clock discrepancy to something plot-significant.
Plant Fenwick’s cufflink earlier.
Make the ceremonial dagger’s access trail clearer.
Rewrite the formal alibi clearances as dramatic reactions.
Replace generated-note lines with natural prose.
Strengthen the false Annabelle suspicion.
Clarify whether Fenwick altered the pendulum only, manually advanced the hands, or did both.
Overall

This is a strong manor-clock mystery and one of the best mechanism drafts so far.

As written: 81/100.

With the timing math fixed and the final chapter polished, this could reach 87–90/100.
