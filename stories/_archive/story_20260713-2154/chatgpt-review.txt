Motive: Hale’s hidden operations/past threatened by Sylvia

What works

The gramophone mechanism is good. It is simple enough for a reader to understand:

Sylvia dies earlier.
Hale plays a recording of her voice later.
Everyone thinks Sylvia was still alive.
The recording creates a false timeline.
Footprints and scuffs near the gramophone expose the staging.

That is stronger than the gate-latch mechanism because it directly explains the mystery: the witnesses heard Sylvia, but not live Sylvia.

The atmosphere is also one of the draft’s strengths. The parlor, fog, rain, gramophone, stopped watch, and hotel isolation all fit the murder method. The title Echoes of Deceit actually matches the mechanism.

Biggest problem

The timeline is muddy.

The story has these competing points:

witnesses heard Sylvia at 8:00
pocket watch stopped at 11:10
actual death was 10:45
gramophone was set to play at 11:10
suspects all anchor their stories to the 8:00 voice

That is too many time anchors. The mechanism will be stronger if it uses one clean false-alive moment.

Best version:

True death time: 10:45 p.m.
False alive time: 11:10 p.m.
Method of deception: Hale plays a recording of Sylvia’s voice at 11:10.
Purpose: makes everyone believe Sylvia was alive after Hale had left or established his terrace alibi.
Physical proof: footprints and scuffs near the gramophone; recording left half-played; watch stopped at 11:10 because Hale staged it.

Then remove or downplay the 8:00 laughter unless it is just ordinary background.

Main issues
1. The Chapter 1 contradiction is backwards

Chapter 1 says:

Sylvia’s laughter was heard at 8:00.
Her watch stopped at 11:10.

But that is not inherently contradictory. If someone heard her at 8:00 and the watch says 11:10, she could simply have died later.

The real contradiction should be:

Witnesses heard Sylvia at 11:10, but medical evidence proves she died at 10:45.

Or:

The watch says 11:10, but body evidence proves she died earlier.

Right now the first hook feels clever, but logically it does not quite bite.

2. Chapter 5 title is broken

Chapter 5 is titled:

Dr.

That should be fixed. Use:

Dr. Finch’s Alibi
The Conference Alibi
The Doctor’s Balance

3. Prompt leakage is still present

This is especially bad:

“Eleanor Voss advanced to the following concrete detail: Footprints lead gramophone room signs struggle.”

And later:

“Eleanor Voss moved quickly to the next concrete detail…”
“the trail bent toward…”

Those lines must be deleted completely. They are not prose.

4. Chapters 9 and 10 repeat the solution

Chapter 9 already clears the suspects and exposes Hale. Chapter 10 repeats the same reveal with nearly the same evidence.

Better structure:

Chapter 9: final reveal and confession.
Chapter 10: aftermath only — authorities arrive, emotional fallout, final echo of the gramophone.

5. The suspect clearances are too mechanical

Lines like:

“You are cleared.”
“You are ruled out.”
“You are innocent.”
“As for myself…”

are clear but report-like. Rewrite as deduction:

Mallory had been under conference lights all evening. Beatrice’s quarrel explained the torn silk, not the murder. Hugo’s bar alibi held. Only Hale had been close enough to set the gramophone and cold enough to let Sylvia’s voice become his alibi.

6. Pronouns still drift

There are still “he/his” references for Eleanor and “her/she” references around Hale. These do not break the plot, but they make the draft feel unpolished.

Fastest fixes
Make the timeline one clean contradiction: actual death 10:45, false voice 11:10.
Remove the misleading 8:00 contradiction or make it background only.
Rename Chapter 5.
Delete all prompt-leakage lines.
Merge Chapter 10 into aftermath only.
Rewrite clearances as dramatic deduction.
Fix pronouns throughout.
Overall

This is a good draft with a strong central device.

As written: 73/100.
