Turning to Hugo Vane, Eleanor cited the meeting logs and witness testimonials. "Hugo Vane's alibi holds. He was in a meeting during the murder, and the records, along with multiple accounts, confirm he could not have slipped away.

The evidence is irrefutable, which proves he is innocent. " The tension that had gripped Hugo's features eased, his jaw unclenching as the verdict was spoken. Their alibis are confirmed by concrete evidence, and neither could have tampered with the mirror or escaped unseen. " She paused, letting the truth settle over the room. ## Chapter 10: The Culprit Revealed

The hush of late night in the marble-floored entrance where salt air drifted through revolving doors was broken only by the faint whisper of wind against the tall windows, the echo of distant waves, and the soft creak of Eleanor Voss's shoes on the marble floor. The air was cold, heavy with the scent of rain-soaked stone and the metallic tang of the brass fixtures, and the glow of lamplight cast long, uncertain shadows across the room. Eleanor paused just inside the threshold, her breath a shallow rhythm in the silence, and let her eyes adjust to the gloom. The world outside was shrouded in vernal darkness, but within these walls, anticipation pressed in as thick as fog. Captain Ivor Hale stood by the reception desk, framed by the pale light that spilled from a single shaded lamp.

His posture was rigid, shoulders squared as if bracing for a blow, and his hand rested on the head of his cane with a grip that threatened to splinter the wood. The confrontation hung between them, unspoken but inevitable-a final reckoning that would not be postponed. Eleanor drew a slow, deliberate breath, the chill of the marble floor seeping through the soles of her shoes, and stepped forward until she stood within the circle of lamplight. "You know why I've asked you to remain, Captain," she said, her voice low but steady. The words seemed to hang in the air, suspended in the hush. "There is nothing left to hide. " She watched his face for any flicker of denial or fear, but he met her gaze with the weary resignation of a man who knows the game is lost.

In the silence that followed, the distant sound of a wireless set-barely audible-carried the static of war news, a reminder that the world's violence did not end at the hotel's threshold. He did not answer at once. Instead, Captain Ivor Hale's eyes shifted to the great mirror that dominated the far wall, its surface reflecting the lobby in fractured, distorted lines. The scratches at the edge of the gilded frame caught the lamplight, glinting like scars. "You've been thorough, Miss Voss," he said at last, his tone brittle. "I imagine you have your theory-your arrangement of facts. " Eleanor's reply was gentle, but her words carried the weight of finality. "It's not theory, Captain. It's what the evidence demands. The mirror-set at exactly thirty degrees-was no accident. The scratches on the frame are fresh, deliberate.

You alone had the knowledge to place it so, to create a reflection that misled every witness. At ten minutes past nine, you were the only one unaccounted for. The muddy impressions that trailed into the windswept dunes, the absence of any credible alibi, the precise timing-all of it points to you. " She let out a breath, the sound rough in the silence. "And the motive? " he asked, his voice barely more than a whisper. "You think it was anger? Or something more petty? " Eleanor hesitated, the sadness of what she must say settling over her like a shroud. "Jealousy, Captain. Dr. Mallory Finch's success-her brilliance-became a shadow you could not escape. You envied her, resented the way she commanded a room, the way others deferred to her.

In that moment, you let jealousy drive you to murder. " Captain Ivor Hale's hand trembled on his cane, and for a moment, Eleanor thought he might deny it. But the fight had gone out of him. "I killed Dr. Mallory Finch," he said, the words flat and unadorned. "I struck her with the letter opener-quickly, before she could cry out. I set the mirror to thirty degrees and slipped away while the others saw only what I wished them to see. It was... all I could do. " He looked up, his eyes hollow. "I told myself I was protecting my reputation, my legacy. But it was jealousy. Nothing more. " The confession fell into the silence like a stone dropped in a well. Eleanor felt a cold ache in her chest, a mingling of triumph and sorrow. She had found the truth, but there was no victory in it-only the echo of loss. "You manipulated the very fabric of the room, Captain," she said, her voice soft.

"You made us all doubt what we saw. But the facts remained: the mirror, the scratches, the footprints, the absence of an alibi. As I questioned Beatrice Quill, Hugo Vane, and Sylvia Trent, each detail of their whereabouts was confirmed-Beatrice with the housekeeper at the time of the murder, Hugo in the lounge with the night porter, and Sylvia reading in the Library, her presence noted by two guests. Their statements matched the records, and not one of them could have tampered with the mirror or left unseen. Only you could have committed the crime. " She nodded, the motion slow, defeated. "I suppose you'll want to summon the authorities. " "They've already been notified," Eleanor replied. "They'll take your statement. The war has made monsters of us all, in ways we never expect. But justice-such as it is-must be served. " A long silence stretched between them.

The lobby, once a crucible of suspicion, now felt emptied of all but regret. The lamplight flickered, casting Captain Ivor Hale's shadow across the marble, and Eleanor watched as the man who had orchestrated such careful deception seemed to shrink beneath the weight of his own admission. The sound of footsteps in the corridor signaled the arrival of Beatrice Quill, her face pale but composed, and Hugo Vane, his cynicism stripped bare by exhaustion. Sylvia Trent hovered at the threshold, her composure brittle. Each bore the marks of the ordeal: Beatrice's hands twisted her pearls, Hugo's notepad hung forgotten at his side, and Sylvia's eyes shone with unshed tears. The truth had changed them all, leaving wounds that would not easily heal. Eleanor turned to the others, her voice gentle but resolute. "You are all cleared.

The evidence, the witnesses, the very order of events-each points away from you. Only Captain Ivor Hale could have tampered with the mirror and escaped unseen. " Relief mingled with sorrow in their faces, and for a moment, the burden of suspicion lifted. "I never thought it would end like this. We are none of us innocent, but-" She broke off, unable to finish. Hugo Vane managed a wan smile. "The world is a stage, Miss Voss, but some acts cannot be undone. " Sylvia Trent simply nodded, her silence more eloquent than any words. Eleanor lingered by the mirror, her hand resting on the frame. The reflection that stared back at her was older, sadder, but resolute.

She had found the truth, but at a cost she could not have foreseen. The Grand Seaside Hotel would heal, in time, but the memory of Dr. Mallory Finch's death would linger in every corridor and every reflection. As Captain Ivor Hale was led away by a pair of silent, unnamed officers, Eleanor felt the weight of the night begin to lift. The lobby, washed in the pale promise of springtime dawn, seemed to breathe again. She watched the others drift toward the windows, drawn by the first blush of light on the sea. The war would continue, the world would turn, but for one fragile moment, there was peace. Eleanor allowed herself a final, quiet breath-half relief, half mourning. The truth had been seen, not in the mirror, but in the silent grief etched on the faces of mourners. She reached for her notebook, the leather cool and smooth beneath her fingers, and turned toward the door.

Outside, the wind carried the scent of salt and the promise of spring. Inside, The Grand Seaside Hotel stood silent, holding its secrets close as the world moved on.
