What works
The compass idea is clearer here than in the earlier version. The story now explicitly shows that when the compass is held level, the bearing is true, but when tilted as the judge habitually held it, the reading shifts by 15 degrees. That gives the case a proper physical demonstration.   Pasted markdown
The ledger evidence is also more usable. The fresh ink, mismatched handwriting, altered timing entries, and post-facto correction all point toward someone rewriting the record. That makes the ledger feel like evidence rather than just atmosphere.   Pasted markdown
Nora is stronger as a character this time. “Torque says otherwise” is overused, but her mechanical worldview, spanner, oil, ledger access, and resentment over misconduct allegations give her a defined shape.   Pasted markdown
Main issue: the compass still does not clearly prove murder opportunity
The reveal says the tilted compass is enough to move a person “from the office to the beach — on paper.” That is a good sentence, but the story still needs a precise map of why that matters.   Pasted markdown
Right now the reader understands:
compass tilted = 15-degree error
ledger altered = false times
Nora knew both
therefore Nora is guilty

But the missing middle is:
What exact false location did the compass create?
Who did the judge think he saw?
Where was Nora actually standing?
How did the 15-degree error protect her?
How does that place her near the office/paperweight at the murder moment?

Use a clean spatial rule:
From the judge’s window, the tilted compass reading placed Nora on the beach path, safely away from the office.
Held level, the same sightline points to the service door beside the hotel office.
The judge did not see Nora on the beach; he saw her leaving the office after killing Gaunt.
That would make the compass decisive.
Opening continuity is confusing
Chapter 1 begins with Montague alive, speaking and holding the paperweight. Then within the same scene he is suddenly slumped dead, and Gwendolyn is calmly serving tea beside the bloodied paperweight. The transition is too abrupt.   Pasted markdown
It reads almost like two scene versions got spliced together: one before the murder and one after. Fix by choosing one.
Better:
Open after the death:
Montague Gaunt lay behind the desk, one hand still near the brass globe paperweight.
Nora Quayle stood by the window, oil on her sleeve, saying only, “Torque says otherwise.”
Or:
Open before the death, then end Chapter 1 with the discovery later.
But do not have Montague alive and dead inside the same continuous moment without a clear time jump.

Humour / Wit: 7/10

The problem is repetition. These phrases recur too often:
“Fact first.”
“Torque says otherwise.”
“As it must be.”
“If only they knew.”
“That would be unfortunate.”

Catchphrases can define voice, but here they become labels. Use each once or twice, not every chapter.
Chapter 8 demonstration is good but under-dramatized
The best bit is Nora physically tilting the compass and showing the needle shift. Keep that.   Pasted markdown
But the demonstration should be tied to a real window/sightline:
Bertram placed the compass on the judge’s windowsill.
“Show us the beach bearing,” he said.
Nora tilted it as the judge always had.
The needle settled where the ledger said it should.

Then Bertram levelled the case.

The line no longer crossed the beach. It ran straight to the office door.
That would give the reader a visual “aha” moment.
Chapter 9 reveal is too thin
Chapter 9 declares Nora guilty, but it does not contain enough emotional or evidentiary force. It lists the compass, ledger, and alibis, then says Nora killed Gaunt.   Pasted markdown
It needs either:
a confession from Nora, or
a final physical link between Nora and the paperweight, or
a specific ledger entry only Nora could have forged, or
a sightline demonstration proving she left the office at the murder time.

Right now the leap from “Nora had access and knowledge” to “Nora killed him” is plausible but not fully satisfying.
Add:
The brass key in Nora’s pocket opened the office drawer where the paperweight had been kept.
The oil on the paperweight matched the machine oil used on her spanner.
The altered ledger line was written with the garage fountain pen, not the office pen.
One of those would lock her to the weapon or post-crime staging.
Chapter 10 is actually good
The final chapter is mostly aftermath, and that is a plus. It does not spend too long re-explaining the evidence. It shows the hotel resuming: evidence collected, Gwendolyn writing to the board, Harriet with cocoa, Theodora leaving, Nora escorted out, Bertram drinking tea.   Pasted markdown
The memory of Montague helping a child with a kite is a good humanizing touch. It makes him more than just “the victim who threatened people.”   Pasted markdown
Keep that kind of victim-life memory.

“She took in the room. She took in the room…” — duplicated phrase.

repeated chapter-ending abstractions: “truth waited,” “evidence waited,” “secrets of the sea,” “world resumed.”

Also, the first chapter needs a clean time jump or rewrite, because Montague is alive, then dead, with no clear transition.   Pasted markdown
Fastest fixes
1. Rewrite Chapter 1 as a clean post-body-discovery scene.
2. Add a map/sightline rule: tilted compass points to beach; level compass points to office/service door.
3. Tie Nora physically to the murder weapon or forged ledger with one decisive clue.
4. Give Nora a clearer confession or breakdown.
5. Reduce catchphrases by 70%.
6. Give Gwendolyn, Harriet, and Theodora one human scene each before the reveal.
7. Keep Chapter 10’s aftermath structure.
Overall
This is the best compass version so far: better atmosphere, better Nora, better final aftermath, and a clearer compass demonstration. But the puzzle still needs one decisive bridge between bearing error and murder opportunity.
As written: 80/100.
With a precise sightline map, cleaner opening, one weapon/ledger lock, and less catchphrase repetition, this could reach 86–88/100.
