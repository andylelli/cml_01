This is a good locked-room mechanism draft, but it is not as strong as the best clock/tide drafts yet because the elevator trick is under-explained and the final chapter still contains obvious validation leakage. The setting and premise are fresh, though: the elevator indicator acting as a false witness is a nice break from clock-only mysteries.

The roles are clear:

Investigator: Eleanor Voss
Victim: Dr. Mallory Finch
Culprit: Sylvia Trent
False suspects: Hugo Vane, Captain Ivor Hale, Beatrice Quill
Murder method: blow to the head with a heavy glass decanter / paperweight confusion early
Cover-up mechanism: elevator bell/indicator manipulated so it seemed the cage never left ground floor
Key clues: elevator indicator fixed at ground floor, bell heard at 9:30, scratch half an inch above latch, grease on brass lattice, shoeprint in service corridor dust, Sylvia’s questions about the elevator, monogrammed handkerchief caught in the cage, forged Finch papers
Motive: Dr. Finch discovered Sylvia’s forgeries and threatened to expose her

I’d score it around 79/100. The opening has a strong impossible-crime setup: Finch is dead inside her locked third-floor suite, the elevator indicator is stuck at ground floor, the bell was heard at half past nine, and nobody recalls the cage moving.

What works

The central impossible setup is strong. The story gives the reader a clear contradiction:

Finch is dead in a locked third-floor suite.
The elevator indicator says the cage stayed at ground floor.
The bell rang at 9:30.
No one saw the elevator move.
The corridor was supposedly empty.
Yet there are fresh marks on the elevator gate.

That is a good locked-room mystery engine. The scratch and grease on the elevator latch are especially useful because they physically undermine Hugo’s claim that the elevator did not move or was untouched.

The suspect narrowing is also solid. Beatrice and Hale have corroborated alibis; Hugo looks suspicious because of forged papers and debts, but his reporter alibi holds; Sylvia’s timeline has the real gap. The staff member placing Sylvia near the service corridor and the shoeprint match move the story toward her fairly.

The best clue chain is:

Sylvia asked detailed questions about the elevator days earlier.
A high-heeled shoeprint in dust matches Sylvia.
Her papers contain Finch’s questionable signature.
A handkerchief with Sylvia’s monogram is caught in the cage.
She had the motive: Finch found the forgeries.
She confesses.

That is strong in principle.

Biggest problem

The elevator mechanism is not explained clearly enough.

The story says:

the bell rang
the indicator stayed at ground floor
the cage did not move
someone reached into the mechanism
the indicator resets only if the cage is moved
Sylvia manipulated the mechanism

But I still do not fully know what Sylvia physically did.

Did she:

ring the elevator bell from below without moving the cage?
move the cage but manually reset the indicator?
open the gate from the service corridor and attack through it?
use the elevator shaft to reach the suite?
stage the bell after the murder to create a false locked-room timeline?

The reveal needs one plain mechanical paragraph.

For example:

Sylvia did not ride the elevator to Finch’s suite. She used the service stair before the corridor was watched, entered the suite, killed Finch, and locked the door behind her. Then, from the ground-floor elevator gate, she reached through the brass lattice and pulled the bell linkage by hand. The bell rang at 9:30, but because the cage itself never moved, the indicator remained at ground floor. The false bell made everyone believe the crucial event happened while the elevator was visibly still.

Or, if the cage actually moved:

Sylvia took the elevator up before anyone was watching, killed Finch, returned by the service stair, and then manually reset the indicator to ground floor by reaching through the latch. The bell at 9:30 was staged afterward, proving nothing about the cage’s true movement.

Pick one. Right now the story has good parts, but the mechanism is still foggy.

Main issues
1. Weapon confusion: paperweight vs decanter

Chapter 1 says a heavy glass paperweight lies near Finch’s hand with dark residue. Chapter 3 says the weapon is a heavy glass decanter with sticky residue. The reveal does not strongly resolve which object killed her.

Use one weapon.

Best choice:

Keep the decanter.
Remove the paperweight or make it harmless desk clutter.
In the reveal, say the wound at the crown of Finch’s head matches the decanter’s weighted base.

2. Hugo appears in the opening as hotelier, then later is almost treated like a suspect/villain

That is not a fatal problem, but the false suspicion against Hugo needs a cleaner link. The forged papers and debts make him suspicious, but the story should distinguish:

Hugo had motive about hotel debts.
Sylvia had the forged Finch papers.
Hugo’s reporter alibi clears him.

Right now the forged-paper thread sometimes feels attached to Hugo, sometimes Sylvia.

3. The final test is not quite a test

The shoeprint comparison is a proper physical test. The elevator demonstration is weaker because it does not clearly prove one theory over another. The line says the test “ruled out one theory and left the other standing,” but the reader is not shown exactly how.

Make the test prove one specific fact:

pulling the bell linkage from ground floor rings the bell without moving the cage
the indicator remains at ground floor
the scratch appears exactly half an inch above the latch
the handkerchief snags in the same place

That would convert the ending from explanation to demonstration.

4. Chapter 9 repeats after the confession

Sylvia confesses before the elevator demonstration is fully complete. Then Eleanor explains the two theories again and performs the test. That order weakens the reveal.

Better order:

compare shoeprint
show handkerchief
demonstrate bell/indicator trick
clear Hale/Beatrice/Hugo
accuse Sylvia
Sylvia confesses
stop proving

5. Chapter 10 still has validation leakage

This paragraph is clearly not story prose:

“Captain Ivor Hale had been elsewhere, and the evidence around Single shoeprint matching sylvia trent shoes held…”
“The evidence around Finch last seen alive five minutes put Beatrice Quill elsewhere…”
“Whatever else stayed uncertain, the evidence around Beatrice quill seen kitchens staff confirm placed Hugo Vane well away from it…”

That is generated checklist leakage. It must be deleted.

6. The hotel name changes

The opening uses Art Deco hotel / Port Selwyn and later Chapter 8 says The Selwyn Hotel. That is probably intended. But the phrasing also shifts from “Art Deco hotel lobby and elevator foyer” to “Hotel lounge” and “The Selwyn Hotel.” Lock it as:

The Selwyn Hotel, Port Selwyn

Then use that consistently.

7. The locked-room geography needs a diagram-level sentence

The story needs one clean explanation of the physical layout:

third-floor suite
main elevator
service corridor/back stair
ground-floor foyer
who could see which passage
what the night porter could or could not observe

Without this, the “locked room” depends on vague movement rules.

Fastest fixes
Explain the elevator trick in one plain paragraph.
Decide whether Sylvia used the service stairs or the elevator to reach/leave the suite.
Make the final demonstration show the bell ringing while the indicator stays at ground floor.
Put the demonstration before Sylvia’s confession.
Use one weapon: preferably the decanter.
Delete Chapter 10 validation leakage.
Lock the hotel name and geography.
Keep the shoeprint and monogrammed handkerchief — those are the strongest physical culprit links.
Overall

This is a good concept draft with a fresh mechanism, but it needs mechanical clarity. The elevator idea could be excellent, but the reader must understand exactly how the bell, cage, and indicator were faked.

As written: 79/100.

With a clean elevator explanation, one weapon, and the Chapter 10 leakage removed, this could reach 86–89/100.
