## Chapter 10: The Cost of Truth

Midnight's residue clung to the hotel ballroom, lamplight bouncing off polished floorboards and setting the dust motes aglow. Wax polish mingled with the scent of damp wool, and an edge of chilled metal seemed to flavor the air after so many taut hours. Wind rattled the tall windows in gusts, the night outside dense and hungry, threatening to swallow what little comfort remained. Beatrice Quill stood just at the border of the dance floor, arms clamped around her waist, bravado faded to a pallor. The room's quiet felt more suffocating than peaceful-a vacuum where nothing dared stir. Eleanor Voss surveyed the gathering from the center, eyes sharp beneath the flickering overhead lamps. Harsh light picked out the drawn shadows sprawled across each wall. Her feet ached from long vigilance, shoulders stiff from shouldering suspicion after suspicion.

Rain tapped a soft, steady rhythm on the pane, but inside, anxiety pressed in close, thick as fog. Hugo Vane, propped against a marble pillar, had traded his usual ease for a nervous, watchful lean. Sylvia Trent, the perennial sentinel, posted herself near the doors, fingers laced so tightly her knuckles shone white. Where once the room had brimmed with laughter and music, now it felt exposed, like a painting stripped of its colors and left only in stark charcoal lines. She crossed to the long central table, where the forged documents sprawled beneath the harsh glow. Picking up the top page, Eleanor felt the coarse paper beneath her gloves, traced the blotted ink and the jagged signature. The evidence offered only one answer. He beckoned Hugo Vane closer. "Mr. Vane, a moment, if you please. " Hugo obliged, shoes squeaking across the floor.

He bent, eyes narrowing as he examined the embossed seal, pausing at the faint whorl of a fingerprint near the margin. "Hugo Vane's alibi was confirmed because multiple witnesses saw him in the lounge at the time. " When Hugo straightened, their eyes met, and the certainty between them was unmistakable. Outside, the world waited, changed and unchanged, ready for whatever day must come.
