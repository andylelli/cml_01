## Chapter 9: The Final Trap

The sound of rain, steady and insistent, drummed against the glass, while the faint scent of coal smoke lingered beneath the sharper tang of cold marble. Eleanor Voss stood at the reception desk, her notes fanned before her, lamplight flickering across the inked columns. The silence was thick, broken only by the echo of Eleanor's heels on the marble as she stepped beneath the brass clock. Its hands, still stubbornly fixed at ten minutes to ten, gleamed dully in the lamplight. The cold in the lobby seemed to deepen, a wintry chill that pressed against every exposed surface. Eleanor let her gaze sweep the room, registering the nervous fidget of Beatrice's gloved hands, the set of Captain Ivor Hale's jaw, the way Hugo Vane's fingers tapped a silent rhythm on the pillar, and the tremor that ran through Sylvia Trent's posture. The moment was poised-one final test, and the truth would be forced into the open. "Thank you for joining me," Eleanor began, her voice steady, though her heart pounded with the weight of what she was about to do. "We stand at the end of a tangled thread. The clock above us has been the silent judge of every alibi, every recollection.

But tonight, I intend to show you that it is not the impartial witness we believed. " She let the words settle, watching as Captain Ivor Hale's brows drew together, Beatrice Quill's eyes flickered with something like panic, and Hugo Vane's mouth tightened. The sound of metal scraping against brass was sharp in the hush as she opened the clock's casing. The hands were set back deliberately, so that when the commotion was heard at ten minutes past ten, the clock insisted it was still ten minutes to ten. " A gasp rippled through the room. "The true time of death, as confirmed by witnesses, was ten minutes past ten. The clock's testimony is a lie, engineered to shield the murderer. Every alibi pegged to its reading collapses. " She let her gaze settle on each suspect in turn. "Now, we must reconsider who had the means, the opportunity, and the motive. " Captain Ivor Hale bristled, his voice rough. "I checked the time as I entered-ten minutes to ten. I've said as much.

But if the clock lied, then my memory is worthless. " He looked to Eleanor, a flicker of fear beneath the bluster. "I never left the lobby. The night porter saw me, as did Mrs. Trent. I could not have slipped away without being noticed. " Sylvia Trent nodded, voice trembling. "It's true. Captain Ivor Hale was at his post. I passed through several times. He never left the lobby for more than a moment, and never during the critical window. " Eleanor met Captain Ivor Hale's gaze, a measure of relief flickering between them. "Your alibi holds, Captain. You are cleared. " Hugo Vane, shifting uneasily, spoke next. "I was in the lounge, listening to the radio. Sylvia saw me pass, and the night staff can confirm. I never left until after the alarm was raised. " Sylvia Trent, voice steadier now, confirmed, "I saw Hugo Vane in the lounge. The radio was on, and he was there throughout.

I checked the corridor myself. " Eleanor nodded, her tone definitive. "Hugo Vane is cleared. His alibi holds. " Eleanor turned to Sylvia Trent. "You were behind the desk, attended by the staff. The kitchen was full, and your presence was noted by more than one person. You could not have left without being missed. " Captain Ivor Hale added, "I saw Sylvia Trent at her post. She did not leave the lobby during the crucial window. " With three suspects cleared, Eleanor let the silence stretch, then turned to Beatrice Quill. "Beatrice, you said you were in the Dining Room after dinner, and that everyone saw you there. Yet you were observed near the clock shortly before the murder. The marbled entrance hall awash with sea-salt air was steeped in a chill that seemed almost physical, the wintry night pressing at the tall windows and the hush broken only by the faint ticking of the clock above. " The echoes of her pronouncement hung as she laid her notebook flat, her hand steady despite the exhaustion in her bones. "If you've found something new, Eleanor, say it plainly.
