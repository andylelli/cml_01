'Some debts can't be paid with ledgers or confessions. ' Eleanor remained by the hearth, the warmth of the dying fire a meager shield against the chill that crept in from the sea. She thought of Dr. Mallory Finch-her sharp words, her refusal to be silenced, the way she had unsettled the fragile peace of the Seabreeze Hotel. The cost of truth, Eleanor realized, was never paid by the guilty alone. It rippled outward, touching each survivor, leaving them changed in ways no verdict could undo. The rain eased, drumming softer on the roof. In the lounge, the survivors gathered what remained of their dignity and their hope, each carrying the mark of the night's reckoning. Eleanor watched them go, her own heart heavy but resolute.

She turned back to the hearth, the last glow of the embers catching in the brass of the torn button, and let her hand rest there, feeling the warmth fade beneath her palm.
