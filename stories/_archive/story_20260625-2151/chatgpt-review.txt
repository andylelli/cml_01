I’ll judge this against the same rubric: role stability, clue logic, chapter balance, reveal quality, and prose leakage. I can already see one major issue: Eleanor appears to be both dead victim and later speaking/acting.

Thought for 13s

This is a step forward in reveal clarity, because the ending finally gives Captain Hale a direct murder method: he stabbed Eleanor with a hidden blade, then tampered with the clock. That is much better than the earlier “I manipulated the clock” endings.

But the draft has one huge continuity problem:

Eleanor Voss is dead in Chapter 1, but she keeps appearing alive and speaking in later chapters.

That caps the score hard. I’d mark this around 56/100.

What improved

The final confession is much better than many earlier ones. Hale says:

“I killed her. I did it with the blade I kept from the field.”

That directly connects him to the murder, not just the clock tampering. This is exactly the kind of reveal improvement we wanted.

The test scene is also better. Beatrice opens the clock, releases the catch, shows the minute hand springing forward, and proves the displayed time was false. That is a real discriminating test rather than just a speech.

Beatrice is also clearer as the investigator. She takes charge, questions witnesses, follows the clock evidence, and pushes through the false solution.

Biggest problem

Chapter 1 says Eleanor is dead:

Eleanor Voss lies at the foot of the staircase.

But later Eleanor speaks, gives statements, reacts emotionally, and is present during the final trap. The ending even has Beatrice catching Eleanor’s gaze after Hale is led away.

That breaks the story unless those scenes are explicitly flashbacks. The text sometimes says “In a remembered moment,” but it is not controlled enough. It reads as if Eleanor is simply alive after death.

Best fix

Do one of these:

Option A — Eleanor is the victim

Then remove living Eleanor from Chapters 2–10.

Replace her later role with another character, for example:

Victim: Eleanor Voss
Investigator: Beatrice Quill
Culprit: Captain Ivor Hale
False suspect: Dr. Mallory Finch
Witness / secret-holder: Lydia Voss, Eleanor’s sister
Other suspect: hotel manager or receptionist

Option B — Eleanor is alive

Then make someone else the victim.

For example:

Victim: Vivian Ashcroft, gala organiser
Investigator: Beatrice Quill
Culprit: Captain Ivor Hale
Witness: Eleanor Voss
False suspect: Dr. Mallory Finch

This may be easier because the later chapters already use Eleanor as a witness/secret-holder.

Other issues
1. Chapter 1 is confusing

It starts with Eleanor entering, then seeing what appears to be her own dead body:

“Eleanor Voss's gloved hand…”
then
“lay a woman's body-her own reflection…”
then
“Eleanor Voss… now lay…”

That is the core confusion. The opening should not use the victim’s viewpoint if she is already dead.

Better:

Beatrice Quill entered the lobby and saw Eleanor Voss lying at the foot of the staircase.

2. The false solution works, but the wrong people are present

Dr. Finch as the false suspect is good. Her solitary library alibi, argument over accounts, and nervousness give her plausible suspicion. But Eleanor’s living presence keeps undermining that structure.

3. Prompt leakage still appears

This is bad:

“Beatrice Quill surfaced the next hard detail…”
“captain, ivor, hale, seen, acting, nervously…”
“The chapter treated those facts as observable evidence…”

That is validation/planning language leaking into prose. It hurts polish and immersion.

4. Chapter 9 and Chapter 10 may still overlap

Chapter 9 already exposes Hale and has him taken into custody. Then Chapter 10 begins another revelation. That is likely repetitive unless Chapter 10 is purely aftermath.

5. Pronouns still slip

There are several moments where “he/him” or “she/her” are attached to the wrong person. This is less severe than the dead-victim issue, but still damages readability.

Overall

This draft has better mystery machinery and a better final confession, but the Eleanor contradiction is too big.

Without that problem, this could be around 72–75/100.

As written, because the victim appears alive repeatedly, I’d keep it around 56/100.
