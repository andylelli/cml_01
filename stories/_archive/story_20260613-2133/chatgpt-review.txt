This version has some of the best clue ideas so far, but it has one major story-breaking problem:

Felix Vautier is the victim in Chapter 1, but he is alive and questioned from Chapter 2 onward.

That continuity error pulls the score down heavily, even though the soot/wax/thermometer mechanism is interesting.

I’d mark this around 53/100.

Biggest issue

Chapter 1 says:

“The body lay slumped in an armchair… the victim unmistakably Felix Vautier…”

But Chapter 2 says:

“Felix Vautier stood near the doorway…”

Then the ending reveals Felix as the culprit.

That cannot work unless Chapter 1’s body is someone else. The fastest fix is simple:

Victim: Lord Holloway / another named estate owner
Culprit: Felix Vautier, estate steward
Investigator: Ellen Lacy
False suspects: Phyllis Lisle, Ronald Somerville, Juliette Drury

Then replace “Felix Vautier” as the corpse in Chapter 1 with the actual victim’s name.

What is working well

The mechanical mystery idea is strong:

soot line inside clock glass → fire burned later than claimed → wax seals cooled unevenly → clock was opened and rewound → thermometer confirms later heat → Felix’s ledger motive → cyanide confession.

That is a much more interesting clue chain than simple “the clock was wound back.” The soot and wax clues feel physical and fair-play.

Chapter-by-chapter issues
Chapter 1
Strong atmosphere.
Victim is named, which is good.
But the victim is Felix, who later appears alive.
The clue is over-explained too early: “The culprit wound back…” style logic appears before investigation develops.
Character introductions are decent: Phyllis, Ronald, and Juliette get role + tension.
“Victim’s study” should be replaced with the victim’s actual name or “the study.”
Chapter 2
Felix appears alive, creating the major contradiction.
The soot clue is repeated heavily from Chapter 1.
Ronald is referred to with “she/her” despite being male-coded.
Phyllis and Juliette have better character pressure here.
The thermometer clue is useful.
Chapter 3

This chapter has major prompt/validation leakage:

“The chapter moves forward through detailed insight instead than recap.”
“Required evidence was kept explicit in scene action…”

Good clue: wax seals partially melted and cooled unevenly.
Too short and too report-like.
“The elapsed time was confirmed as forty minutes” still sounds like a note.

This is one of the better chapters dramatically.

Chapter 7
This chapter is mostly filler.

“The chapter moves forward through vivid detail instead than recap.”

It raises a useful doubt — mechanical fault vs tampering — but does not develop it enough.
It is too short compared with the surrounding chapters.
Chapter 8
Good purpose: preparing the test.
But it mostly recaps existing clues instead of setting up a dramatic test.

More prompt leakage appears:

“Evidence handling remained explicit and on-page…”

The test needs a clearer hypothesis:

If Felix rewound the clock after poisoning, the wax, soot, and thermometer should align with a later fire.

Pronoun slips continue.
Chapter 9
This should be the discriminating test chapter, but it does not actually perform the test.

It contains more validation leakage:

“Required evidence was kept explicit in scene action…”

It is too short and feels incomplete.
It mentions “clock face showing quarter past six,” which conflicts with the recurring “ten minutes past eleven.”
Chapter 10
The confession is clearer than in many earlier drafts.
Felix admits to both clock tampering and cyanide poisoning, which is good.
But the reveal fails because Felix was the Chapter 1 victim.
The clearance section is too report-like.
Pronouns break again: officers escort “her” away when Felix is male-coded.
Best fixes
Make someone else the victim.
Felix should be the living steward/culprit, not the corpse.

Remove all prompt/validation leakage.
Delete lines like:

“The chapter moves forward…”
“Required evidence was kept explicit…”
“Evidence handling remained…”

Fix pronouns.
Ellen = she/her. Felix = he/him. Ronald = he/him. Phyllis = she/her. Juliette = she/her.

Make the Discriminating Test actually happen.
Chapter 9 should physically demonstrate the timing:

recreate fire → observe soot line → warm clock area → examine wax cooling → show that the clock must have been opened after poisoning.

Keep the time consistent.
The story uses ten minutes past eleven, quarter past six, and forty minutes, but the relationship between them is muddy. Lock one clean timeline.
Overall

This has a stronger mystery mechanism than many earlier drafts, but the Felix contradiction is severe. Once fixed, this could jump from about 53/100 to the low 70s because the soot/wax/thermometer idea is genuinely good.
