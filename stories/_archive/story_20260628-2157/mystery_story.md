## Chapter 9: The Revelation

Later that morning, the hush in the Hotel Lobby was broken only by the faint sound of rainwater trickling from the eaves and the low, anxious murmur of guests beyond the velvet ropes. Warm candlelight flickered along the marble floor, casting a shifting glow that softened the sharp lines of tension etched into every face. The air was thick with anticipation and the lingering scent of freshly baked bread from the breakfast room, but beneath it all, a chill clung to the edges of the room-a reminder that the events of the past night had left their mark. Eleanor Voss stood at the centre, her back to the tall windows where the last beads of rain clung and the twinkling stars of the midsummer night had been replaced by pale, uncertain daylight. She drew a slow, steadying breath, echoing the final moment of stillness that had settled over The Grand Seaside Hotel only minutes before.

The clock above the reception desk, so long the silent accomplice to deception, loomed over them all. Eleanor's fingers curled around the edge of her notebook as she surveyed the assembled guests-Beatrice Quill, her composure brittle as spun glass; Sylvia Trent, pale and watchful; Hugo Vane, his battered notebook pressed to his chest; and Captain Ivor Hale, standing apart, his posture rigid, eyes shadowed. The tension was palpable, the sound of the rain outside a muted counterpoint to the storm gathering within. Eleanor's voice, when it came, was low but unwavering. 'We have all waited for answers,' she began, her gaze sweeping the room. 'The time has come to lay out the truth, as plainly as I can. ' Her tone was measured, yet the emotional toll was evident in the tremor that threatened to break through. She paused, letting the silence settle, then continued. 'The murder of Dr.

Mallory Finch was not a crime of passion, nor a random act. It was deliberate-planned, and executed with a chilling precision. ' She turned first to Beatrice Quill. 'Beatrice Quill is cleared. Her alibi holds because multiple witnesses, including the staff and several guests, confirm she was in the Dining Room throughout the dinner hour. The time on her wristwatch matches the travel clock exactly, which proves she could not have tampered with the lobby clock or committed the murder. ' Beatrice's breath caught, her shoulders sagging as relief and grief warred in her expression. The brittle mask she had worn for days cracked, and she dabbed at her eyes with a trembling hand. Eleanor's attention shifted to Sylvia Trent. 'Sylvia Trent is cleared as well. Witness statements, including my own, confirm that she was with me in the Dining Room during the crucial window.

Her watch matches the standard clock, therefore her alibi is confirmed-she could not have left my sight or committed the crime. Eleanor addressed her gently. 'Hugo Vane is ruled out because his alibi is confirmed by the staff, who saw him in the lounge between ten and eleven PM. His pocket watch matches the travel clock, which proves he could not have tampered with the mechanism or committed the murder. ' Hugo's lips parted in a silent exhale, and he closed his notebook, the narrative complete but the sense of closure distant. Eleanor did not spare herself from the scrutiny. 'Eleanor Voss was cleared because her own presence in the Dining Room was confirmed by Sylvia Trent and several guests.

My alibi holds, and I could not have committed the crime. ' She let the words hang, a rare admission of vulnerability, before turning to the final figure. All eyes fixed on Captain Ivor Hale. The candlelight caught the silver in his hair, the rigid set of his jaw. Eleanor's voice softened, but the steel beneath it was unmistakable. 'Captain Ivor Hale, you were seen near the lobby clock before dinner. I found a small, dense weight hidden inside the clock's mechanism-a device that slowed the clock by exactly twenty minutes. This created a false timeline, making it appear that Dr. Finch died at ten minutes past eleven, when in truth, the moment was twenty minutes later.

'When I compared the hotel clock's pendulum to the travel clock, I found the swing was irregular. The mechanism bore fresh scratches and oily marks-proof of recent tampering. Captain Hale, you alone were unaccounted for at the true time of death. Your position allowed you privacy, and your motive was clear. ' A hush fell as Eleanor's final deduction connected every clue.
