'I suppose I'll return to London,' she said, her tone thin. 'Try to forget. But I don't think I ever will. ' Sylvia Trent managed a wry, sad smile. 'Perhaps I'll write about it. The investigation was over, but the consequences would ripple outward, touching each life in ways she could not foresee.

As the others drifted from the lounge-Captain Ivor Hale with his measured gait, Beatrice Quill with her head bowed, Sylvia Trent pausing at the threshold-Eleanor remained behind. She pressed her palm to the glass, feeling the cool smoothness beneath her skin, and allowed herself a final moment of quiet. The cost of truth, she thought, was never borne by one alone. Outside, the sound of the sea rose and fell, steady as breath. Eleanor closed her eyes, letting the echo of the waves anchor her to the present. When she opened them again, the lounge was empty, the mirror reflecting only sunlight and the promise of spring.
