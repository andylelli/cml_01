Captain Ivor Hale turned away, his shoulders slumped, the burden of suspicion finally lifted but replaced by something close to sorrow. In the aftermath, Beatrice felt neither triumph nor relief. Instead, a cold ache settled in her chest-the knowledge that truth, once revealed, left wounds that could not be stitched closed by justice alone. The clock, its hands now set right, marked the hour with a mechanical indifference that felt almost cruel.

The cost of revelation was written in the faces of those who remained: Eleanor's devastation, Captain Ivor Hale's quiet regret, and the hollow resignation in Dr. Mallory Finch's final glance. A gust of wind rattled the windowpanes, drawing every eye momentarily to the darkness beyond. The lounge was thick with the scent of rain and the iron tang of endings. Beatrice closed her notebook for the last time, her fingers lingering on the battered cover. The truth, at last, had been spoken-but the price was a silence more profound than any before. In that silence, the guests of the vintage seaside hotel were left to reckon with their secrets and their grief, as the autumn night pressed close.
