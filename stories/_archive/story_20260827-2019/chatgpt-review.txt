What works

The hourglass clue is now much easier to follow:

The hourglass takes 20 minutes to empty.
At 11:10, it is half full.
Therefore it was turned around 11:00 if exactly half full, or around 10:50 if the story means half the upper bulb had already drained.
Hale claims he turned it at 10:45.
That claim is false.

The draft’s final test improves on the previous version by explicitly giving a corrected timing: the hourglass must have been turned at 10:50, not 10:45.

The brass statue murder method is also clean. The wound, blood, hair, and statue are all aligned early, so the reader is not juggling multiple weapons.

The false suspects are handled cleanly. Beatrice is in the kitchen, Sylvia is in the lounge, and Hugo is on the telephone/dining room, leaving Hale as the only person whose account depends on the hourglass lie.

Biggest remaining problem

The hourglass math still needs one exact sentence.

The draft says:

At 11:10, the hourglass was half full.
A twenty-minute hourglass at the ten-minute mark is half full.
Therefore it was turned at 10:50.

That only works if “half full” means half the sand remained in the upper bulb after ten minutes of flow.

But in Chapter 1, the upper bulb is “clearly half full” at 11:10. If the hourglass was freshly turned, that implies it was turned at 11:00, not 10:50. The draft’s wording creates a small but important ambiguity:

Upper bulb half full usually means ten minutes have elapsed.
Ten minutes past 10:50 is 11:00, not 11:10.

So either the actual turn time should be 11:00, or the observed time should be 11:00, not 11:10.

Best clean version:

Claimed turn: 10:45.
Observed state at 11:10: upper bulb half full.
Twenty-minute glass, half full = ten minutes elapsed.
Therefore actual turn: 11:00.
Hale lied by fifteen minutes.

Or:

Claimed turn: 10:45.
Observed state at 11:00: upper bulb half full.
Twenty-minute glass, half full = ten minutes elapsed.
Therefore actual turn: 10:50.
Hale lied by five minutes.

The first version is stronger because a 15-minute lie matters more.

Main issues
1. The murder window is still not sharp enough

The draft says Finch was alive at 11:10, but also that Hale used the hourglass deception to cover the murder. If Finch was alive at 11:10 and the body is found at 11:10 or almost immediately after, the killing window collapses.

You need a clean sequence:

10:45 — Hale claims he turned the hourglass.
10:50 — Finch is last seen alive.
11:00 — Hale kills Finch and turns/resets the hourglass.
11:10 — body is discovered; hourglass is half full.
False story: the hourglass had been running since 10:45.
Truth: it had only been running since 11:00.

This makes the hourglass actually hide the murder.

2. Hale’s confession still phrases the trick weakly

He confesses that he “staged the hourglass,” but he should explicitly admit the lie:

“I said I turned it at a quarter to eleven. I did not. I turned it after the blow, at eleven, hoping the half-fallen sand would make everyone accept my earlier routine.”

That is the missing confession sentence.

3. The test is repeated after the reveal

Chapter 8 already has the demonstration and confession. Then the draft continues with more hourglass comparison language and “Run again in front of them all…” That feels like scaffold leakage and repetition.

Cut everything after Hale’s confession except a brief emotional close.

4. Chapter 9 repeats the clearances

Chapter 9 is mostly aftermath, but it again explains Beatrice, Sylvia, and Hugo’s alibis. It is clearer than the earlier version, but still unnecessary after Chapter 8.

Make Chapter 9 emotional fallout only:

Beatrice realizes Hale killed to protect her.
Sylvia’s social composure cracks.
Hugo writes nothing for once.
Eleanor leaves the hourglass unturned.

5. Generated leakage remains

These lines still hurt:

“The record now held: Witness testimony victim last sighting timing; Body discovery scene victim severe head.”
“It had taken twenty minutes in all.”
“I spun the vintage clock hand…” / “I twisted the brass key…” instead of consistently turning the hourglass.

The worst leakage is in Chapter 3, where internal evidence labels appear directly in the prose.

6. Object wording is inconsistent

The draft calls the hourglass action:

spun the battered stopwatch
spun the vintage clock hand
twisted the brass key
turned the hourglass

Use only:

turned the hourglass

Anything else confuses the mechanism.

Cut the duplicated demonstration after the confession.
