This version is more ambitious, but it is also more confused than the previous one. The setup starts well — Dr. Finch investigating Beatrice’s death — but the ending breaks the story because Beatrice is both the victim and the culprit. That is a major continuity problem.

I’d mark it around 48/100.

Area	Mark	Notes
Premise / concept	7/10	The clock tampering, stopped pocket watch, scratch on winding key, and false timeline are good mystery ideas.
Opening hook	6/10	Beatrice dead in the study with a stopped watch is a clear hook.
Plot structure	4/10	The investigation has stages, but the culprit reveal contradicts the opening.
Character clarity	2/10	Beatrice is dead, then later alive and guilty. Mallory/Dr. Finch identity also shifts awkwardly.
Dialogue	5/10	Mostly understandable, but repetitive and too direct.
Atmosphere / setting	5/10	Rainy study atmosphere works, but some generated lines remain.
Mystery clues	5/10	The clues are promising, but the logic collapses when the victim becomes the culprit.
Pacing	5/10	It moves chapter to chapter, but several chapters repeat the clock-tampering point.
Ending / reveal	2/10	The reveal is not coherent because Beatrice cannot be both murdered victim and murderer.
Prose / polish	4/10	Pronoun errors, note-like lines, and broken phrases remain.
Main issues by chapter
Chapter 1: Discovery
Strong opening idea: Beatrice is found dead in her study, holding a stopped pocket watch.
Dr. Finch / Mallory identity is confusing. The chapter begins with “Dr. Finch,” then later calls her “Mallory.” This is okay only if introduced as Dr. Mallory Finch.
Pronoun errors: “Captain Hale… beside him,” “the doctor moved closer, his heart racing.”
Note-like exposition: “The interval… came to forty minutes” and “The mechanism relied on clock, tamper, and The Study…” feel like planning notes.
Good clue: the scratch on the winding key is useful and better than vague “tampering.”
Chapter 2: Initial Reactions
Repetition: the forty-minute interval and clock contradiction are repeated again.
Eleanor’s alibi is unclear: she says she left at ten, then was gone only a short while to speak with Hale.
Pronoun errors: Eleanor’s words are followed by “His words trailed off.”
Mallory’s pronouns switch: “eyes of both Eleanor and Hale upon him,” then “steeling himself.”
Good tension: Hale lighting a cigarette with trembling hands works well.
Chapter 3: The Clock’s Secrets
This repeats the clock-tampering explanation from Chapters 1 and 2.
The scratch clue is repeated almost word for word.
Eleanor’s line repeats from Chapter 2: “I was only gone for a short while…”
“Eleanor and Ivor” suddenly appears, but Hale was previously called Captain Hale. Use “Captain Ivor Hale” once, then pick either Hale or Ivor.
The chapter does not add enough new evidence.
Chapter 4: Interrogating Eleanor
This chapter has a clearer purpose: Eleanor’s alibi and possible motive are questioned.
Good detail: Beatrice had threatened to expose deceit. This gives a possible motive.
But the neighbor is too vague: “a woman, the neighbor” should be named.
Pronoun errors continue: “his words trailed off,” “eyes… upon him,” “he said.”
**The chapter is too long and repeats “every detail matters” several times.
Chapter 5: Captain Hale’s Alibi
Hale’s pub alibi is useful.
But the clock behind the bar is suddenly also still/tampered with, which confuses the central clue. The important clock should stay the study clock.
Mallory pronouns switch repeatedly: “his instincts urging his to dig deeper.”
Hale’s alibi is not actually verified here. He says people at the pub can confirm it, but no one does yet.
The chapter creates suspicion but does not advance proof enough.
Chapter 6: Revisiting the Clock
Good idea: the clock casing being warmer than the room suggests recent handling.
But “twenty degrees Fahrenheit” appears as a random note. It needs to be rewritten naturally or removed.
The scratch on the winding key is repeated again.
The chapter explains the mechanism but still vaguely: “The clock’s mechanism relied on its tampering…”
This chapter is better than repeated interrogations because it adds a physical clue.
Chapter 7: The Discriminating Test
The title finally matches the action somewhat. Mallory demonstrates clock tampering.
But the actual test is still vague. What exactly does she test? Temperature? scratch? gear alignment? timing difference?
Major logic issue: it says the clock’s manipulation suggested Beatrice Quill had been involved, but Beatrice is supposed to be dead.
The chapter points suspicion at Eleanor, not Beatrice, so the ending later feels unsupported.
It ends with Mallory thinking she needs to confront them, but she has already confronted them in the scene.
Chapter 8: Clearing the Suspects
Good purpose: Eleanor is cleared by the neighbor and fingerprints not matching her.
Opening sentence repeats twice.
Strong clue: fingerprints on the clock casing are not Eleanor’s.
But if fingerprints matter, whose are they? This must be answered before the reveal.
Hale’s alibi is still not fully confirmed.
Pronoun errors remain: Mallory turns “his attention,” “he asked.”
Chapter 9: The Culprit Revealed
Major contradiction: Beatrice is alive and confronted, even though Chapter 1 says Beatrice lay lifeless.
The accusation line is impossible: “You had the opportunity to tamper with the clock before Beatrice’s death,” said to Beatrice herself.
Dr. Finch’s alibi is nonsensical because Dr. Finch is the investigator, not a suspect.
The culprit reveal should point to someone alive and consistently present, probably Hale or Eleanor.
The final summary is too report-like: “The case was closed…”
“They were taken into custody” is vague. Who is “they”?
Best fix

Choose one of these two clean versions:

Option A: Beatrice is the victim

Victim: Beatrice Quill
Investigator: Dr. Mallory Finch
False suspect: Eleanor Voss
Culprit: Captain Ivor Hale

Then Chapter 9 should accuse Hale, not Beatrice.

Option B: Beatrice is the culprit

Victim: someone else, such as Lord Ivor Voss
Investigator: Dr. Mallory Finch
False suspect: Eleanor Voss
Culprit: Beatrice Quill

Then Chapter 1 must not show Beatrice dead.

Right now, because Beatrice is both dead and guilty, this draft drops below 50 despite having some good clues.
