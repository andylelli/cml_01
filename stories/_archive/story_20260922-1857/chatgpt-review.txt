This is stronger than the Lockwood/Ottoline clock draft and much cleaner than the mannequin-shadow story. It is close to the earlier choir-school watch version, but this draft has a slightly better emotional atmosphere and more character-life texture. The main weakness is still that Chapters 8–10 blur together: Chapter 8 accuses Agatha, Chapter 9 reveals Agatha, and Chapter 10 half-reveals/half-aftermaths her again.
I’d score it around 84/100.

Victim: Leonard Nettleship
Culprit: Agatha Innes
False suspects: Millicent Ashcombe, Violet Radcliffe
Murder method: blow to the head with heavy candlestick
Cover-up mechanism: Agatha advances the manor hall clock by fifteen minutes and corrects/controls the official ledger so Leonard appears to have died at 7:30, while his pocket watch proves the true time was 7:15

What works
The opening is clear and fair. Leonard’s pocket watch stopped at 7:15, while the manor hall clock reads 7:30, immediately establishing the puzzle. The discrepancy is simple enough for the reader to hold in mind, which is important.   Pasted markdown
The theme is also strong: this is not just about a clock being wrong. It is about official truth versus private truth. Agatha controls the clock, the ledger, the keys, the school, and the institutional story. Leonard’s watch becomes the small personal object that resists authority. That is a good thematic match.   Pasted markdown
The character texture is better than usual. Leonard is not only a corpse; he has memories, jokes, music, kindness, a father, a battered piano, and a relationship with Millicent and Violet. That raises the story above a pure mechanism puzzle.   Pasted markdown
Main issue: the final three chapters overlap
Chapter 8 already lays out the decisive proof: pocket watch 7:15, hall clock 7:30, ledger correction in Agatha’s hand, and Agatha as the only person with motive and means.   Pasted markdown
Chapter 9 then repeats the clearance and directly says:
“Agatha Innes killed Leonard Nettleship.”

That means the culprit is fully revealed in Chapter 9.   Pasted markdown
Chapter 10 then returns to Agatha in the study and says the outcome is settled, but it still feels like part of the reveal rather than pure aftermath.   Pasted markdown
Best structure:
Chapter 8: The Final Trap — full clock/ledger demonstration, accusation, Agatha exposed.
Chapter 9: Clearing the Innocent — very brief emotional clearance of Millicent and Violet; no proof recap.
Chapter 10: The Rector’s Unimpeachable Word — aftermath only: school, music, keys, children, changed authority.

Right now Chapter 9 is doing both Chapter 8 and Chapter 9’s job.
Mystery logic: strong, but make the mechanism more physical
The logic is good:
Leonard’s watch = 7:15.
Hall clock = 7:30.
Ledger correction supports official 7:30.
Agatha controls ledger and clock access.
Therefore official time was manufactured.
But the draft could use one more physical detail proving the clock was advanced, not merely inaccurate.
Add something like:
A fresh scrape marked the winding arbor where the key had slipped.
The oil on the regulator was still tacky.
The minute hand had been lifted and reset, not drifted naturally.
That would make the proof feel less verbal and more forensic.
Agatha’s motive works, but sharpen it
Agatha’s motive is clear: preserve the school and prevent scandal. Her note says another scandal could close the school, and she cannot permit the legacy to be destroyed.   Pasted markdown
But the draft still needs one more specific threat from Leonard:
What exactly was Leonard going to reveal?
Fraud in school accounts?
Abuse of authority?
Forged admissions records?
A past death or cover-up?
Misuse of choir funds?

The current motive is emotionally plausible, but a sharper secret would make the murder feel more inevitable.

Millicent’s line works:

That is elegant and tied directly to the clock theme.   Pasted markdown

Chapter 10 is good aftermath material
The final chapter has the right ingredients: Agatha diminished, keys returned, Violet managing her debts, Millicent arranging music for children, Leonard remembered through his photograph and patience, the school resuming under a changed moral order.   Pasted markdown
The ending line is good:
“The music, in time, would return. The clocks would be wound. And the house would go on, measured now not by authority, but by the quiet, uncompromising tick of the truth.”

Keep that.
The only issue is that Chapter 10 still includes too much evidence-object staging with candlestick/keys/study. Make it more emotional and less evidentiary.
Scaffold/prose issues to remove
These are the main generated-feeling patterns:
“Leonard’s unmeant joke…” repeated too often
“His voice hovered / lingered in the hush” repeated
“Yes.” / “No.” as flat-answer pattern too often
“That remains to be seen” repeated
“How utterly...enlightening” repeated
“The truth stood revealed”
“the memory of Leonard hovered”
“the outcome was settled”

The Leonard memory device works, but it is overused. Keep maybe three Leonard memories total, not one per chapter.
Fastest fixes
1. Keep Chapter 8 as the full proof/reveal.
2. Make Chapter 9 only emotional clearing of Millicent and Violet; remove “Agatha killed Leonard” from Chapter 9 if already said in Chapter 8.
3. Make Chapter 10 pure aftermath: music returns, Agatha’s keys surrendered, children rehearse, Edith leaves.
4. Add one physical clock-tampering detail.
5. Specify the scandal Leonard threatened to expose.
6. Reduce repeated Leonard memory lines.
7. Keep the Violet/Millicent humour; it is one of the draft’s best features.
Overall
This is a good upper-mid draft. It has a clean central clue, strong theme, good dry humour, and better human texture than many of the clock/mechanism stories. It is not quite top-tier because the reveal structure is still muddled and the motive needs one sharper factual secret.
As written: 84/100.
With the reveal/aftermath separation fixed, one stronger physical clock clue added, and Agatha’s secret specified, this could reach 89–91/100.
