## Chapter 7: Secrets and Lies Surface

Eleanor Voss set her notebook down with a soft thud on the low table, the sound oddly sharp in the dim glow of the hotel lounge as early evening pressed in. Lamplight cast long, wavering shadows across the smooth wood, and the faint fragrance of old tobacco lingered beneath the sharper scent of polish. The warmth of the room was at odds with the cold, vernal dusk visible through the tall windows, where the last daylight flickered against the glass. Eleanor's mind still reeled from the collapse of Captain Hale's alibi-her thoughts a tangle of timelines and contradictions-but she forced herself to focus on the present: the living, the nervous, the not-yet-cleared. Sylvia Trent hovered by the fireplace, her arms folded tightly across her starched apron. The fire's glow did little to warm her expression.

Her hands, usually so steady, trembled as she poured tea from the heavy silver pot, the liquid sloshing dangerously close to the rim. "I was in the lounge, Miss Voss," she said, her voice low, each word measured as if she feared tripping over the truth. "Between the first chime and the second pot of coffee. The guests saw me. It was not the fear of guilt, but of being wrongly accused-of the hotel's routines unraveling beyond repair. Beatrice Quill, perched on the edge of a velvet chair, twirled her pen between gloved fingers, the movement betraying a restlessness at odds with her usual poise. The lamplight caught the sharp angle of her jaw as she fixed Eleanor with a half-smile. "You're determined to pin this on someone, Eleanor. I admire your tenacity, if not your optimism. Beatrice's lips curled in a wry smile. "Nerves are a professional hazard. I was looking for a story, not a murder. And I certainly wasn't looking for a winding key. " The bravado rang hollow, and Eleanor caught the way Beatrice's foot tapped a silent rhythm against the floor-a tell she'd noted before.

The performance was convincing, but the subtext was clear: Beatrice was hiding something, though not, Eleanor suspected, the knowledge of clockwork. At the far end of the lounge, a pile of documents lay half-concealed beneath a stack of guest ledgers. Eleanor reached for them, her fingers brushing the rough edge of a folder marked with Hugo Vane's name. There were references to silent partnerships, debts paid in cash, and a series of withdrawals that suggested a motive deeper than mere convenience. She did not comment, but the detail settled in her mind like a stone dropped into still water. Hugo's self-effacing charm, his anxious hovering near the bar, took on a new cast in the lamplight. Sylvia's voice broke the silence.

"If you're looking for someone with a reason to hurt Dr. Finch, you won't find it here. " Her hands shook as she set down her teacup. "She was strict, but fair. I owed her my job-my place here. I'd never..." She stopped, swallowing hard. Eleanor nodded, her tone gentler. "Your alibi is sound, Sylvia. The guests and the log both place you here, in the lounge, until after the murder. That's more than most can claim. " Relief flickered in Sylvia's eyes, but it was tinged with something else-resentment, perhaps, or the lingering fear that innocence might not be enough. Beatrice leaned forward, her voice dropping. "You know, everyone keeps talking about the reception clock-how it showed ten minutes past ten, how every guest swears by it. But what if the clock's the liar? " Her gaze darted to Eleanor, then away. "I saw Hugo near the bar that morning, fussing with something behind the counter.

He said it was nothing, but..." She trailed off, the implication left hanging. Eleanor made a note, her mind already leaping ahead. The guests' unwavering faith in the reception clock was a comfort, but also a trap-the kind of certainty that could be weaponized. A brief, brittle laugh escaped Beatrice. "If only clocks could testify, we'd all be out of a job. " The tension in the room eased for a heartbeat, the absurdity of their predicament surfacing in a flash of shared irony. Eleanor allowed herself a faint smile. "If only, Miss Quill. Until then, we have to trust the living-and their paperwork. As the lamplight deepened and the spring dusk pressed against the windows, Eleanor gathered her notes. The hotel lounge, once a refuge of routine, now felt charged with secrets and the weight of unspoken fears. The clocks still ticked, the guests still whispered, and the truth-whatever it was-remained just out of reach. But for the first time, Eleanor sensed the pattern emerging: not in the certainty of the reception clock's ten minutes past ten, but in the uneasy silences and the shadows cast by hidden motives. The question was no longer who had the opportunity, but who had the need.

## Chapter 8: The Discriminating Test

"We'll begin now," Eleanor Voss said, her voice carrying just enough to hush the low murmur in the hotel reception area, evening shadows stretching across the marble floor. The air was thick with expectation, the faint scent of polish mingling with the sharper tang of damp wool and the distant echo of foghorns rolling in from the vernal coast. Lamplight flickered against the brass fixtures, casting a pale glow over the assembled faces-Captain Ivor Hale, Beatrice Quill, Sylvia Trent, and Hugo Vane-each arranged with the studied casualness of people who know they are being watched. The reception clock's steady tick cut through the hush, its authority now a subject of suspicion rather than comfort. Eleanor's hands were steady as she lifted the reception clock's winding key from the desk, the metal cool and slightly rough beneath her fingers.

She set beside it a standard key, borrowed from the maintenance room, its surface unmarred by the deep, uneven abrasion marks that ringed the original. The contrast was stark in the lamplight: one key smooth, the other scored and dulled by recent, forceful use. The silence in the room was broken only by the faint sound of wind pressing against the glass and the distant chime of the kitchen clock, marking time with serene indifference. Eleanor glanced at the faces before her, her gaze lingering for a moment on Hugo Vane, whose jaw tightened as she placed the keys side by side. "Sylvia, the staff log and multiple guest testimonies confirm you were in the lounge between the clatter of breakfast trays and the first chime of the library clock. ## Chapter 9: Suspect Clearances
