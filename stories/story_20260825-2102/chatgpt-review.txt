What works

The best part is the suspect narrowing. The story does a good job clearing the false suspects through evidence that does not depend on the reception clock:

Dr. Finch is in Room 12 with a guest and chambermaid.
Hale is in the smoking room with multiple witnesses.
Beatrice is in the kitchen/staff corridor.
Sylvia is the one with desk access, clock access, fingerprints, ink, and motive.

That gives the reveal a proper narrowing structure. Chapter 8 is especially clear because Eleanor lays out which evidence is tied to the kitchen clock and staff records rather than the false reception clock.

Sylvia’s motive is also stronger than Beatrice’s in the previous version. Hugo publicly humiliating her before the board, threatening to expose her debts and thefts, and endangering the hotel gives her a real reason to panic. The overheard office exchange in Chapter 7 plants this well before the confession.

The final accusation has a coherent evidence chain:

Sylvia had desk access.
Sylvia’s fingerprints are on the clock glass.
Her ink smudge matches the mark near the clock.
The reception clock disagrees with the kitchen clock, staff timecard, and telegram receipt.
Hugo threatened Sylvia.
Sylvia confesses.

Biggest problem

The time math does not work.

The story repeatedly says:

reception clock: 10:10
kitchen clock / staff timecard: 10:45
telegram receipt: 11:00
discrepancy: thirty minutes
reception clock was advanced by thirty minutes

But 10:10 to 10:45 is 35 minutes, and 10:45 to 11:00 is 15 minutes. Also, if the reception clock shows 10:10 while the kitchen says 10:45, the reception clock is behind, not advanced.

Chapter 9 says:

“The only way the records can be reconciled is if the reception clock was advanced by thirty minutes before the murder.”

That is backwards. If the reception clock shows an earlier time than the kitchen clock, it was set back or running slow, not advanced.

Clean fix:

Reception clock false time: 10:10.
Kitchen clock true time: 10:40.
Telegram receipt true time: 11:00.
Discrepancy: reception clock is thirty minutes slow.

Sylvia set the reception clock back by thirty minutes after killing Hugo, making it appear he died earlier and shifting suspicion toward someone whose false-window alibi collapsed.

Or use:

Reception clock false time: 11:10.
Kitchen clock true time: 10:40.
Discrepancy: reception clock is thirty minutes fast.

Sylvia advanced the reception clock by thirty minutes to make Hugo seem alive later than he truly was.

Pick one. Right now the draft says advanced, but the given times imply set back / slow.

Main issues
1. Murder weapon confusion

Chapter 1 says a heavy brass letter opener lies near the body. Chapter 3 introduces a heavy brass candlestick with blood and hair. Chapter 9 says Sylvia struck Hugo with the candlestick.

Use one weapon.

Best choice:

Keep the candlestick.
Remove the letter opener from Chapter 1, or make it obvious set dressing.
In the reveal, say the crescent temple wound matches the candlestick base.

2. Generated timing leakage remains

These lines should be removed:

“Thirty minutes had passed before it was done.”
“It had taken thirty minutes in all.”
“By then it was a quarter to eleven. By then it was ten minutes past eleven.”

They are raw mechanism notes leaking into prose.

3. The “final trap” is more comparison than trap

Chapter 9 calls itself a discriminating test, but Eleanor mostly lays out records already gathered. That is fine, but less dramatic than the title promises.

Make it active:

Sylvia is asked to reset the clock herself.
Eleanor reveals the ink transfers from Sylvia’s notebook to the clock glass.
The clock cannot be adjusted without leaving the exact scratch at the eleven mark.
Sylvia’s notebook edge matches the smudge.

That would make it feel like a true trap rather than a summary.

4. The telegram/kitchen clock relationship is confusing

The telegram receipt is stamped 11:00, but the kitchen clock reads 10:45. The draft says the telegram matches the kitchen reading, but those are not the same time unless there is a known delay or the receipt was stamped later.

Fix by making the evidence line consistent:

kitchen clock: 10:45
staff timecard: 10:45
telegram receipt: 10:45

Or:

kitchen clock: 11:00
telegram receipt: 11:00
reception clock: 10:30

5. Chapter 10 is much cleaner than the previous draft, but still repeats the clearances

Chapter 10 is mostly aftermath, which is good. But it restates the alibi clearances again:

Finch cleared by staff log and guest testimony.
Hale cleared by pocket watch and housemaid.
Beatrice cleared by staff logbook.

That is not terrible, but it would be stronger if Chapter 10 focused only on emotional fallout after Sylvia’s confession.

Fastest fixes
Decide whether the reception clock is fast or slow.
Make the times match the mechanism: exactly 30 minutes, not 25/35.
Use only one murder weapon, preferably the candlestick.
Remove raw timing-note leakage.
Make the final trap more physical: ink smudge, fingerprint, scratch, reset mechanism.
Shorten Chapter 10’s repeated alibi summary.
Keep Sylvia as culprit — her motive and access are strong.
Overall

This is a solid draft with a strong culprit and better motive planting than the previous lobby-clock version, but the clock mechanism currently contradicts its own numbers.

As written: 80/100.

With the time math fixed and the weapon cleaned up, this could reach 87–89/100.
