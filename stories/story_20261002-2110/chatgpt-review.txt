This is solidly better than the theatre-clock draft, but not as strong as the rotating-wall Halloway Manor draft. The setting is excellent — fog, cliffside hotel, ledger, cellar, fishing knife, smuggling pressure — and the aftermath has real mood. The main weakness is that the solution mechanism is too busy: ledger swap, mistaken identity in fog, lock-picking, glove marks, cellar door, knife handle, diary note, shipment records. It works, but it is less elegant than the rotating wall/panel mechanism.
I’d score it around 82/100.

What works
The opening has a strong fog-noir/coastal mystery feel. The ledger is wrong, the body is outside in the fog, the knife blade is missing, and the witnesses cannot clearly say what they saw. That gives the story a strong physical and atmospheric puzzle from the start.    Pasted text
The ledger clue is good. The duplicate/overlapping entries put Reginald Jr. in impossible positions, and the handwriting comparison suggests deliberate identity confusion rather than clerical error.    Pasted text
The fog identity trick also works. Witnesses think they see Reginald Jr., but the footprint chart and coat evidence point toward Isabel being mistaken for him in the fog. That is the best central deception in the draft.    Pasted text
Main issue: too many proof mechanisms
The solution currently rests on all of these:
ledger/name swap
mismatched handwriting
fog mistaken identity
Reginald Jr.'s coat
cellar lock-picking
knife handle smudge
glove comparison
smaller footprints
diary note
shipment records
smuggling blackmail

That is a lot. The reader can follow it, but the reveal feels like an evidence pile rather than one beautiful click.
The cleaner version should be:
Main deception:
Isabel used the fog and Reginald Jr.'s coat to be mistaken for him near the cliff.

Supporting proof:
1. Footprints near the cliff are Isabel's size, not Reginald Jr.'s.
2. Her glove marks match both the knife handle and cellar lock.
3. The ledger swap was used to place Reginald Jr. in two impossible positions.

Everything else should support those three points, not compete with them.
The reveal is clear, but it needs a sharper “aha”
Chapter 8 names Isabel and gives the mechanism: she handled the fishing knife, picked the cellar lock, used the fog, and acted during the 8:45–9:15 window.    Pasted text
But the final proof would land harder if Eleanor said:
“The fog made witnesses uncertain. The ledger made Reginald impossible. But the glove made Isabel definite.”

Then:
“Reginald Jr. could not be in the cellar and on the cliff. Isabel could be in the fog, wearing his coat, leaving her own prints, and carrying the knife.”

That would make the logic cleaner.
Isabel’s motive is good but needs one concrete line
The confession says:
“Reginald Gresham was about to expose me.”
“If he’d lived, I’d lose Cliffhaven Bay.”    Pasted text

That works emotionally, but add one specific:
“He had the dock receipts,” Isabel said. “Not rumours. Receipts.”

Or:
“He knew which boats carried fish and which carried contraband.”

That would tie the motive directly to the smuggling/dock world.

Overall
This is a good atmospheric coastal-hotel mystery. The fog setting is strong, Isabel is a better culprit than Kenneth in the theatre draft, and the aftermath is emotionally richer than many earlier attempts. But the final proof is less elegant than the rotating-wall Halloway draft because it relies on too many moving pieces.
As written: 82/100.
With the reveal simplified, Isabel’s motive specified, Chapter 9 cut down, and scaffold removed, this could reach 87–89/100.
