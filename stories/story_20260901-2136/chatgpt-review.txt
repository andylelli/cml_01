What works

The mechanism is more interesting than a basic clock reset. A clock that gives a false chime sequence is a good mystery object because people trust what they hear without checking the mechanism. The opening plants the essential conflict well: the clock gives three strikes, while Finch’s pocket watch is stopped at 9:10.

The draft also does a better job of creating a false accusation. Hale’s bar alibi depends on the chimes, so Eleanor initially pressures him, then realizes the clock itself has been made dishonest. That gives the investigation a useful turn instead of moving in a straight line from clue to culprit.

Hugo works well as the culprit because he is tied to the clock through the maintenance logs and mechanism knowledge. Chapter 7 does a good job of turning his earlier presence near the tower into meaningful suspicion, and Beatrice’s memory of him boasting about making the clock “sing backwards” is a nice character-flavored clue.

The final evidence package is solid in concept: reversed striking cam, marked hammer, Hugo’s screwdriver notch, stopped watch, and false chime sequence. That is a proper physical chain, not just a confession.

Main problem: the time/chime logic contradicts itself

The intended clean version appears to be:

True time of death: 9:10
Pocket watch: stopped at 9:10
False clock signal: three strikes at 9:15
Effect: witnesses think the critical moment was 9:15, not 9:10
Culprit: Hugo, who knows how to alter the striking mechanism

That works.

But the draft sometimes says:

“three strikes at a quarter past nine”

and sometimes:

“three strikes at a ten minutes past nine”

Those are not the same. Chapter 3 specifically says the clock gave “three strikes at a ten minutes past nine,” which destroys the five-minute contradiction.

Chapter 6 also says:

“Three strikes at a ten minutes past nine”
“the pocket watch … stopped at ten minutes past”
“If the watch is right, then we have been mistaken about the hour…”

But if both are 9:10, there is no contradiction.

Fix every instance to one locked version:

The pocket watch stopped at ten minutes past nine.
The hotel clock struck three times at quarter past nine.
The five-minute gap is the lie.

And never write “three strikes at ten past nine.”

Mechanism clarity needs one clean explanation

The draft says the hammer was reversed, the striking cam was reversed, and the clock struck three times. But the reader still needs a simple mechanical explanation.

Add this explanation to Chapter 8:

“The hands were not the trick,” Eleanor said. “The strike was. Hugo altered the cam so the clock gave the quarter-hour signal five minutes late. Everyone heard the sound and trusted it. But Dr. Finch’s watch stopped when she fell, and it stopped at ten past nine. The chime did not mark the murder. It covered it.”

That would make the solution much clearer.
